Waterloo, 1815

Create an epic, beautiful, historically researched Waterloo battlefield toy set that people want to display in their living room and take down to play. The user and Autonomous's workshop intend to fabricate the first prototype and play it in real life as soon as the digital package is complete. Prioritize a coherent, practical first playable set over an enormous miniature army or an elaborate fabrication process. Do not promise an unmeasured fabrication time or report physical tests that have not happened.

This is one Spark mixed-material Make product. The landscape and its permanent scenery arrive fully assembled, painted and finished. Troop stands, commanders, guns and necessary play aids also arrive complete, ready to reposition by hand. Repositioning finished pieces is play, not customer assembly. There are NO moving mechanisms, articulated figures, working weapons, powered effects, electronics, motors, springs, magnets, apps or networking. Nothing needs to be animated mechanically. The strategic experience comes from human choices and the physical battlefield.

The customer should be able to form lines, columns and squares; change facing and orders; attack from the front or flank; concentrate cavalry and artillery; move at different paces; hold, rally or withdraw; and decide when to commit reserves. Create original, clear, bounded tabletop rules that make these choices interact. This must be genuinely playable from the included instructions, not a diorama with a few suggested stories. Define components, setup, player/side roles, legal actions, order limits and resolution, formations and facing, terrain effects, engagements, morale, end conditions, and how to reset. Choose a manageable level of abstraction so playing does not require moving hundreds of individual soldiers. Use several finished sub-stands where needed to make a formation's footprint visibly change. Do not make squares invulnerable or cavalry/elite units automatic wins. All rules, unit quantities and physical pieces must agree.

Create one researched historical deployment, one main battle scenario and three shorter alternative-order scenarios, all using the same finite roster of supplied pieces. Strong possibilities from which to choose are allocation of troops around Hougoumont; different support and formations for the main infantry assault; coordinating horse, foot and guns; the struggle for La Haye Sainte; Prussian arrival and the French defense around Plancenoit; and committing the final reserve differently. The general rules should support further unscripted replay without additional components. Clearly label counterfactual decisions. Do not claim a changed order guarantees a historical alternative outcome. Develop precise original rules; do not copy a commercial wargame's text, art or proprietary scenario package.

Make the 1815 geography matter: Wellington's ridge and reverse slope, farm compounds, roads and sunken approaches, fields, orchards/hedges, and enough eastern terrain for Plancenoit and a meaningful Prussian entry route. Preserve the relationships of the major strongpoints and approaches. Use selective scale compression where necessary and explain it plainly; do not claim a survey-accurate full battlefield at figure scale. Waterloo has rolling agricultural terrain, not invented alpine cliffs. Exclude later monuments such as the Lion's Mound. Represent three armies on two sides: Napoleon's French, Wellington's multinational Anglo-Allied force and Blucher's cooperating Prussians. Do not reduce the coalition to British redcoats or erase the Prussian contribution.

The detailed pieces should include recognizable period infantry, skirmishers, elite troops, cavalry, artillery and commanders. Choose a restrained, useful selection with distinct silhouettes and researched 1815 uniforms/flags. They must look like compelling miniature soldiers, horses and guns, not generic pawns, cylinders or purely abstract blocks. Select figure size, minimum robust feature sizes and the available supported printing process together. Unless a later explicit fabrication specification is provided before this Wish launches, target FDM printing with a 0.4 mm nozzle and individual production parts that fit a 220 x 220 mm usable bed; do not assume a resin printer or resin-scale detail. Preserve the actual Make print checks. Declare a finite unit/stand roster, printed copy counts, stock operations and prototype fabrication assumptions before detailed CAD. Reuse well-designed production families and representative groups rather than adding hundreds of unique fragile ornaments. First check the largest line, column, square and artillery footprint against usable routes, destinations and hand access around scenery. Select a practical tabletop footprint from those needs; do not sacrifice play to force everything onto a tiny decorative base.

Use mixed materials purposefully. Printed plastic supplies figures, horses, cannons, precise architecture/detail and formation carriers. Plywood provides a rigid display base and finished border. Shaped rigid foam, layered card/paperboard and cork can supply terrain volume and surface character. Thin wood can provide farmhouse details, fences and gates. Restrained felt, short scenic fibers and fixed foliage can form fields, hedges, orchards and trees while keeping movement areas stable and usable. Supported fabric or printed paper provides period flags and suitable scenic details. Texture paste, fine aggregate, adhesives, paint and protective finishes complete the terrain and figures. Paper/card supplies the operating rules, command aids and scenario/deployment sheets. Choose actual stock properties, dimensions and joining processes before fixing interfaces. Omit a proposed material if it adds fabrication difficulty without improving the product, and explain that decision internally.

Make the finished object visually exceptional: a cohesive wooden-framed landscape, recognizable farms, carefully composed relief, believable material colors/textures and beautiful painted miniature formations. The complete hero should show the actual full set in a historically identified display arrangement. State the approximate historical moment; any supplied pieces not on the field in that deployment belong in a clearly explained reserve/display area, not an invented simultaneous deployment. Secondary static views should show depth, close detail and how line/column/square arrangements differ using the real pieces. Include an approachable customer play guide with an illustrated first turn, edge cases and scenario reset. Public presentation is the complete product and how to play; it must not expose the internal BOM or workshop instructions.

Autonomous's engineers and workers perform sourcing, cutting, printing, painting, assembly and qualification. Produce the full assembled STEP package and the internal manufacturing manifest/BOM covering fixed components, every loose stand and play aid, stock/cut quantities, consumables, fabrication patterns or exact dimensions, compatible adhesives/finishes, assembly sequence, paint guidance and inspection steps. Keep printed production sources separate from nonprinted and combined scene sources; print-gate only printed production parts. This is mechanically static, so use truthful applicable evidence under the existing Make contract without inventing a mechanism or bypassing required verification. Include all required visual feedback, independent review and final checks. Keep source, supplier details, fabrication files and the workshop handoff private under the existing public/internal asset boundary. Allowlist complete reviewed public product imagery/STEP and customer rules/scenario documents through the normal mixed-material contract.

Exercise the exact proposed rules through concrete worked turns and complete games or suitable bounded deterministic checks using native-agent judgment and tools. Look for illegal states, undefined ties, nontermination, dominant automatic actions, unusable formation footprints and contradictions between the rules and supplied pieces. Record what was actually checked and repair it. Native walkthroughs or deterministic rule checks are digital evidence, not physical playtesting or proof of enjoyment/balance. Supply a short prioritized physical prototype plan for fit, stability, grip, fragile details, piece identification, independent teaching, first full game and counterfactual replay. Do not add a separate Playtest lifecycle or a Python agent/prompt loop. Do not purchase, fabricate or ship anything as part of this native run; authorized publication remains the normal host-owned Spark Release.
