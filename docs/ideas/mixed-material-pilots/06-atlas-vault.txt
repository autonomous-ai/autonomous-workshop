Atlas Vault

Make a finished mechanical puzzle box shaped as an original miniature archipelago. Terrain hides tactile patterns: taking a paper rubbing and looking through a simple mirrored viewing chamber reveals the order for several large mechanical dials. The correct order releases a concealed compartment. The viewing chamber uses one simple bought battery-powered light with a momentary switch to illuminate the clue, giving the light a necessary optical role. Provide several reconfigurable clue routes so the owner can set a new puzzle for another player without sourcing materials or dismantling the mechanism.

Use CNC/laser-cut wood construction; printed interlocks, dials, clue holders and original molds/jigs; cast plaster information-bearing relief tiles; explicitly specified paper/air-dry clay scenic clue shells and polymer-clay tactile keys/markers where those properties serve the puzzle; paper/card clue and rubbing strips; a sourced mirror and clear plastic viewing insert; felt pads; metal hardware; paint and compatible adhesives. Include the bought light, battery holder, momentary switch, insulated leads and required mounting geometry. Keep electricity to the simple light circuit, with no controller or firmware. Modeling clay can be an internal master-making aid if useful, but unused material options cannot count as manufactured coverage.

Signature experience: the player transforms a three-dimensional clue into a readable trace and reflection, then sees a real mechanical lock state change. Plaster relief, clay markers, paper and optics carry necessary information; they must not be decorative additions to satisfy a material checklist. Internal casting molds, jigs and tools are manufacturing resources, not shipped play items. Record supplier-specific preparation, curing, baking and finishing for each chosen medium.

Internal human prototype checks: measure correct and incorrect unlock trials and dial torque. Check rubbing legibility, reflected clue order, illumination, optical access and tile registration. Inspect cast shrinkage, chipping, clay/paint wear, adhesive retention and retained-part integrity under intended handling. Verify every supplied clue configuration leads to its intended solution. Measure the simple light's operating behavior and enclosure temperature under specified conditions. Fragile or unreliable parts require a recorded revision rather than a claim that the puzzle is physically ready.
