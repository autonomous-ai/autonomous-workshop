Switchyard Relay

Make a finished modular tabletop signal game containing original tipping, rolling, spring-transfer and flag modules, plus dominoes. Players construct two routes that make one signal arrive first or both meet at a shared mechanical finish. Use one clear common interface and a bounded set of challenge cards with several possible solutions. The product must arrive with enough complete modules and play pieces for satisfying games, without customers sourcing household objects.

Use a wood base and wood dominoes/modules; printed clips, tracks, guides and interlocks; metal springs, axles, formed wire and hardware; rubber bands, routed cord, foam bumpers and felt catch areas; paper/chipboard flags and printed challenge cards. Use captured magnets with specified ferrous counterparts to register a meaningful set of reconfigurable modules; capture and test them rather than relying on loose magnetic attachment. The magnet registration, elastic signal interfaces and module-to-module attachment must be represented in the complete assembly and internal BOM.

Signature experience: independent physical routes act as competing or cooperating messages. Players can see where a chain succeeds or stops, understand why and reconfigure it. A route game and mechanical finish are essential; an automatic moving domino dispenser is outside this pilot's promise.

Internal human prototype checks: measure input force/travel and output motion for each module. Repeat transfers in several valid configurations, counting false releases, incomplete resets and unintended detachments. Inspect magnets and fastener retention and count successful whole-route trials. Check route timing repeatability and verify physical solutions for the supplied challenges. Keep unsolved reliability issues in the private engineering handoff.

Shared product brief
Create an ORIGINAL finished product, using the reference play category as inspiration rather than copying any existing product's branding, appearance, mechanism layout or instructions. Aim for stronger play and visual quality, but describe improvement as an aspiration until real comparative evidence exists. Keep this one Spark Make product; do not expand into another lifecycle, invent a Python agent loop, or introduce Raspberry Pi, custom boards, firmware, apps or networking.

Autonomous has an equipped internal workshop with 3D printing, laser cutting, CNC, woodworking and textile/handcraft capabilities. Its engineers and workers source, fabricate, assemble, test and ship the finished product. Customers do not buy an unfinished kit and are not asked to source, print or assemble components. Choose real stock and purchased components with dimensions and operating properties adequate for their interfaces. Give materials necessary functional or experiential roles. Resolve unsupported substitutions explicitly rather than filling the design with generic placeholder parts.

Use Make's mixed-materials capability. Produce the complete assembled design, including visible and hidden nonprinted components, and the internal manufacturing BOM, stock/cut quantities, sourcing specifications, fabrication instructions, assembly sequence and physical prototype checks. Distinguish installed parts, loose play objects, consumables and reusable tools. Keep STEP as the geometry deliverable and existing printed-part, mount, powered-product and visual evidence where applicable. Do not print-gate nonprinted parts or promise unsupported machine toolpaths.

Make the complete assembled product look beautiful and physically coherent, with authored component colors and recognizable material appearance. Public assets show the finished object and its use, with a compelling hero and appropriate motion/secondary views. Keep BOM, suppliers, source, fabrication/assembly instructions and engineering diagnosis internal. Use the explicit public/internal artifact boundary; do not expose an internal archive merely by hiding its links. Preserve truthful distinctions between designed, digitally checked, physically fabricated, tested and shipped.
