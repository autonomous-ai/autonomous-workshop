Cloudline Coaster

Make a finished motorized marble skyline in which a slow lift feeds two selectable gravity routes. A visible bascule transfers marbles between levels, and route settings create clearly different journeys and arrival timing. One simple switch runs the lift; the child predicts and changes destinations, then watches repeated circulation. Create an original silhouette, track plan and transfer choreography, rather than reproducing a reference coaster's outline or lift decoration.

Use CNC/laser-cut wood towers and supports; printed bespoke tracks, carriers, route switches and bascule pivots where justified; bought marbles; specified rigid or transparent plastic track inserts; a specified silicone or rubber traction sleeve with a functional grip role; foam/felt quiet catches; metal shafts, springs and fasteners; a simple compatible DC gearmotor, battery holder, switch and insulated leads/connectors. Keep powered behavior to this bounded circuit. Source the motor envelope, shaft/mount dimensions and operating requirements before fixing its mates. No controller or firmware.

Signature experience: a route choice changes where and when the marble re-enters the skyline's circulation, while the bascule makes the transfer legible. Repeat play should offer meaningful route/timing predictions, not only an unchanging display. Include all necessary marbles and route modules in the finished product.

Internal human prototype checks: record complete circuits and failure locations; test lift slip, bascule reset and every track join using actual specified marble samples. Measure loaded motor behavior, runtime and jam response under named conditions. Verify wire routing, access, component retention and the promised timing differences. A nominal motor or track envelope in CAD is not evidence of torque or reliable circulation.
