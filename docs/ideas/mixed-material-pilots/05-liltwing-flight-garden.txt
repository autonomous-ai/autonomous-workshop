Liltwing Flight Garden

Make a finished moth-shaped glider game with a bounded hand-operated elastic launcher and three distinct soft landing meadows. A lightweight paper message travels with the moth. Players choose a meadow, adjust a simple trim or launch setting and attempt a delivery. Make the glider, launcher and fabric landscape visually original; a successful soft landing should feel like arriving with a tiny message. The entire game arrives assembled with its play pieces and understandable operating instructions.

Use cut foam wings, a light printed nose/trim fitting, a card/paper tail and message carrier, a specified elastic or spring launch mechanism, a wood launcher frame, rods/fasteners and cord where needed. Use sewn fabric/felt meadows with yarn/cord markers and real retrievable message pockets, so textiles have a functional role in landing and delivery. A clear plastic window may expose the launch setting when useful. Keep wing compliance, mass distribution, payload and launch energy coherent; do not turn the aircraft into an untested heavy printed object. Choose actual sheet thickness, density, flexible parts and elastic operating dimensions.

Signature experience: carrying a tiny message gives flight tuning a purpose, and the three landing destinations create distinct attainable challenges. Repeat play involves selecting a destination, changing a bounded setting, sending, observing and retrying. This is a character-led flight product; do not describe it as demonstrating powered flapping, walking or crawling.

Internal human prototype checks: record repeated launch behavior and landing dispersion at each setting. Compare trim effects and payload sensitivity; measure operating effort and inspect wing/nose damage after repeated landings. Check launcher and component retention, fabric-target behavior and reset access. Verify that the three destinations offer meaningful choices under the actual tested indoor conditions, not just in a staged hero image.
