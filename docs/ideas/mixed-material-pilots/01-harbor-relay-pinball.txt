Harbor Relay Pinball

Make a finished tabletop pinball harbor with two generous flippers, three delivery docks and a final lighthouse destination. Capturing a ball at a dock should raise its paper signal and visibly change a gate, so the next shot has different possibilities. Completing the deliveries opens the lighthouse destination. Define a small fixed ball supply, understandable scoring or success condition, and an easy complete reset. Give the product an original curved harbor silhouette and cheerful physical feedback. Keep this first pilot passive and mechanically manageable.

Use materials for their actual jobs: a CNC/woodworked base and dowels; a selected smooth, stable playfield; printed custom flippers, rails, guides and joints; laser-cut wood/chipboard harbor structures; folded cardstock/paper flags; bought marbles; rubber bands and a specified return/plunger spring where appropriate; metal axles, brads and fasteners; felt catch lining; foam stops; rubber feet; paint and compatible adhesives. Do not force cardboard to be the rolling surface if another specified material performs better. The flippers, dock gates and paper signals must be modeled and assembled as a working system, not merely a decorated board.

Signature experience: one captured delivery visibly changes the harbor and opens the next tactical possibility. Repeat play comes from choosing a route, trying a limited-ball delivery sequence, resetting and improving. All required play pieces should be part of the finished-product offering.

Internal human prototype checks: record successful launches, flipper returns, dock registrations, unintended stalls and complete resets across repeated games. Measure actual ball diameters and launch/flipper force. Inspect impact joints, board distortion, felt/foam wear and access to clear a stalled ball. State the test conditions and unresolved physical risks without claiming a test passed from a render.
