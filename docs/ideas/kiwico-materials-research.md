# KiwiCo materials and mixed-material product research

Research date: 2026-09-10. This is reference research for original Workshop
products, not a plan to reproduce KiwiCo designs. The associated implementation
brief is [Mixed-material Spark MVP](mixed-material-spark-mvp.md).

## What the evidence supports

KiwiCo's useful pattern is a complete play experience assembled from materials
chosen for different physical jobs. A rigid frame positions a mechanism; cord
transmits a pull; a spring supplies motion; felt creates a character; a marble
or pom-pom gives the mechanism something to act upon. Printing every element
would discard advantages that the other materials provide.

This study uses official product inventories, public crate instructions and
design notes. It is representative, not an exhaustive supplier catalog. Public
pages frequently omit thickness, resin, wood species, elastic force, fastener
standard and motor rating. Their component names establish material families
and combinations, not procurement-ready specifications. Regional and education
pages sometimes expose details absent from the US storefront's fetched HTML.

The accepted Workshop direction differs from KiwiCo's kit business: Autonomous
engineers and workshop staff fabricate, source, assemble, test and ship finished
products. The customer sees the complete product. BOMs and manufacturing
instructions are internal; the following kit examples inform design methods.

## Material choice changes behavior

KiwiCo's [Eureka pinball design notes](https://www.kiwico.com/eureka/pinball)
describe choosing a plastic playfield after exploring wood and paper-based
alternatives: rolling and shape retention mattered. Rubber feet reduced
sliding. These development notes concern a different presentation from the
younger-age retail Pinball Machine; their details should not be merged into one
exact inventory.

The [music-box design notes](https://www.kiwico.com/eureka/musicbox) describe
replacing steel-washer hammers with wooden hammers to soften the sound. The
[Mechanical Claw teacher-guide sample](https://icdn.kiwicrate.com/shop_banners/Classroom_Packs/MechanicalClaw_TeacherGuide_Sample_Pages.pdf)
describes rubber squares improving grip on smooth plastic fingertips. This is
a distinct classroom claw, not the wooden Arcade Claw above, and the older
sample is not a current packing list.

These examples support a practical conclusion: a geometrically similar
substitution can change friction, sound, stiffness, mass and performance.
Workshop must specify the required physical property and test an actual
substitute. Rendering wood grain or a rubber loop establishes appearance, not
acoustic quality or available force.

## DIY evidence is a separate category

KiwiCo's household tutorials reveal useful construction techniques. They do not
establish the contents of similarly named retail kits.

- [Monster Automaton](https://www.kiwico.com/diy/stem/motion-mechanics/monster-automaton)
  combines laminated cardboard cams, a box, skewer shafts, straw sleeves and
  paper/cup characters. Cardboard performs real mechanical work; straw sleeves
  permit shaft rotation.
- [Cardboard Elevator](https://www.kiwico.com/diy/stem/engineering-building/simple-lift)
  combines cut/folded box material, a wooden dowel, cord, tape and glue. Winding
  the axle lifts a platform. Structure, transmission and attachment are separate
  design problems.
- [Mini Foosball](https://www.kiwico.com/diy/play-learn/fun-games/mini-foosball)
  uses a box, dowels, cardboard players, rubber bands and a ping-pong ball.
  Here end bands retain shafts rather than power the game.
- [Magnetic Fishing Game](https://www.kiwico.com/diy/play-learn/pretend-play/magnetic-fishing-game)
  uses paper fish with paper clips, a stick, cord and a magnet. The passive
  magnetic interaction needs no electronics, but its magnet grade and retention
  specifications are not supplied.
- [Motorized Paper Airplane Launcher](https://www.kiwico.com/diy/stem/engineering-building/paper-airplane-launcher)
  uses two hobby motors, wooden wheels with rubber-band rims, a wooden support,
  battery holders and paper aircraft. Rubber supplies traction. This is distinct
  from both the spring-powered retail launcher and KiwiCo's nonmotor paper
  launcher tutorial.

## Implications for Make

The internal product description needs independent fields for component
identity, material, fabrication or sourcing route, quantity, physical
representation and evidence. “Printed,” “cardboard,” “motor” and “spring” answer
different questions. One purchased sheet may produce several panels; one cord
spool may supply multiple installed lengths. Tools, stock, consumables,
permanent parts and loose play objects need distinct roles.

Precision printed connectors can combine with laser-cut board, CNC-machined
wood, cut/sewn fabric and acquired springs, bearings, mirrors or motor modules.
This is an engineering inference for Autonomous's equipped internal workshop,
not a claim about KiwiCo's manufacturing methods. Staff need dimensions,
interfaces, stock and cut schedules, joining instructions, sourcing
specifications and observable assembly checks. Customers need a beautiful,
truthful presentation of the finished product.

The first scope can stay at passive mechanics and simple battery, motor, light,
switch and lead assemblies. No examined example requires a Raspberry Pi,
custom PCB, firmware project or network service. Casting and richer textile
work provide future material breadth without making every researched process
an MVP-supported capability. Cement was not substantiated as feedstock in the
checked products. Neither digital models nor these reference pages establish
that a new Workshop product has been physically fabricated, tested or shipped.
