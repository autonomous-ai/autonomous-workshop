Create a corrected revision of Rainward Sun while preserving its historical
backgammon rules, 24-lane topology, setup, capacity and overall Sun dimensions.

1. Remove the two block-like crest lobes from the central bar. Keep the bar
spacious, circular and unobstructed. If an orientation cue is needed, use a
low, smooth engraved solar-prominence motif on the far side, outside every
captured-piece landing area.

2. Replace the blank dice with finished dice whose 1–6 pip layouts are modeled
as shallow recessed geometry directly in CAD, using standard opposite pairs
1–6, 2–5 and 3–4. Canonical renders must show the recessed pips filled in a
contrasting dark color. The instructions may require filling the engraved
recesses with paint after printing, but must not require the user to position
or draw the pips manually. Do not ship visually blank cubes. Do not claim
physical statistical fairness without testing.

3. Redesign both checker silhouettes so an unprimed viewer reads them as
plasma droplets, solar rain or comet-like flowing matter—not animal heads,
forks or generic polygon tokens. Give both a rounded condensed leading body
and a swept, tapering plasma tail. One player has one flowing tail; the other
has a clearly bifurcated tail that splits near its tip with rounded branches.
Avoid square heads, sharp fragile tips and parallel prongs resembling ears.
Keep flat stable undersides, stackability, minimum printable thickness and
the full 15-piece lane capacity.

Blind visual review must separately confirm that the dice visibly have pips,
the counters read as flowing plasma rather than creatures, and the central
bar contains no unexplained block-like objects.
