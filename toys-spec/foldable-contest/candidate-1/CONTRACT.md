# Crease Moth

A desk toy that stands on the desk as one plain upright slab, closed like a
book. Press the top of the slab straight down with one finger and the slab
splits along its crease: its two leaves swing apart in mirror, lie down flat
and turn over their hidden inner faces, and the slab has become a moth twice
as wide as it was tall, with eye-spot wings and a small pin rising out of the
crease where its head is. Lift one wing tip and the whole moth folds back into
the slab.

Designed by the Transformer personality for `trend-lab`: one continuous
motion turns the toy into a different shape, both shapes read clearly, and the
transformation never needs a second hand. The base sits on the desk under its
own weight; nothing is held while the finger presses.

## Trend Hook

The fold: two equal 64 x 44 mm leaves that close into one 64 x 44 x 8 mm slab
and open flat into a 122 mm spread, split by exactly one crease, the moth's
body. Countable and pointable: two leaves, one crease, and the open spread is
twice the closed slab's height plus the crease. The inner faces are hidden in
the slab and only show once it opens, the way a folding screen hides its inner
half. No phone, brand, logo, camera layout or product shape appears anywhere;
the slab is a plain rounded tablet with nothing on its outer faces.

## Signature Motion

What moves: the body (a sliding carriage) and the two leaves. What drives it:
a fixed double-sided rack post on the base, in front of the body, meshes a
toothed sector on the front hub of each leaf. Each leaf hinges on a steel pin
carried by the body. When the body is pressed down into the base's pocket,
the fixed rack turns both sectors in mirror, so each leaf swings 90 degrees
outward, from standing upright face to face to lying flat with its inner face
up. The leaves never mesh each other; each meshes only the rack.

What the player does: presses the top edge of the closed slab straight down
with one finger, 9.4 mm, until the body lands on the pocket floor. To close,
the player lifts either wing tip with one finger: that leaf's sector drives
the rack the other way, which raises the body and swings the other leaf up
in mirror. The closed extreme ends when the two inner faces meet flat; the
open extreme ends when the body lands on the pocket floor.

## Shape and parts

Unique geometries (five Components, four shapes):

| Geometry | Count | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| base | 1 | Tray with a guiding pocket and the fixed rack post |
| body | 1 | The moth's thorax: sliding carriage that holds both hinge pins |
| leaf-left | 1 | Left leaf: plate, two arms, front hub with sector, back hub |
| leaf-right | 1 | Mirror of leaf-left |

The two steel pins are purchased Ø3 x 68 mm dowels, not printed.

Focal Component: the two leaves read as one wing spread, and the eye spots on
their inner faces are the focal point. The cost, accepted: the body is a plain
rounded carriage and the base a plain tray, with no surface detail of their
own, so nothing competes with the wings.

### Frame

Assembly coordinates in the Display Pose (fully open): X to the right, Y to the
back, Z up; the toy's front is -Y, where the rack post stands. Z 0 is the desk
under the base, X 0 the crease's centre line, Y 0 the body's centre.

### Base

Tray 64 (X) x 84 (Y) x 18 (Z), Z 0 to 18, vertical edges rounded R10. A pocket
26.6 x 54.6, X -13.3 to 13.3, Y -27.3 to 27.3, from Z 4 (pocket floor) to the
top at Z 18, guides the body. The rack post stands on the top at X 0,
Y -36 to -32: a 3.5 mm core (X -1.75 to 1.75), 4 mm deep in Y, rising from
Z 18 to a rounded tip at Z 41. Its two X faces carry module 1, 20 degree
rack teeth from Z 21.5 to Z 39, pitch lines at X -3 and X 3. Prints on its
bottom face; the rack teeth are horizontal ridges whose flanks lean 20 degrees
from vertical.

### Body

Rounded block 26 (X) x 54 (Y) x 28.5 (Z), its top edges rounded R4. In the
Display Pose it spans X -13 to 13, Y -27 to 27, Z 4 to 32.5. Two Ø2.95 press
bores run its whole length along Y at X -9 and X 9, Z 25.5, the hinge axes;
the two steel pins press into them and stick out 9 mm at the front
(Y -36) and 5 mm at the back (Y 32). Prints on its bottom face; the press
bores lie parallel to the bed and get teardrop tops.

### Leaves

Each leaf is a plate 64 (Y) x 44 x 4 thick, corners rounded R10, held off its
hinge axis by two arms. In the leaf's own frame, the plate lies 5 to 9 mm from
the axis across its thickness and 8 to 52 mm out from the axis along its
width; its inner face is the one 9 mm from the axis. Two arms, 4 mm thick in
Y, at Y -31.5 to -27.5 and Y 27.5 to 32, join the plate's root to two hubs on
the axis. The front hub runs Y -36 to -27.5, Ø8 with a Ø3.4 free bore; its
front 4 mm, Y -36 to -32, carries a module 1, 20 degree toothed sector of
pitch radius 6 over a 150 degree arc, so the 90 degree travel keeps one spare
tooth at each end. The back hub runs Y 27.5 to 32, Ø8 with a Ø3.4 free bore.
The hubs straddle the body with 0.5 mm end play at each face.

Inner face (shows only when open): one raised eye ring, outer Ø18, band 2.5
wide, 0.8 high, centred 30 mm out from the axis at Y 0, and three raised vein
ribs 1.2 wide and 0.6 high fanning from the plate's root edge to its free
edge. Outer face: plain and flat, no detail. Each leaf prints standing on its
back end face (Y 32), so the sector's teeth are vertical extrusions.

leaf-right is leaf-left mirrored about X 0.

### Display Pose

Fully open: both leaves flat, inner faces up at Z 34.5, plates spanning
X -61 to -17 and X 17 to 61; the body bottom on the pocket floor at Z 4; the
rack post's rounded tip 8.5 mm above the body top, at the crease's front end.
Envelope 122 (X) x 84 (Y) x 41 (Z).

Closed (the other shape): the body is raised 9.42 mm, the hinge axes at
Z 34.92; both leaves stand upright, their inner faces meeting flat at X 0,
forming one slab 8 (X) x 64 (Y) x 44 (Z) from Z 42.92 to 86.92 on top of the
body. The rack post's tip at Z 41 sits 1.9 mm below the slab's bottom edge,
hidden under it.

### Motion and fit

- Travel: the body slides 9.42 mm in Z; each leaf turns 90 degrees
  (6 mm pitch radius x pi/2 = 9.42 mm of rack).
- Body in pocket: 0.3 mm clearance a side; at closed the body still sits
  4.6 mm deep in the pocket.
- Hubs on pins: Ø3.4 bore on a Ø3 pin, 0.2 mm a side.
- Hubs to body faces: 0.5 mm end play front and back.
- Sector hub to rack post side faces: 1.0 mm.
- Open: sector tips clear the base top by 0.5 mm.
- Closed: the slab bottom clears the rack tip by 1.9 mm and the body top
  by 1.0 mm.
- Stops: open ends when the body bottom lands on the pocket floor; closed
  ends when the two inner faces meet. No other stop.
- Assembled, not printed in place: press the pins into the body through the
  leaves' hubs, then drop the body into the pocket with the sectors meshed on
  the rack, leaves upright.

Motion plan: `--check-motion true` on the wish and on every resume and
correction; one sweep of the full travel, at most 10 steps, against only the
parts a moving part can reach; one one-tooth sweep per mesh, at most 10 steps,
with no obstacles; a skipped, killed or timed-out sweep fails the motion
requirement, never passes it.

### Fixed frame

Release's product frame at 35 degrees azimuth and 22 degrees elevation,
against `#f5f0e6`: the open spread fills the width, seen from the front-right
and slightly above, so both inner faces and their eye rings show, with the
crease and its rising pin at the centre front.

### Handling

The toy is pressed, lifted and knocked over on a desk. The first parts to
suffer are the leaves' arms and the rack teeth. Arms are 4 mm thick, the rack
core 3.5 mm, the plates 4 mm; nothing load-bearing is under 3 mm. A leaf
pulled hard comes off its pin only after the pin is pressed out of the body.

### Print

Workshop's 0.4 mm nozzle, no supports. Every point, chisel, keel and V
underside ends in a flat land at least 0.8 mm across (one print minimum at a
0.4 mm nozzle). A drawn detail under the print minimums is enlarged to the
minimum; when the enlarged detail does not fit its spot, it is left out, and
this contract names it. Stances: base and body on their bottom faces, each
leaf standing on its back end face.
