# Uncontrolled Permanence

## Definition
Irreversible, permanent changes to a game's components are triggered by unpredictable randomness rather than a traceable player decision. Players can't feel ownership of a change they didn't choose, and the designer loses control over when in the campaign it happens, since a rare roll could hit turn one or the final turn.

## Relations
- mitigated-by:: [[rule-patterns/player-authored-permanence]], [[rule-patterns/milestone-gated-content]]

## Notes
- Permanence magnifies ordinary output randomness because its cost persists beyond the current session.
- Milestone gating controls when irreversible choices become available, while player authorship preserves ownership of what changes.
- sources: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/what-if-you-can-t-take-it-back-i-risk-legacy-i-and-permanence https://cmsw.mit.edu/wp/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/hawke-jjotto-sm-cms-2022.pdf
- [yt:ZSVREGmO1Xw] medium: Risk Legacy's triple-six roll could permanently destroy a board region on any turn at random; the designer concluded permanent changes must be player-authored, not dice-authored, in every subsequent legacy game. (GDC Festival of Gaming 2021)
- [yt:F_1YcCcBVfY] medium: D&D's level-draining (permanent XP/level loss) caused outsized dread and was eventually removed from later editions — permanent loss feels far worse than an equivalent one-time setback. (GDC Festival of Gaming 2018)
- [yt:P3P70PrE8i4] medium: Charterstone blocks placing a new sticker over any space still holding tokens, so permanent legacy markers create needless dead ends even when the tokens no longer matter. (Stonemaier Games 2022)
