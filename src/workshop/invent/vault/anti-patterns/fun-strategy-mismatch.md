# Fun-Strategy Mismatch

## Definition
The strategically optimal way to win diverges from the game's intended enjoyable actions, so players who play well must avoid the parts designed to be fun. Because winning is compulsory but fun is optional, skilled play ends up feeling worse than the designer intended, and players blame the game rather than themselves.

## Relations
- mitigated-by:: [[rule-patterns/incentive-aligned-core-play]], [[rule-patterns/degenerate-strategy-counterpressure]]

## Notes
- If optimization repeatedly removes the game's most enjoyable activity, reward structure—not player attitude—is the primary design problem.
- When players consistently enjoy an unintended style more, repositioning the game around that behavior may be better than suppressing it.
