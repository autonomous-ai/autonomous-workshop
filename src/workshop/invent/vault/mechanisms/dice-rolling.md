# Dice Rolling

## Definition
Players roll one or more dice to generate a random value that is then applied to resolve an action — movement, combat, resource gain, or an action's strength — with the exact face(s) unknown until the moment of the roll. Tension comes from the gap between hope and result: players commit to an action before knowing its outcome, and the shape of the probability curve (flat d20 vs. bell-curved 2d6, single die vs. dice pools) determines how much of that outcome is skill-shaped versus pure chance.

## Notes
Complaints about dice being "swingy" are usually really complaints about missing post-roll decisions — pure roll-and-move draws the harshest criticism because the roll is the whole turn.
Common variance-taming tools (rerolls, flat modifiers, dice pools that average out) sit on top of the base mechanism rather than replacing it.
