# Drafting

## Definition
Drafting distributes items among players by having each player repeatedly choose one thing from a pool of options and remove it from that pool, so every choice both advances the chooser's own plan and denies that option to everyone else. In the common pick-and-pass form, players get a hand of cards, keep one, then pass the rest to a neighbor, so hands rotate through the table over several rounds; in open/market drafts, players instead pick from a shared, refilling display. The tension comes from double-duty decisions: a pick is judged not just on its value to you but on how much you're taking away from opponents, and early picks reshape what later picks are worth to you.

## Notes
Multi-round pick-and-pass drafts conventionally alternate passing direction each round specifically to cancel out any seating-position bias.
Because picks are usually revealed immediately, drafting leans on visible counterpicking/denial rather than hidden information, which is what produces its signature 'track what's gone so I can infer what's coming back' overhead.
