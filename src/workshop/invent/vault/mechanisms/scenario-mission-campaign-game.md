# Scenario / Mission / Campaign Game

## Definition
The game is broken into discrete scenarios or missions, each with its own map, setup, objective, or win condition, and these are often strung together into a campaign where the outcome of one session (unlocks, upgrades, narrative branches, permanent losses) carries forward into the next. Tension arises because players must optimize two horizons at once: winning the immediate scenario and steering the long-run campaign trajectory, often trading short-term efficiency for durable advantages or story consequences. Because objectives and rules change from scenario to scenario, the game avoids a single static strategy and instead forces repeated re-adaptation.

## Notes
Difficulty and reward/punishment swings compound across linked scenarios faster than in single-session games, so campaign pacing (e.g. Gloomhaven vs. the deliberately gentler Jaws of the Lion) is a primary design lever.
Permanent consequences (stickers, scarred characters, destroyed components) buy narrative weight at the direct cost of replayability.
