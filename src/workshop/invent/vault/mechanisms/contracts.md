# Contracts

## Definition
Contracts give players standing objective cards, public or private, that specify a bundle of goods, resources, or a delivery to produce in exchange for a fixed payout in points or money. Players juggle several open contracts at once, deciding which to commit resources toward given their current production or position, and often must choose between contracts publicly visible to everyone versus a private hand only they can act on. The tension comes from opportunity cost under scarce resources and turns: taking on a contract locks you into a plan while opponents may race you to the same public one or draw better private ones, and unfulfilled contracts frequently carry an end-game penalty that punishes over-committing.

## Notes
Public contracts create a race for the same targets; private contracts remove that race but push the game toward multiplayer solitaire.
End-game penalties for unfulfilled contracts bias players toward conservative, low-variance picks rather than ambitious ones.
