# Ladder Climbing

## Definition
Ladder climbing establishes a hierarchy of plays where each player must match or exceed the previous card or card set value to continue participating in a round. Tension emerges from the escalating constraint: players must decide whether to commit higher cards, pass and bow out, or conserve resources for future rounds, creating a push-your-luck dynamic around card hierarchy and hand information.

## Notes
Round structure (who leads next) often rewards the last player to play, not the winner of the hand—this creates incentive structures that diverge from traditional trick-taking.
Works best with 2–5 players; scaling outside that range can amplify analysis paralysis or dead-range issues.
