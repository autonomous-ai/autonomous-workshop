# Take That

## Definition
Take That is a direct-interaction mechanism where a player's turn or card play is spent not on building their own position but on actively hindering a specific opponent — stealing resources, discarding their cards, skipping their turn, or redirecting an attack onto them. The tension comes from choosing a target: hitting the leader slows the game's frontrunner but invites retaliation, while hitting a weaker player can look petty or vindictive, so every disruptive action doubles as a social/political statement among the players.

## Notes
Frequently doubles as an informal catch-up mechanism because attacks gravitate toward whoever is visibly ahead, which can undercut a separate formal catch-up system if one exists.
Tolerance scales inversely with playtime: short, light games (Exploding Kittens) absorb repeated targeting as banter, while longer games risk a targeted player's frustration compounding into a hopeless spiral.
