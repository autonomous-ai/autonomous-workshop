# Matching

## Definition
Matching requires players to identify and pair corresponding elements—cards, tiles, or features—whether through memory, sequential play continuation, or attribute comparison. Tension emerges from the constraint that not all matches are always available; players must work within what's currently possible while navigating incomplete information about unseen or upcoming options.

## Relations
- risks:: [[anti-patterns/analysis-paralysis]], [[anti-patterns/dead-range]], [[anti-patterns/first-player-advantage]], [[anti-patterns/luck-swing-endgame]]

## Notes
Matching's success depends heavily on information state: memory-based games add randomness; sequential matching can devolve into rote play with no real decisions.
The mechanism anchors many classic games (dominoes, mahjong, shedding card games) by providing immediate feedback and clear correctness, making rule overhead minimal.
