# Character proportions and appeal

A figure's character is read from a few ratios before any detail: heads
tall, eye line, eye size, limb thickness and the dominant shape. Set them as
parameters and assert them; ornament comes after
([[product-aesthetics#making-a-figure-splendid]]).

## Head count sets the style

The head is the unit. Fewer heads put the eye on the head and read younger
and friendlier; more heads put the weight on the body.

| style | heads tall | head / height | reads as |
|---|---|---|---|
| fashion croquis | 9-11 | 0.09-0.11 | elegant, elongated |
| heroic | 8-8.5 | about 0.12 | power; Loomis's ideal has shoulders 2 1/3 heads wide, hips 1.5 |
| realistic adult | 7-7.5 | 0.13-0.14 | neutral, documentary |
| cartoon | 3-4 | 0.25-0.33 | friendly, young |
| chibi, super-deformed | 2-3 | 0.33-0.5 | cute; a Nendoroid is about 2.5 heads at about 100 mm |
| baby, kawaii, vinyl, blind box | 2 or under | 0.4-0.5+ | the head is the toy; the body is a stand |

**Print size picks the band.** A finger is about 1.2 % of stature (a 21 mm
index finger on a 1767 mm man,
[[body-dimensions-for-scale#hands-and-fingers-adults]]) and a wrist roughly
3 %. At 60 mm a 7.5-head figure has 2 mm wrists and 0.7 mm fingers, under the
pin minimum in
[[fdm-minimum-feature-sizes#features-pins-and-gaps]]. It needs about 100 mm
before the wrists reach 3 mm. Fingers never print at toy scale, so they become
mitts or fists. Under about 80 mm, stay at 4 heads or fewer. A charm or key
figure of 30-50 mm is a 2-3 head design. Short, thick limbs are where baby
schema and printability agree.

## Baby schema, measured

Lorenz (1943) listed the infant features that read as cute: a large head, a
high, bulging forehead, large eyes, chubby cheeks, a small nose and mouth,
short, thick limbs and a plump body. The effect is measured, it adds up
feature by feature, and it carries over to animals and objects:

- **Real infant faces** (Glocker 2009): edits within +-2 SD of face width,
  forehead, eye width, nose and mouth size moved cuteness and the wish to
  care, high > unmanipulated > low, all significant. The real infant mean puts
  the eye line at 43 % of head height above the chin, below the middle.
- **Eye height** (Sternglanz 1977, Geldart 1999): faces with features placed
  high were liked least. Medium and low beat high, and in drawings medium beat
  low. Lower the eye line, but not without limit.
- **Eye size** (Geldart 1999): 97 % of adults preferred the larger-eyed
  version of a drawing, and 63 % of a photograph. The effect is strongest on
  drawn, stylised faces, so a stylised figure can push eye size far past life.
- **Animals** (Borgi 2014): children aged 3-6 rated baby-schema faces cuter
  for humans, dogs and cats alike. The eyes took about 46 % of fixations, the
  nose 13 % and the mouth 6-7 %.
- **Teddy bears** (Hinde and Barden 1985): over 80 years, bears moved from a
  forehead ratio (eye-to-crown ÷ eye-to-chin) of 0.8 to 2.0, and from a snout
  ratio of 1.4 to 0.9. With 504 people aged 3-58 (Manzer and Harrington), the
  more infantile bear won every pair, and the forehead (eye placement)
  outweighed the snout, though people only ever named the snout. Children of
  4 showed no preference, of 6-8 did (Morris 1995): design for the buyer.
- **Mickey Mouse** (Gould 1979): as the character softened, his eye grew from
  27 to 42 % of head length, his head from 42.7 to 48.1 % of body length, and
  his ears moved back to round the forehead. The adult-coded rival's snout is
  80 % of head length, against Mickey's 49 %.

| measure | adult-coded | cute target |
|---|---|---|
| head ÷ total height | ≤ 0.14 | ≥ 0.33 |
| eye line above chin ÷ head height | 0.50 | 0.33-0.45 (infant 0.43, cutest bear 0.33) |
| eye width ÷ head width | 0.20 (fifths) | 0.20-0.30, a starting range to tune by render |
| inner eye gap ÷ eye width | 1.0 | 1.0-1.5 |
| snout or nose length ÷ head length | long (0.8 for the rival) | short (about 0.5 or less) |
| limb thickness | slender | thick; slender limbs read older |

## Shape language: one dominant shape

- **Circle** reads soft, friendly and cute; **square** strong, steady and
  dependable; **triangle** sharp, fast and dangerous. Choose from the
  direction's three words ([[product-aesthetics#one-direction-stated-before-any-shape]]).
- **One shape dominates the primary mass;** the others are accents (a round
  bear with triangle ears is friendly but alert). Competing shapes make the
  character hard to read, and a hard-to-read face lacks appeal.
- **Big, medium, small.** Head, body and limbs, or body, head and feet,
  differ clearly in size, never equally (Bancroft). This is the 70/30 rule
  of [[product-aesthetics#proportion-and-hierarchy]] applied to masses.
- **Straight against curve.** Set a straight line opposite a curve across a
  form: a straight back of the leg against a curved calf, a straight spine
  under a curved belly. That gives soft forms structure and direction; all
  curves read as dough. Draw contours as single C or S curves; a W or M
  contour breaks the flow.

## Silhouette and line of action

- Pass the black-silhouette test first ([[product-aesthetics#silhouette-and-stance]]).
- **One line of action.** Run a single gentle curve (a C) down the spine and
  exaggerate it beyond what life would give. A straight vertical spine reads
  static. Tilt the head off vertical and lean the torso along the curve.
- **Symmetric face, asymmetric pose.** A symmetrical face reads appealing.
  A mirrored pose (Disney's "twins") reads lifeless: offset one arm, turn the
  head, shift the weight onto one foot.
- **Identifying features on the outside of the curve,** where they extend the
  silhouette: ears, tail, horn, beak. A limb held clear of the body shows in
  the silhouette, but on a print it becomes a thin cantilever (below).

## Exaggerate what identifies, shrink the rest

Caricatures exaggerate how a face differs from the average. They were
recognised faster than true drawings, anticaricatures were recognised more
slowly, and the best likeness people chose was itself a caricature (Rhodes
1987). So name the subject's one to three identifying features (a rabbit's
ears, an owl's eyes and tufts, a fox's tail and snout), enlarge them and show
them in the main silhouette view. Shrink or delete the generic, drop detail
the print cannot render ([[printed-part-count#how-much-detail-is-enough]]),
and draw what stays bolder, not finer.

## Stylising an animal

Baby schema works the same on animals, so stylise in this order: enlarge the
head into the cartoon or chibi band; raise the forehead by moving the eyes
down and the ears back; shorten and thicken the snout; shorten and thicken
the legs; round the body. Once the snout and body are babyfied, a bear, a dog
and a cat converge on one round blob. The identifying feature is then all that
separates them, so it is the one part that is not shrunk: keep the ears long,
the tail big, the antlers or horn proud, the beak a clear contrasting shape
(the "archetype lost at size" row of [[product-aesthetics#failure-classes]];
plates, manes and tails: [[product-aesthetics#stylising-feathers-manes-and-tails]]).
A creature meant to stay fierce keeps a long snout and triangles; every gain
in eye size costs it menace.

## Faces at small scale

The eyes take about half of all looking time, so they are the focal feature
and take the detail budget; the nose and mouth shrink to a dot and a line.

- **Placement.** An adult has the eyes halfway from skull top to chin, one
  eye width apart and one from each outer corner to the side of the head.
  Cute puts the eye line below the middle (table above), the eyes large, the
  gap one to one and a half eye widths, and the nose a dot or nothing.
- **Size check.** At 40 mm a 2.5-head figure has a 16 mm head. Eyes at 0.24 ×
  head width are 3.8 mm, and a quarter-eye highlight is 1 mm, at the
  two-line limit of a 0.4 mm nozzle. The highlight is the first feature the
  slicer drops: enlarge the eye or leave the highlight out.

| eye method | how | limit |
|---|---|---|
| colour inlay | a flush eye body cut from the head, printed in colour; the only way on a field skin ([[smooth-skin-from-a-field]]) | light over dark needs at least 3 layers ([[fdm-multi-material-design#design-rules]]); measure the union ([[fdm-multi-material-design#inlays-fool-the-mesh-gates-measure-the-union]]) |
| socket and raised pupil | a sunk socket with a pupil dome standing in it; one colour reads by shadow | same-colour relief vanishes on black or white bodies: contrast it or give it 1.0 mm of height ([[fdm-multi-material-design#design-rules]]) |
| dimple | a spherical pocket that reads as a dark dot under top light | smallest and cheapest; the minimum cut widths are in [[fdm-minimum-feature-sizes#text-logos-and-surface-details]] |
| inserted bead | a glued bead or plug | for children it must not come off ([[toy-safety-constraints#small-parts-the-choke-test]]) |

## Print-driven proportion limits

- **Thin limbs, tails and horns are cantilevers.** For root diameter `d`,
  length `L` and a push `F` at the tip, `sigma = 32 F L / (pi d^3)`. A 20 mm
  tail standing in Z (31 MPa across layers,
  [[layer-anisotropy#how-much-weaker-across-the-layers]]) under an
  assumed 10 N finger push with a factor of 2 needs `d >= 5.1 mm`; laid flat
  in XY (63 MPa) it needs 4.0 mm. Fillet the root and stack the factors as in
  [[beam-and-plate-stiffness#safety-factors-for-printed-plastic]].
- **Necks.** A big head on a thin neck is the weakest section. On a toy for
  under-8s the torque test puts 0.45 N·m on the head
  ([[toy-safety-constraints#use-and-abuse-tests-what-breaks-off-means]]):
  `tau = 16 T / (pi d^3)` with half of 31 MPa in shear and a factor of 2
  gives `d >= 6.7 mm`. Chibi necks are short or absent: seat the head wide.
- **Big heads make the pose's lean count.** On a 2-3 head figure the head is
  roughly half the volume (a 24 mm sphere holds 7.2 cm³, as much as a
  16 × 36 mm body). A head moved x forward moves the centre of mass about x/2.
  Worked: 60 mm tall, centre of mass at 33 mm, feet 12 mm deep: atan(6/33) =
  10.3°. Lean the head 4 mm forward and it drops to atan(4/33) = 6.9°. Keep
  the head's centre over the feet, hollow the head, and use big feet, which
  are in style; 10° is the table-top floor ([[stability-and-tipping#how-much-angle-is-enough]],
  [[stability-and-tipping#making-it-stand]]).
- **Keep the face uncut.** Cut where the design already has a line
  ([[fdm-joining-split-prints#where-to-cut]]): the neck or collar, the
  hairline, or a plane behind the face. A left-right split runs the seam down
  the nose. It suits a long-faced animal read in profile
  ([[carved-figures-on-split-prints]]), never a big-eyed front-facing
  character. Make glued seams flush
  ([[form-and-finish-heuristics#seams-and-parting-lines]]). A head printed as
  its own part also avoids supports under a round chin
  ([[overhangs-and-print-orientation]]).

## Parameters and asserts

```python
TOTAL_H, HEAD_H, HEAD_W = 60.0, 24.0, 25.0   # chibi band, 2.5 heads
EYE_Z = 0.40 * HEAD_H        # eye line above the chin
EYE_W = 0.24 * HEAD_W
EYE_GAP = 1.2 * EYE_W        # between the inner corners
assert 1 / 3 <= HEAD_H / TOTAL_H <= 1 / 2, "chibi: head is a third to a half"
assert EYE_Z / HEAD_H < 0.5, "eye line at or above mid-head reads adult"
assert EYE_Z / HEAD_H >= 0.30, "below the cutest measured bear (0.33)"
assert EYE_W <= EYE_GAP <= 1.5 * EYE_W, "eyes neither crossed nor drifting"
assert HIGHLIGHT_D >= 2 * LINE_W or HIGHLIGHT_D == 0, "slicer drops it"
assert LIMB_D >= (32 * F_PUSH * LIMB_L * SF / (math.pi * SIGMA_Z)) ** (1 / 3)
assert math.degrees(math.atan(D_MIN / COM_Z)) >= 10, "CoM with the head's real lean"
```

## Review checklist

Review at the print's real size on screen as well as zoomed in:

1. Style band named; head ratio asserted.
2. Eye line below mid-head for cute, at mid-head for adult or heroic.
3. Eyes the largest, contrasting detail; every face feature passes the
   minimum-feature sizes or is dropped.
4. One dominant shape; big, medium, small masses; straights against curves.
5. Identifying features enlarged and in the silhouette.
6. One line of action, the head tilted, the pose not mirrored.
7. Thinnest limb, neck and tail pass the cantilever and torque asserts.
8. Tipping angle holds with the head's real offset.
9. Split lines on existing lines; the face uncut.
