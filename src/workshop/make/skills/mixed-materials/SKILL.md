# Mixed-material products

Autonomous's engineers and workers fabricate, assemble, inspect, and ship the
finished product. Customers receive that product. The internal BOM, sourcing,
fabrication patterns, assembly instructions, and engineering evidence serve
the workshop; customer assets show the complete object and its experience.

This capability applies to Spark Make. Continue the same Make Goal and its
existing CAD, motion, powered-product, visual-review, and finalizer process.
Manufacturing and shipment remain later human/host-authorized operations.

## Design the complete object

Choose materials for their function and appearance: stiffness, mass, friction,
elasticity, softness, translucency, acoustic response, or a surface people use.
Use the workshop's printing, laser cutting, CNC, woodworking, textile, and
handcraft capabilities where useful. A material family is not a qualified
stock specification, and a process label is not a machine-ready toolpath.

Resolve fit-critical stock and purchased components before fixing their mates.
Use `step-parts` and `cad` for purchased geometry and mounts. Use
`electromechanical-integration` for even a simple motor, light, or powered
load. The initial product territory is mechanical toys and simple battery
circuits; a controller, firmware, network, or custom PCB needs a separate
explicit brief. No purchasing or manufacturing authority comes from this skill.

Give every installed part a stable component identity and every placed copy a
named occurrence. Model the full assembled shape, including nonprinted parts,
cord routes, springs, fabric/card shapes, hardware, and purchased components.
Use documented approximate envelopes when exact geometry is unavailable and
record that limitation internally. Hidden parts may be occluded in the beauty
view; they still belong to the complete assembly and BOM.

Use the descriptor generated by CAD, not an independently authored occurrence
list. `gen --write` places it at
`<cad-project>/__cadgen__/models/<entry.step.py>/assembly.json`. Copy those exact
bytes to root `assembled.step.json` and `internal/assembled.step.json` before
finalization removes derived caches; bind both copies to the final full STEP.

Keep the part's identity, material/specification, fabrication method, stock,
and placed occurrences distinct. One sheet can make several panels; a cord
spool can supply several cut lengths. Record stock totals, waste/spares when
needed, consumed adhesives, and reusable tools separately from installed parts.

## Produce the workshop handoff

Read [the manifest contract](references/manifest.md) before writing the BOM.
Write `internal/manufacturing.json` inside the Make product root and bind it
from root `product.json` using `manufacturing.schema_version`,
`manufacturing.manifest_path`, and `manufacturing.manifest_sha256`.

Each component identifies its material, process, quantity, occurrence names,
specification, and exact supporting files. Purchased parts include sourced
dimensions and sourcing information. Fabricated parts link their stock and
source/drawing/instruction files. Assembly steps name the components,
consumables, tools, attachment actions, and necessary checks. Engineers should
be able to identify what remains physically untested without reverse-engineering
the render or a CAD script.

- Printed components use explicit `PRINTABLE = True` source entries and retain
  the existing nozzle-specific print gates. Nonprinted source entries use
  `PRINTABLE = False`; name auxiliary entries `part_<role>.step.py` so the CAD
  project keeps its single combined entry. Never print-gate wood, card, cloth,
  rubber bands, or purchased motors merely because they appear in the scene.
- Flat stock needs its thickness, outline, holes, fold/score locations, and
  assembled pose. A calibrated PDF drawing can explain cutting and folding.
  STEP remains the geometry deliverable; this MVP adds no DXF, mesh export,
  CNC toolpath generation, or machine control.
- Cords and wires need diameter, cut length, endpoints, route, slack, and
  attachment/connection details. Bands and springs need free dimensions,
  installed configuration, and sourced operating properties where function
  depends on them. A rendered curve cannot establish stiffness or fatigue.
- Felt, fabric, and foam need thickness and a joining method. Casting and
  handcraft need the chosen material's preparation, finishing, and cure/dry
  instructions. Digital geometry does not establish those processes worked.

## Present the finished product

Render the complete assembled geometry with its intended colors and material
appearance. Preserve card thickness, textile volume, visible cords and hardware,
and the bought components that contribute to the silhouette. Use the existing
Make inspection and blind-review images to check the finished object. Never
use an invented marketing image to fill in missing physical components.

Place only deliberately public assets under `public/`: an exact copy of the
complete assembled STEP, the reviewed finished-product hero, optional motion
imagery, and customer-facing content. Declare every public file and its hash in
`public_assets`. Keep this a presentation copy of Make's existing approved
bytes, not a second final render family; for example use `public/hero.png`
copied from the canonical `snap/iso.png`. A public page describes what the
product does, its finished appearance, and use. It contains no BOM, supplier
list, fabrication instructions, workshop source, or prototype diagnosis.

All other Make files remain internal. Product title and summary are customer
copy; keep manufacturing details out of those fields and out of public HTML,
documents, metadata, and media. The host copies the selected public files
byte-for-byte and does not render, redesign, or validate geometry again.

Validate after the source, assembly, public copies, and manifest are stable:

```bash
"$WORKSHOP_PYTHON" .agents/skills/mixed-materials/scripts/manufacturing_manifest.py \
  <product-root> --cad-project-path <cad-project-relative-to-product>
```

The normal Spark Make finalizer runs the same structural check before sealing.
It checks identities, references, occurrence coverage, manufacturing routes,
and public selection. It does not prove fabrication, electrical performance,
elastic force, assembly success, or human enjoyment. Record physical prototype
work honestly for Autonomous's engineers; do not claim it happened from CAD.
