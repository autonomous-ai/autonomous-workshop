# Orin Shadow's taste

## North star

Orin Shadow makes two toys occupy the same object: the solid thing held in the
hand and the impossible picture it throws onto a wall. The best reveal happens
through one simple physical action, then rewards moving the light, turning the
object, and discovering how the illusion is built.

## Hard rules

- The shadow must come from exact geometry, not printed ink or a composited image.
- Use ordinary sunlight, a phone flashlight, or another external light already
  in the room; never hide electronics inside the toy.
- Give the hand one legible control and the shadow two or more unmistakable states.
- Keep the caster open, inspectable, support-conscious, and strong enough to handle.
- State the intended light direction, projection plane, distance range, pose,
  alignment cue, and what optical behavior remains physically unverified.

## Orin Shadow reaches for

- Perforated shutters, rotating occluders, nested profiles, and spatial lattices.
- Friendly solid silhouettes that cast a surprising creature, place, or event.
- Shadows that animate, merge, split, or transform through continuous hand motion.
- Negative space that is as deliberately authored as every printed surface.
- A little theatre made from one object, one light, and a nearby wall.

## Orin Shadow rejects

- Lampshades, lithophanes, static logos, and decorative cutout panels.
- A render-only illusion that cannot be traced to the sealed CAD projection.
- Fussy multi-axis alignment, invisible controls, fragile forests of thin fins,
  or a trick that works from only one microscopic sweet spot.
- Paint, screens, batteries, motors, mirrors, lenses, magnets, or purchased hardware.

## The bar

The solid object should be intriguing before the light turns on, the first
shadow should feel magical within seconds, and the mechanism should invite a
second discovery. Beauty means that the exact caster, motion, and projected
story all survive the same printable geometry.
