## Dominant strategy

There is a strong default, but not a line that is correct every turn. From the setup sky, the first player has two reaches into three occupied, indistinguishable Serpent wells. Pulling any one is equivalent. If it is safe, pulling a second is better than stopping on base points alone: after a first moon the incremental expectation is `(18*2 + 15*1 - 14*1) / 47 = 37/47`, and after a first star it is `(17*2 + 16*1 - 14*2) / 47 = 22/47`. So the opening reach sequence is effectively forced until a void appears. The end-of-turn rotation is still consequential. A one-groove move preserves three reaches next turn, but a larger move can deny a successor a public face-up tile or the constellation that would complete their shortest slot. The value of that denial changes with the visible rails, exposed tiles, remaining void count, player count, and turns left, so one groove is not universally correct.

## Real decisions

The opening has one real decision, the sky rotation; the choice among the three hidden Serpent wells is fake and the second pull after a safe first pull is mathematically favored. Developed turns normally have two to four real decisions: which constellation or exposed tile to reach for, whether to TAKE certainty or PULL for a larger catch, whether to stop as the catch grows relative to the public void risk, and which disc/direction/distance best trades successor denial against the player's own next reach allowance. Wells of the same constellation that are all knob-up remain equivalent, but they do not erase the cross-constellation, exposed-tile, stop, and rotation decisions.

## Reaching an ending

The ending is unconditional. The dawn peg advances once after every turn, including busts and turns with no usable well, and the game stops after turn 24. Depletion cannot stall it: when the bowl empties, wells may remain empty, but turns and dawn advancement continue. Banking is also reachable because any non-void catch banks on a voluntary stop or exhaustion of reaches, while voids permanently leave the pool and make later pulls safer.

## Length

There are exactly 24 turns total. At roughly 45 to 75 seconds for one to three reaches, a stop decision, refill, and a spatial rotation choice, play is about 18 to 30 minutes, with setup and early learning bringing the stated 30-minute target into range. The clock does not scale upward with player count.

## Player count

Twenty-four turns gives 12 turns each at two players, eight each at three, and six each at four. At two, every sky is direct opponent interaction and denial can be weighed against one's own next-turn reach loss. At four, each player still materially constrains their clockwise successor; visible rails and face-up tiles supply concrete targets, so it does not collapse into parallel solitaire. The incentive to spend two or three grooves is weaker when it harms only one of three opponents, but leader denial and late-game exposed tiles keep those moves situationally live. Equal turns remove a basic seat-count imbalance at every supported count.
