# Blindcap: Duel — prior-art check

Checked 2026-08-23. This is a product-design search, not legal advice, a trademark opinion or a freedom-to-operate opinion.

## Bottom line

Do not claim that Blindcap invented the broad idea of using a probe to reveal concealed game information. That territory has clear prior art.

The defensible product distinction is the complete combination: movable player-planted pieces with a keyed shank and two optional parallel tunnels; two marked angled tile channels; the probe's resting height as both result and public board state retained through the decision phase; visible owner but hidden species; and partial deduction feeding spatial grove building plus commitment-before-reveal scoring. I did not find that complete combination in the sources below. Absence from this search is not proof that it does not exist.

## Closest mechanical precedents

### US 2,889,634 — Educational game board with sensing probe, filed 1958

Source: https://patents.google.com/patent/US2889634A/en

This is the closest simple-mechanical precedent. It describes hidden intermediate stops inside sockets and a depth-sensing probe that distinguishes blocked from unblocked sockets. Its purpose is checking predetermined quiz answers; it does not describe keyed movable hidden-species pieces, two independently marked routes per piece, permanent public probe records, placement or area scoring. Google Patents lists the US patent as expired, while warning that its legal-status field is not a legal conclusion.

### US 3,866,920 — Playpiece and base member selectively interengageable to actuate effect means, filed 1974

Source: https://www.freepatentsonline.com/3866920.html

This uses shallow and deep receptacles plus operative/dummy pins. Relative orientation determines whether an electrical signal lights a piece. It is further evidence that mechanically or electrically reading different recess depths in game parts is old. It does not use retained probe heights as public information or Blindcap's deduction/scoring structure.

### Black Box, mid-1970s

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Box_%28game%29

Black Box charges the seeker for rays used to infer hidden atoms. In the original two-player form a hider, book or computer resolves each hit/reflection/detour. It is the strongest rules analogue for scarce probes and inference, but not a self-adjudicating movable-piece mechanism or spatial claiming game.

### Electronic Stratego, 1982

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratego#Electronic_Stratego

Electronic Stratego gives every piece a unique bump pattern read by an electronic board. A player may spend a turn probing an opponent piece to hear an approximate strength. This is close on hidden piece identity and an explicit probe action. Blindcap's distinction is passive printed geometry, two independently readable bits, persistent owner-marked results, player placement and grove scoring—not the generic idea of probing an opponent's hidden piece.

### US 6,027,408 — Interactive probe game, filed in the 1990s

Source: https://patents.justia.com/patent/6027408

This describes a powered hand probe detecting passive concealed devices in a board or movable pieces and returning light or sound. Again, it occupies the broad electronic-probe concept, not Blindcap's passive depth states or rules combination.

## Theme and title checks

Searches for `"Blindcap: Duel" game`, `"Blindcap" tabletop game`, `"Blindcap" trademark game` and `"Blindcap" mushroom game` returned unrelated swimming-cap, pipe/electronics and fantasy references, not a tabletop product. The earlier candidate search also found a mushroom card game named Toadstone, which is why that title was abandoned.

Before public sale:

- Run an actual trademark clearance in the intended classes and territories.
- Have counsel review the final mechanism against live patent families, not only the expired/older examples above.
- Preserve dated CAD, rules, search queries and public disclosure records.

## Claims allowed in copy

- "The printed geometry resolves every probe."
- "No app or moderator."
- "A probe's height is both the answer and the board's memory until harvest."
- "Two buried bits, three probes, three claims."

## Claims not supported

- "First ever."
- "Patented" or "patent-pending."
- "No game has done this before."
- "Legally cleared."
