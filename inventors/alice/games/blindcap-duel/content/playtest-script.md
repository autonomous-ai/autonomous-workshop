# Blindcap: Duel — blind playtest protocol

The facilitators do not teach strategy and do not answer a legality question by suggesting a move. One facilitator observes; neither player has seen the design files or earlier rules.

## Minimum evidence before public sale

- 3 independent pairs.
- 2 complete games per pair, swapping the initial starter in game two.
- 6 games total.
- New printed probes for at least two groups, or a recorded inspection that all probe tips and channels remain within the same fit tolerance.

## Materials to record

- Rules PDF SHA-256.
- STL/project manifest SHA-256.
- Printer, material, layer height and calibration profile.
- Player consent receipt and anonymous group/trial IDs.
- Full move log: seed species, plant socket, action, target socket/channel, observed probe height, crown target.
- Final species map, groves, scores and every tiebreak quantity.
- Any forced intervention or rules question.

## Procedure

1. Put the unopened set and printed rules in front of the pair. Start the timer.
2. Let them set up without instruction. Record the first point where either asks for help.
3. Randomly assign owner marks. Randomly choose the first starter in game one; swap it in game two.
4. During play, record whether each probe result is called correctly from a normal seated position before anyone touches the pin again.
5. At harvest, let the players identify groves and score unaided. Independently recompute the result afterward.
6. Between games, allow only the rules to change their understanding. Do not give strategy advice.

## Hard failures

- Any legal probe gives an ambiguous low/high reading.
- A player can see a rival shank from a normal seated position.
- A pin collides with a neighbouring cap or pin.
- A mushroom, crown or tile joint requires damaging force.
- Players cannot complete setup, a turn, harvest or scoring from the rules.
- The move sequence ever lacks a legal required action.

## Player questions after each game

Rate 1–7, then explain the rating:

1. I understood what each probe told me.
2. I cared where I planted each mushroom.
3. Choosing when to probe versus crown was difficult in a good way.
4. I could read the board without remembering hidden facts in my head.
5. I would play again immediately.

Also ask:

- Which decision felt most important?
- When did you first form a theory about a rival grove?
- Did any probe feel wasted? Why?
- Could you predict the winner before harvest?
- What rule did you have to reread?

## Acceptance thresholds

- 100% correct physical readings across all probes.
- 0 rules interventions after the first complete game for each pair.
- At least 5 of 6 games reach harvest and score correctly without facilitator repair; any aborted game is investigated and replayed, not silently excluded.
- Starting-seat win-rate difference no greater than 20 percentage points in this small human sample; keep collecting rather than claiming balance from six games.
- Median ratings of at least 5/7 for probe clarity, decision tension and immediate replay interest.
- No dominant opening repeated by all three groups unless counterplay appears in game two.

Simulation can reject a version before this test. It cannot replace this test.
