# Art direction — Midnight Manhattan

Status: governing direction for the digital prototype.

## Character

**Nocturnal. Architectural. Heirloom.**

The set should look at home in an architect's studio or a dark penthouse. It
borrows Manhattan's visual grammar—setbacks, vertical ribs, masonry bands,
water tanks, parapets, bridge cantilevers, and the street grid—without copying
one protected building or assigning a tourist landmark to every rank.

## The two sides

- **Stone** — warm limestone in product imagery; broad horizontal setback bands
  and one tactile groove around every base.
- **Steel** — midnight graphite; restrained vertical fins and two tactile base
  channels held between three broad printable rails.

Both sides keep the same rank envelopes so chess remains legible. Geometry, not
color alone, must identify the side from overhead and by touch.

## Rank silhouettes

- **Pawn — rooftop water tower.** A stout tapered drum, domed roof, and solid
  stepped plinth. No spindly legs.
- **Rook — corner tower.** Square plan, four deep parapet notches, visibly
  heavier than every minor piece.
- **Knight — bridge cantilever.** The only directional silhouette: a forward
  prow, deep arched eye, and stepped rear mass. It must still read as a knight,
  not a literal bridge model.
- **Bishop — avenue cut.** A narrow tower with one bold diagonal negative-space
  crown cut, preserving the classic bishop cue without delicate tracery.
- **Queen — fan crown.** The widest crown, three robust Art Deco fins, and open
  shoulders.
- **King — neo-Gothic peak.** Tallest piece, narrow stepped crown, and a chunky
  cross-shaped antenna with no needle spire.

## Board

The board is a calm street plan, not 64 miniature buildings. A seamless
two-level square field and orthogonal border streets preserve instant grid
reading. One restrained, stepped Broadway cue stays wholly in the outer border
so it cannot confuse legal squares.

## Product palette

- Board: charcoal slate.
- Stone: bone limestone.
- Steel: midnight graphite.
- Accent: oxidized copper, used sparingly in imagery or optional finishing.
- Light: warm raking dusk with a cool night fill.

## Forbidden shortcuts

- Statue of Liberty queen, Empire State king, taxi pawns, skyline clip art.
- Generic Staunton bodies with buildings pasted on top.
- Logos, building names, text labels, or color-only side identity.
- Needle spires, thin fire escapes, cables, unsupported railings, open windows,
  or façade noise that exists only for a render.
- Cute faces, twee colors, or gift-shop caricature.
- Presenting the mood image as CAD, print, fit, inventory, or assembly evidence.
