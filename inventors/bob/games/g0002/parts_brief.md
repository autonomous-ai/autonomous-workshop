---

## 1. The wound

**Mechanism:** the *ordinal lock* — one printed plug in one printed shell whose
rotation angle physically counts how many chambers are correct **in order from
the front**, and stops at the first mistake.

**What it must DO (measurable):** with five hidden pins loaded and a key inserted
with five slider settings, one steady clockwise push turns the plug to exactly
one of six angles and stops dead there:

| first wrong chamber | plug stops at | pointer reads |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4° | `0` |
| 2 | 20° | `1` |
| 3 | 36° | `2` |
| 4 | 52° | `3` |
| 5 | 68° | `4` |
| none | 90° | `OPEN` + latch click |

Correctness of chambers *after* the first mistake must have **zero** effect on
the angle. The stop must be a hard mechanical wall, repeatable to ±2°, on all
8⁸ ≈ 16.7 M slider/pin combinations, with no reading achievable by wiggling.

**Why print is load-bearing:** the whole signal is a 1.2 mm height ladder read
through a 0.4 mm shear gap by gravity-fed loose pins. Cardboard needs a lookup
table and a trusted human — that human is the referee the hood exists to delete.
Injection moulding locks one fixed pin catalogue behind a parting seam that runs
straight through the shear line.

**Physics question to prove first (golden part, §8):** *does a one-rung (1.2 mm)
error reliably stop the plug while a correct chamber reliably passes, across all
8 rungs, on our printer?* One 1.5 h, ~$1.50 single-chamber test cell answers it.
Nothing else gets printed until it does.

**Signature part** (rules_lint wants exactly one): `shell_01` + `plug_01` as the
assembled lock. It is what a shopper remembers and what four table seats
independently named as "the one physical piece this game could not exist
without".

## 2. How the mechanism works (the builder must understand this before cutting geometry)

Gravity-fed pin tumbler, plug axis horizontal, chambers vertical at 12 o'clock,
no springs.

Radii from the plug axis, all in mm:

- **plug OD land = 22.0**
- **shear radius = 22.4** ← the pin top and the driver nose both sit here when a chamber is correct
- **shell bore = 22.8**

So a correct chamber leaves a **0.4 mm gap above and below** the shear plane.

Per chamber *i* (i = 1..5), with S₀=4°, S₁=20°, S₂=36°, S₃=52°, S₄=68°:

- **Correct** — pin top at 22.4. The driver's flange rests on the shell shoulder
  and holds the driver's nose at 22.4, hovering 0.4 mm clear of the plug land.
  Nothing in either direction touches. The plug is free.
- **Slider set too low** (stack short by n×1.2) — the pin never lifted the driver
  to the shoulder, so the driver's nose sits n×1.2 mm **below** the plug land. It
  drops into the plug's **gate notch** (arc `[−Sᵢ₋₁ , 0]`, depth 3.4) and rides
  it until the notch's radial end wall. Plug stops at **Sᵢ₋₁**.
- **Slider set too high** (stack tall by n×1.2) — the pin top protrudes n×1.2 mm
  **above** the shell bore. It rides the shell's **arc channel** (arc
  `[0 , +Sᵢ₋₁]`, depth 9.4) until that channel's radial end wall. Plug stops at
  **Sᵢ₋₁**.

Both error directions therefore stop chamber *i* at the same angle Sᵢ₋₁, and
S is strictly increasing, so the plug stops at S of the **lowest-index** wrong
chamber. That is the ordinal reading, generated by geometry, with no state, no
electronics and nothing to fudge. Chamber 1 has a 4°-long notch and a 4°-long
channel — a wrong chamber 1 twitches and dies at the `0` mark.

Three consequences the builder must not "improve away":

1. The **flange-on-shoulder** datum is the whole measurement. It must be a
   crisp flat annular face, printed with the shoulder facing up.
2. The **end walls** of every notch and channel are **radial faces, 0° draft, no
   chamfer, no fillet above 0.3 mm**. A ramp there turns a stop into a climb.
3. The **fall of a too-low driver is capped by the notch floor** (3.4 mm). A
   driver 7 rungs low hangs by its nose on the notch floor and still meets the
   end wall. This is why the notch does not need to be 8.4 mm deep.

## 4. The ladder

8 rungs, **1.2 mm step** (was 0.4 mm — see §9.1).

| rung | pin height | slider setting | lifter top above blade roof |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3.0 | 1 | 9.8 |
| 2 | 4.2 | 2 | 8.6 |
| 3 | 5.4 | 3 | 7.4 |
| 4 | 6.6 | 4 | 6.2 |
| 5 | 7.8 | 5 | 5.0 |
| 6 | 9.0 | 6 | 3.8 |
| 7 | 10.2 | 7 | 2.6 |
| 8 | 11.4 | 8 | 1.4 |

`lifter(s) + pin(r) = 12.8` exactly when `s = r`. Stack error = `1.2 × (r − s)`,
range ±8.4 mm.

The slider tabs are **stepped bars with 8 flat lands**, not ramps: a detent that
is 0.5 mm off in travel produces **zero** height error. That single choice
removes the largest term from the tolerance stack and is not negotiable.

## 5. Arithmetic — one legal solution exists

Coupled dims checked, all mm:

| # | constraint | check | margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | bore working length `B = 22.4 − 9.6` | **12.8** | — |
| C2 | tallest pin + shortest lifter ≤ B | 11.4 + 1.4 = **12.8** ≤ 12.8 | 0.00 (equality by construction) |
| C3 | lifter travel = ladder span | 9.8 − 1.4 = 8.4 = 7 × 1.2 | exact |
| C4 | correct chamber clears both faces | plug 22.0 < shear 22.4 < bore 22.8 | **0.40 each side** |
| C5 | 1-rung engagement | 1.2 − 0.4 = **0.80** | 0.50 after ±0.30 RSS print error |
| C6 | notch floor above the keyway roof corner | 18.60 vs corner r 13.10 | **5.50** — no breakthrough |
| C7 | arc channel swallows max over-stack | depth 9.40 ≥ 8.40 | **1.00** |
| C8 | shell material between channel roof and shoulder | 34.6 − 32.2 | **2.40** |
| C9 | keyway corners inside the plug | max corner r 18.70 < 22.0 | **3.30** wall |
| C10 | chamber pitch vs feature width | 16.0 vs 6.9 | **9.10** |
| C11 | blade reaches chamber 5 | blade 96 vs chamber 5 at 88 | **8.00** |
| C12 | driver body = shoulder − shear | 34.6 − 22.4 = **12.2** | exact |
| C13 | pin cannot fall through the roof aperture | pin ⌀6.20 vs aperture ⌀5.40 | **0.40** shelf per side |
| C14 | lifter passes the aperture with blade side-play | lifter ⌀4.80 + 0.30 play vs ⌀5.40 | **0.30** |
| C15 | every part fits the bed | max 142 × 40 × 26 vs 251³ | **109** |

**The tolerance stack that decides the game (C5).** Chain from the shoulder
datum: shoulder flat ±0.10 → driver body ±0.10 → pin height ±0.10 → lifter land
±0.10 → blade roof ±0.10. RSS ≈ **±0.22**, worst case ±0.50. With a 0.40 mm
half-gap and a 1.20 mm step: a correct chamber survives up to ±0.40 of stack
error, and a one-rung error still engages by 0.80 − 0.22 = **0.58 mm** at RSS.
Worst-case-everything gives 0.30 mm of engagement, which still stops a
finger-torqued plug. **This is the number the golden part proves.**

**Pre-authorised fallback if the golden part fails.** Do not redesign — apply
this table and reprint:

| dim | build value | fallback |
|---|---|---|
| ladder step | 1.2 | **1.5** |
| pin heights | 3.0…11.4 | 3.0…13.5 |
| `B` | 12.8 | 14.5 |
| plug roof face r | 9.6 | 7.9 |
| shell arc channel depth | 9.4 | 11.5 |
| shell shoulder r | 34.6 | 36.7 |
| driver body length | 12.2 | 14.3 |
| shell height | 84 | 89 |
| lifter travel / rod travel | 8.4 / 28.0 | 10.5 / 28.0 (land step 1.5) |

Everything else is unchanged. No other dimension in this brief is coupled to the
step.

## 6. Hidden state

The only public signal is the pointer. Everything below must stay dark:

- `hood_01`: **4 perimeters, 2.5 mm minimum wall, zero gaps, one open face**
  (Locksmith side). Seats 3.0 mm into the shell rebate so there is no sight line
  under the skirt. Print open-face-down: no seam on a sight path.
- `shell_01` arc channels are **blind** — closed outward by ≥3.0 mm of solid
  wall. A protruding pin must never be visible or feelable from outside.
- `lever_01` lids the chimney row in all three of its positions. Driver stem
  height reveals *too high / too low* per chamber; it must never be readable
  from a Cracker's seat even with the hood momentarily off.
- Plug keyway walls ≥2.5 mm and opaque. The bore apertures are the only
  openings into the chambers.
- Pin rung numerals are engraved on the pin **side**, not the top face. The top
  face is the shear datum and must stay flat (see §9.8).

### ⚠️ Flag, loudly, now rather than at the build gate

**21 h is over budget and the shell is 6 h of it.** Two levers, in order of
preference:

1. **Open-frame the shell.** It is currently a box with a bore through it. It

2. **Ship 2 key blanks instead of 3** (−1.4 h). Costs real feel: at 4 players

`pin_r*` or `key_01`. Those four are the tolerance chain; they stay at

## 8. Golden part — print this and nothing else, first

**`GP1` — single-chamber test cell**, ≈1.5 h, ≈$1.50:

- stub plug: 60 mm long, ⌀44, one bore at mid-length, one gate notch at 36°,
  one keyway section, printed on the real machine at the real settings
- stub shell block: cradle + one chimney with the shoulder + one 36° arc channel
- 1 × `slug_01`, all 8 × `pin_r*`, one stub key with one slider (8 settings)

**Test:** all 64 (pin, slider) pairs. Pass = **64/64**:

- 8 matched pairs → the stub plug turns freely to 36° and beyond, every time
- 56 mismatched pairs → the stub plug stops dead at 0–4°, every time, and does
  not release under a firm two-finger twist

**Also proved on the same part, same hour:**

- pins and slugs fall freely under their own weight, lock upright, 20 cycles
- pulling the key at 20° jams it, and the `RESET` slide position frees it
- a correct chamber does not scrape the plug land (listen for it)

**64/64 → build.** **≤62/64 → apply the §5 fallback table and reprint GP1.**
Nothing downstream is printed until GP1 passes. The receipt this exists for:
text2cad burned $102 proving a brief had zero legal parameter pairs. GP1 costs
$1.50 to prove this one has 64.

## 9. Reconciliation with rules.md / idea.json / bill.json

Everything below is a **deliberate, recorded** disagreement. The builder follows
this brief; `rules.md` needs the noted text edits before publish.

1. **Ladder step 0.4 mm → 1.2 mm.** `idea.json` calls the game "a 0.4 mm
   tolerance ladder" and `rules.md` prints rungs 3.0–5.8. FDM cannot hold it:
   a 0.4 mm step with a 0.4 mm shear gap leaves **negative** engagement before
   the first tolerance is counted. Rungs stay **8**, labels stay **1–8**, so
   **gameplay is unchanged** — the sim (1000 games × 3 counts, all gates pass)
   and the four LLM tables only ever touched rung *labels*. This also settles
   the rules_lens finding that idea.json's "3.0–6.0" disagreed with the bill's
   3.0–5.8: both are superseded by **3.0–11.4**.
2. **Deduction cards → paper pad.** 4 print-in-place shutter cards = 160
   shutters, ~6 print-hours, a large failure surface, for a component the rules
   describe as "memory aid only — it never forces a play". Rules edit: setup
   step 2, "open every shutter on your card" → "take a fresh deduction sheet";
   reset text likewise.
3. **Cut rail → cut tray + 5 marker pegs.** The rail was 5 print-in-place
   thumbwheels. A tray of 8 columns × 5 sockets with pegs is cheaper to print
   **and** better: rules §5 Load step 2 currently has the table cross-check
   dialled numbers against the pins, which the tray makes automatic. Rules edit:
   "dial their rungs onto the cut rail in ascending order" → "drop one marker
   into the tray column of each rung". The §8 void-round case for a mismatched
   rail applies unchanged to markers.
4. **Score board + 4 pegs → paper score pad.** Round board kept — the time rail
   bleeding down is the round's tension and belongs on the table as an object.
5. **Key blanks 4 → 3.** The Locksmith never probes, so a 4-player game has at
   most 3 Crackers. `bill.json` said "per_player: true, qty 4"; it should be
   "one per Cracker seat, qty 3". Rules edit: setup step 2, "each player takes
   one key blank" → "each Cracker takes a key blank; the Locksmith takes none".
6. **Key envelope 132 × 30 × 14 → 142 × 40 × 26.** The blade must carry an
   8.4 mm lifter range and five 28 mm slider travels. The bow grew to hold the
   sliders, and the sliders are now **public and readable across the table**,
   which the rules require ("Settings are public").
7. **Lock envelope grew.** `plug_01` ⌀34 × 96 → **⌀44 × 110**; `shell_01`
   118 × 74 × 66 → **132 × 78 × 84**. Forced by C1/C2 (bore length 12.8 must
   exceed the 11.4 tallest pin) and C7 (the shell must swallow an 8.4 mm
   over-stack). Both still fit the bed with >100 mm to spare.
8. **Pin rung engraved on the top face → on the side.** The top face is the
   shear datum. An embossed numeral there would sit *in* the 0.4 mm gap and
   break the measurement. Rules edit: "The rung number is engraved on the top
   face" → "on the side of the pin".
9. **New part `cap_01`;** `lever_01` is now a three-position **slide** that also
   lids the chimney row. Rules text for the reset lever ("lifts all five driver
   slugs clear") is still exactly true.
10. **`slug_01` is now a flanged T**, not a plain ⌀6 × 8 cylinder. The flange
    resting on the shell shoulder is the correctness datum — a plain cylinder
    cannot distinguish "correct" from "too low" at all. Rules text for slugs
    ("what falls across the shear line and jams the plug when a slider is set too
    low") is still exactly true.

**Sim / table findings vs the bill:** no contradiction. `sim_gate.json` passes on
all counts and touches no physical behaviour. Tables 1–3 ended at 1 move — an
engine-loop fault, not a mechanism fault — and table 0 ran 149 moves with no
physical complaint. All four seats at table 3 independently named the lock as the
irreplaceable object, which is the wound this brief prints.

**Forwarded, not resolved here (rules text, not geometry):** the fresh reader's
"opening Probe" ambiguity in §5 Re-pin, the missing tiebreak for first Locksmith
in setup step 4, and the worked example's prose undercounting paid turns by one.
None of these change a dimension.
