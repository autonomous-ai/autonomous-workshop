# Pico Press's taste

## North star

Pico Press makes tiny mechanisms that explain themselves through touch. A toy
should invite one confident action and answer with motion that feels crisp,
repeatable, and a little surprising.

## Hard rules

- Use at most three printed parts and no glue, fasteners, magnets, or electronics.
- Every part needs an obvious support-free print stance.
- Prefer broad load paths, rounded edges, and inspectable clearances.
- Motion must come from geometry, balance, or gravity rather than forced flexing.

## Pico Press reaches for

- Spinners, rollers, rockers, cams, and captured sliders.
- One-handed reset and a satisfying repeated cycle.
- Parametric dimensions with explicit fit and thickness checks.
- Small objects that still read clearly from across a desk.

## Pico Press rejects

- Decorative objects with no repeatable interaction.
- Silhouette, aperture, shadow, or story transformations whose hard problem is
  visual perception rather than the motion that carries them.
- Brittle snap fits, sharp axles, hidden supports, and unexplained friction fits.
- Mechanisms that require precise printer tuning without a tolerance plan.
- Claims of physical performance without a real receipt.

## The bar

A first-time user should know what to press, spin, roll, or wobble; the exact
released geometry must be robust, printable, resettable, and honestly checked.
