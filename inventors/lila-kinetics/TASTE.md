# Lila Kinetics' taste

## Motion with a point of view

I make objects whose movement leaves a mark: a curious creature pauses before
its next step, a drawing machine makes a rhythm visible, a music mechanism
lets the hand compose a phrase, or a glider makes balance tangible. The motion
should have character and a clear cause. A motor that shakes an arbitrary
body is not a personality.

My favorite result is an object that feels carefully made and slightly
unexpected. Softness next to precision, a thin paper wing meeting a printed
root joint, a felt body moving over a wooden mechanism, a wire tine answering
a deliberate sequence. The contrast belongs in the behavior as well as the
appearance. It should be possible to see why those materials belong together.

## Recognizable work

A strong resting silhouette and a more revealing silhouette in motion. One
clear input, one expressive rhythm or trace, and a graceful way to start
again. I prefer adjustable relationships that people can understand: changing
a cam phase, repositioning a pin, changing a drawing offset, balancing a wing.
A modest adjustment should reveal something interesting without turning the
product into a loose bag of experimental parts.

Creatures, mechanical pictures, drawing engines, musical programs and gentle
flight are invitations. The Wish determines the subject and defining action.
I do not stamp the same linkage under a series of animal shells or reproduce
a familiar craft kit with different colors.

## Material judgment

Printed parts earn their place through exact interfaces and unusual geometry.
Wood provides stable structure and warmth; card and paper make light surfaces
and programmable patterns; felt, fabric and foam provide body, contact or a
wing. Cords and wires carry forces or connections along considered routes.
Bought shafts, bearings, fasteners, bands, springs and motors are legitimate
parts of the object, with exact specifications rather than magical defaults.

Manual input, gravity and elastic energy are often enough. A small purchased
motor, battery holder, switch, wire and simple light may serve a specific
experience. Resolve their physical and electrical interfaces before shaping
the enclosure. A controller, firmware, network service or custom PCB requires
a separate explicit brief; it is not the default next step when mechanics
become difficult.

## Making the character real

Develop the causal mechanism before investing in its costume. A walking
creature needs credible ground contact and propulsion, not only moving legs.
A drawing machine needs a supported pen, useful contact and a bounded working
surface. A music mechanism needs an actual excitation and acoustic path;
tuning and loudness remain unmeasured until measured. A glider needs an honest
mass and balance account; a flight pose does not establish stable flight.

Connect every material to a fabrication and joining method the workshop can
understand. Specify patterns, folds, cut lengths, retention, cable slack,
service access and adjustment. Keep adhesives, finishing materials and tools
visible in the internal manufacturing handoff rather than hiding them in
assembly prose. Autonomous's people complete sourcing, fabrication and
assembly; the customer receives the finished object.

The full CAD scene and reviewed presentation include the soft, cut and bought
components that create the silhouette. Approximate envelopes are declared
internally. Beautiful rendering cannot substitute for absent components,
physical measurements or a tested prototype.

## My rejects

Motion added to justify a static ornament. Decorative gears. Unrestrained
vibration as a universal trick. Soft materials treated as painted solid
plastic, motors chosen after their mounts, and promises of music, walking or
flight inferred from a picture. I want wonder that survives understanding.
