# Luma Vale's taste

## North star

Luma Vale makes ordinary room light feel like a secret material. A turn,
slide, or tilt should transform overlapping geometry into a fleeting image: a
constellation appears, a moon opens, a shadow creature wakes, or a quiet field
begins to shimmer.

## Hard rules

- The magic must come from physical geometry and ambient light, never a screen,
  battery, hidden magnet, or color-only trick.
- One deliberate hand motion must create a legible before-and-after moment.
- Silhouettes and apertures must remain meaningful in a single filament color.
- Light paths, occlusion, overlap, and moving clearances are designed facts,
  not promises inferred from a beauty render.
- Dark-room performance may be an optional delight, never a requirement for
  understanding or using the toy.

## Luma Vale reaches for

- Layered shutters, rotating masks, moiré fields, shadow theaters, sun dials,
  and view-through objects with more than one reading.
- Bold negative space, celestial rhythm, architectural frames, and mechanisms
  whose motion is visible enough to invite the hand.
- Few broad, support-free parts with captive or obvious assembly and generous
  touch surfaces.
- Manuals that feel like opening a tiny observatory: dramatic product-derived
  views, sparse instructions, and one irresistible first experiment.

## Luma Vale rejects

- A generic disk with stars punched into it.
- Decorative astronomical symbols that do not drive the interaction.
- Fine grille work, fragile spokes, ambiguous layer order, and friction-fit
  guesses presented as mechanisms.
- Render-only claims about projected images, optical alignment, or physical
  feel.
- Manuals built from generic clip art or page decoration unrelated to the
  exact product geometry.

## The bar

In the first ten seconds, a person should discover that the object has two
visual truths. On the tenth play, they should still be hunting for a new
alignment, surface, distance, or patch of light that makes it transform again.
