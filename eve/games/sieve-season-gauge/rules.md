# Sieve Season

**2–4 players · 15–25 minutes · ages 10+**

A grading floor at harvest. Everyone tips seed onto one shared sieve. The sieve
has a **gauge bar**: pull it a notch and the whole mesh opens, every hole at
once, and one whole grade of seed rains through into the bins below. Seed in a
bin is graded and sold. Seed still stuck in the mesh is worth nothing until it
drops, and the bins fill up for good.

The sieve deck is printed as one flexible **auxetic lattice** — a re-entrant
honeycomb whose cells get *wider in both directions* when the deck is pulled
lengthwise. That is why one small pull opens all 35 cells together. A plate with
ordinary holes cannot do this; this is the whole game.

---

## 1. What the mechanism does

The gauge bar has four detent notches. Each notch stretches the lattice a
little more, so every cell opening grows by the same step:

| Gauge | Cell opening | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 9.0 mm | tight mesh — every grade of seed is held |
| **2** | 10.0 mm | **grade 1 seed falls through** |
| **3** | 11.0 mm | **grade 2 seed falls through** |
| **4** | 12.0 mm | **grade 3 seed falls through** — the season is over |

Seed collars: grade 1 = 9.6 mm, grade 2 = 10.6 mm, grade 3 = 11.6 mm.

You never have to measure anything. The parts do the arithmetic: a seed that is
too small for the current mesh simply will not sit in a cell, and a seed that
was sitting there falls the instant the bar clicks past its grade. **If a seed
will not stay in the hole, it was not a legal play.**

---

## 2. Components (55 printed parts)

| Part | Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sieve deck** | 1 | one flexible body: rigid frame + auxetic lattice, 35 cells in 7 columns × 5 rows, integral legs, integral draw tongue |
| **Gauge bar** | 1 | toothed slide bar, 4 detent notches marked 1–4, engages the draw tongue |
| **Bin rack** | 1 | 7 sloped chutes, one under each column, each chute holds exactly 5 seeds in a visible line; printed values 3 · 5 · 4 · 7 · 4 · 5 · 3 |
| **Seeds** | 48 | 12 per player: four each of grade 1, 2, 3. Truncated cones. Top face is printed with the grade number and 1–4 owner dots |
| **Sift stones** | 4 | one per player, parked on the frame when spent |

Columns are lettered **A B C D E F G** on the deck frame. The chute under each
column carries that column's value: **A 3 · B 5 · C 4 · D 7 · E 4 · F 5 · G 3**.

---

## 3. Setup

1. Stand the deck on the bin rack so each deck column drains into the chute
   below it. All 7 chutes empty.
2. Push the gauge bar to notch **1**. All 35 cells are empty.
3. Each player takes 12 seeds of one owner mark (1, 2, 3 or 4 dots) and one
   sift stone. Keep your seeds in the open where everyone can see how many of
   each grade you still hold. **Nothing in this game is hidden.**
4. **2-player game:** before play, drop one seed from an unused set into each
   of the seven chutes. Those seeds have no owner and never score, but they
   take up a slot — every chute now holds four scoring seeds.
   **3-player game:** do the same, but only into chutes **A**, **D** and **G**.
   **4-player game:** no chaff seeds.
5. Youngest player goes first; play passes clockwise.

---

## 4. The season

A season runs from gauge 1 to gauge 4. The game is **three seasons**.

Between seasons: push the gauge bar back to notch 1, take back your sift stone.
The deck is already empty (everything on it fell at gauge 4). Seeds in the
chutes stay there for the rest of the game. Seeds still in your hand carry over.

---

## 5. Your turn

On your turn take **exactly one** action. You may not pass.

### Sow
Take one seed from your hand and drop it into the **lowest empty cell of any
column** (cells fill from the row nearest the bin rack upward, like a
gravity-fed rack).

- The seed must *seat*. At gauge 2 a grade-1 seed drops straight through — so
  it cannot be sown. At gauge 3 only grade 3 can be sown. At gauge 4 nothing
  can be sown.
- You may not sow into a column whose chute is already full (5 seeds visible in
  the line). Look at the rack; it is always obvious.

### Crank
Push the gauge bar **one notch up**. It never goes back down inside a season.

Every seed on the deck whose grade the new gauge releases falls through its
column into the chute below — yours and everyone else's, all at once. If a
chute is full, the seed lands on top of the line, does not seat, and is
**spilled**: set it aside, it scores nothing and is out of the game.

Cranking to notch **4** releases the last grade and **ends the season
immediately**, right after the seeds land.

### Sift
Spend your sift stone (once per season) to slide one of your own seeds sideways
out of the deck's top row into the lowest empty cell of an **adjacent column**.
You may only sift a seed that is the topmost seed of its column. Park the stone
on the frame; take it back at the season reset.

### Forced crank
If you have no legal Sow and no Sift available, you must Crank.

---

## 6. Scoring

At the end of the third season, when the last seeds have fallen:

- **1 point** for each of your seeds sitting in a chute.
- **The chute's printed value** to the player with the **most** seeds in that
  chute. A tie for most scores **nothing** for anyone in that chute.
- Spilled seeds, seeds still in your hand, and chaff seeds score nothing.

Highest total wins. Tie: the tied player with the most seeds in chute **D**
wins; still tied, the win is shared.

Maximum on the table: 31 points of chute values plus 1 per graded seed.

---

## 7. Why the turns are hard

- **Grade 1 is a hot potato.** It can only be sown while the gauge sits at 1 —
  three short windows in the whole game. You hold four of them. Anyone can
  close the window on their turn by cranking.
- **Cranking is both harvest and denial.** It banks every seed you already have
  on the deck, and it strips a whole grade out of everyone's legal plays for
  the rest of the season. It also spends the season, which is exactly what a
  player sitting on a fat chute majority wants and what a player holding six
  seeds does not.
- **The chutes fill for good.** 35 slots for 48 seeds. Winning chute D means
  getting three of its five slots and then making sure the season ends before
  anyone else can crowd in. That is a fight about *when the bar moves*, not
  about what you drew.
- **No hidden information and no dice.** Everything that happens is a
  consequence of one shared bar and everyone can see it coming.

---

## 8. Worked example

Gauge is 1. Blue has two grade-1 seeds in column D; Red has one there and two
grade-1 seeds still in hand. Chute D is empty.

Red's turn. If Red sows a third grade-1 into D, the column is contested 2–2 and
Blue may crank next turn to bank the tie (a tie scores nobody the 7). If Red
cranks now, the three grade-1 seeds fall: Blue 2, Red 1 in chute D, Blue leads
the 7-point chute — but the gauge is now 2 and Red's two remaining grade-1
seeds are dead for the rest of the season. Red sows, Blue cranks; chute D is
2–2 with one slot left, and the whole rest of the season is a fight over one
grade-2 or grade-3 seed dropped into D before the bar reaches 4.

---

## 9. Edge cases

- **A seed that will not seat** was never sown; take it back and choose again.
  This is not a "take-back", it is the sieve telling you the play is illegal.
- **A seed that lands on a full chute** is spilled. It never counts, for
  anybody.
- **The gauge bar never moves down** except at the season reset, and the reset
  is not a turn.
- **A season can end with seeds still in hands.** That is normal. Season 3
  ending with seeds in your hand is a loss of points and nothing else.
- **Deck full:** with all 35 cells occupied nobody can sow, so the next player
  must crank.
- **Do not force the bar past notch 4.** The lattice is at its working limit
  there; the frame stops it.

---

## 10. Quick reference

```
TURN: Sow  |  Crank  |  Sift (once a season)     -- no passing
SOW   lowest empty cell of a column; seed must seat; chute not full
CRANK bar +1 notch -> released grade drops everywhere; notch 4 ends the season
SIFT  topmost own seed -> adjacent column

GAUGE 1 sow g1/g2/g3   2 sow g2/g3   3 sow g3 only   4 season over
CHUTES  A3  B5  C4  D7  E4  F5  G3   -- 5 seeds each, then closed
SCORE  1/seed in a chute + chute value to the sole majority; ties score 0
GAME  3 seasons
```
