# Neptune against Uranus at the product's own frame

Ranks 5 and 6, Ø21.89 and Ø22.02 mm -- 0.13 mm apart in a set whose
ranks are told apart by size. Measured on the canonical side-by-side
renders at the hero frame (azimuth -55, elevation 22), one per army.

## The size cue, which is no use here

| | Neptune | Uranus | apart |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| rank | 5 | 6 | 1 |
| globe diameter mm | 21.89 | 22.02 | 0.13 |
| piece height mm | 24.89 | 25.98 | 1.09 |
| obliquity deg | 28.32 | 97.77 | 69.45 |

0.13 mm is 0.6 per cent of Neptune's own diameter and less than one
third of the 0.40 mm nozzle. **Size cannot separate these two pieces**,
and it is the only pair in the ladder of which that is true: the next
closest, Venus and Earth, are 0.17 mm apart.

## The colour cue, which is the strong one

| | sealed hex | luma of 255 |
|---|---|---:|
| Neptune globe `blue` | #004EA8 | 67.9 |
| Uranus globe `cyan` | #00FFFF | 200.8 |

Sealed, the two separate **132.9** of 255 luma levels, which is four
times the 9.4 this project accepted as its narrowest on Saturn, and
they separate in hue as well: a dark navy blue against a full cyan.

## The silhouette cue, which this correction added

Until this run the two pieces had the same silhouette -- a ball on a
disc -- and everything separating them was paint. Uranus now carries a
ring, and it carries it standing up: its equatorial plane is 7.77
degrees past vertical, so the hoop is a band over the globe rather than
a brim beside it. Against Neptune's bare sphere that is a difference a
reader sees before any colour arrives, and it is the first cue in this
pair that survives being looked at from across a table in poor light.

| | Neptune | Uranus |
|---|---|---|
| silhouette | a bare sphere on a disc | a sphere with an upright hoop |
| piece height mm | 24.89 | 25.98 |
| widest, ring included | Ø21.89 | Ø24.02 |

## The surface cue, and what it says about Uranus

The two surfaces are not the same KIND of marking, which is the cue a
reader uses once they are close enough for paint. Neptune wears eight
short tapered wisps at eight different angles plus a dark oval; Uranus
wears a broad soft polar hood at each pole, boundary at latitude 55,
in the palest warm tone the shop stocks. **They are distinguishable at
table distance, and comfortably so** -- on silhouette now as well as on
surface, and on neither count is it the ladder doing it.

What the frame used to show, and no longer does. Uranus's single
`white` band was exactly what Neptune's three bands were before
Neptune's own correction: a closed belt of latitude, hard-edged,
exactly parallel, standing upright on the visible face, and next to
eight short wisps it read as the painted stripe on the pair. That was
recorded here rather than repaired at the time because Uranus was the
last world in the chain still to come. It has come: the band is gone,
and the hood that replaced it is a region rather than a line.
