# Independent blind visual review

Reviewed only iso.png, signature.png, playing/top.png, and counters/iso.png in the specified CAD snap directory. No Wish, stage packet, concept, source, or design text was read before recording these observations.

The held object reads as a square tabletop checker game with a removable architectural roof. The tray has an eight-by-eight field with pieces on alternating squares, twelve pale pieces facing twelve orange pieces. The roof is a very shallow cone on a square plate, with an inclined tube on a rectangular pedestal. That tube and roof combination suggests a stylized telescope building or observatory, although the cone could initially be read as a volcano or broad tent roof. The tube is much stronger astronomy evidence than the counters.

The apparent action is lifting the roof to reveal and play checkers. The roof, tray, and orderly internal pieces have a clear container-and-contents relationship. There are two tactile counter silhouettes: a circular body with one tapering projection, and a circular body with two parallel projections. Unprompted, these suggest a light bulb or comet versus a tuning fork or rabbit head; their precise subjects are ambiguous. One face of each counter has an inset curved symbol with a jagged extension; this appears to mark a changed game status, but the symbol is not self-explanatory.

The distinctive signature is the telescope-like roof hiding a full checker game. That is readable and potentially appealing as a desk object. The subdued blue, pale yellow, and orange give it a coherent appearance. The large roof provides a satisfying reveal in the exploded composition. The board itself is restrained and looks practical, though visually less distinctive than its cover.

Concrete visible weaknesses: the closed view has a substantial continuous gap above the tray rim with orange pieces visible, so it reads more like a loosely perched cover than a closed box. The roof's cone is extremely broad and shallow, making the observatory identity less immediate than a dome would be. The tube support is a plain rectangular block and its circular opening is only weakly legible. Alternating board cells have very low contrast; thin relief edges carry most of their identification. Counter subject identity is weak without explanation, especially the pale forked silhouette. The king/status marking is visually small and abstract, though visible in the close-up. No obviously misplaced pieces, gross unintended intersections, or broken solids are apparent in these four views. Printability, clearances, containment under movement, rules, and dimensions cannot be established from these images.

Overall blind assessment: coherent lidded checker set with a plausible observatory theme, an attractive reveal, and tactile team differentiation. Main presentation risks are the incomplete-looking cover closure and limited immediate recognition of the individual counter subjects. This is visual interpretation, not physical testing or compliance assessment.
