# Blind visual review — round 1

Inspected only `iso.png` and `signature.png` supplied for this review. No brief, source, metadata, or earlier review was consulted.

## Unaided reading

The object reads as a tabletop balancing or seesaw toy on a broad rounded rectangular grey base. A grey upright supports a long tilting beam carrying two thick, blocky animal figures. The left ochre figure reads most readily as a cat, with pointed ears and small muzzle dots; the right brown figure reads as a bear, with round ears and a dark oval nose. They are squat, deliberately stylized characters rather than naturalistic animals. Their bodies have substantial box-like depth in the isometric view, although their faces and front silhouettes dominate the design.

I do not clearly identify a separate object held in either character's hands. A narrow ochre horizontal bar runs between them behind their lower sides, through a tall central tab with an oval opening. This could be a shared handle or linkage, but hands and ownership of that bar are not explicit. A silver shaft and grey block behind the central tab suggest a mechanism, without making its operation visually self-explanatory.

## Action and relationship across the panels

The three front views show left animal high/right animal low, then a level state, then left animal low/right animal high. The composition clearly communicates a shared seesaw: both animals are carried by the same sloping beam about a central support. Their spacing remains consistent and their bodies tilt with the beam.

The faces appear to change with the seesaw pose. In the left-high view, the cat has a large open smiling mouth, while it has a closed straight mouth at level and right-high. The bear appears neutral at level and left-high; in the right-high panel it gains two pale downward-pointing shapes below its muzzle. My first reading of those shapes is exposed teeth/fangs or a surprised open-mouth detail, but the exact intended expression is ambiguous. The alternating facial response is the most distinctive visible interaction. The three stills imply that rising causes the corresponding expression to change, but cannot establish a mechanical cause or working motion.

## Desirability and visible issues

The warm two-color animal pairing and reciprocal poses are appealing and easy to recognize. The grey stand gives the object a stable display-toy presence, and the character depth makes it feel more substantial than a flat illustration.

The principal ambiguity is the bear's changed expression: the pale triangular marks do not resolve into a clearly bounded mouth, and can read as fangs, tears, or exposed internal pieces. The central upright tab and rear blocks are visually prominent and compete with the characters. Small light-blue slivers appear behind the bear's ears and near its sides; they read as exposed mechanism rather than intentional character features. The rectangular side projections and large flat faces make the animals somewhat boxy, with little separation of arms, body, and legs. In the isometric view, dark triangular gaps near the lower outer corners and broad internal seams are visible, although I cannot determine from these images whether they are intentional clearances or defects.

No obvious gross floating part or wholly disconnected assembly is visible. Still images do not verify mechanism operation, retention, fit, printability, strength, or physical manufacture.
