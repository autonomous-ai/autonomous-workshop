# Blind read — 22 renders

Everything below is from the images only.

## A. HELD OBJECT
A small square boxed board game — a shallow tray of playing squares holding two sets of coloured discs, with a domed lid that caps the whole thing.

## B. VOLUMETRIC FORM
A flat square tray, roughly 8 units wide by 8 deep and maybe 1 unit tall, with a raised wall around all four sides and a short post standing up at each of the four corners. The inside floor is the playing surface. On top sits a separate square plate that carries a large half-sphere dome; the dome is about two-thirds the width of the plate and about as tall as it is in radius, so the closed assembly reads as a low box wearing a helmet. The plate drops onto the four corner posts, so the closed object is: tray (bottom) / loose discs inside / plate + dome (top). It comes apart into exactly two big pieces plus the loose discs. Underneath there is nothing but the flat tray floor; the discs live in the void between the tray floor and the underside of the lid plate.

## C. SUBJECTS
1. Square tray with perimeter wall, four corner posts, and two small semicircular thumb notches at the mid-points of two opposite edges (playing/top_el90).
2. A grid of shallow rounded-square tiles set into the tray floor, on alternating cells, 8 x 8 overall.
3. A square flat lid plate.
4. A hemispherical dome standing on a low circular collar on that plate.
5. A vertical slot cut through the dome from base to apex, part of it bridged by a flat band.
6. A short stubby tube poking out of that slot, carried on a bracket with two thin vertical struts and a triangular gusset.
7. Orange-brown discs with an eight-rayed starburst mark — 12 of them.
8. Pale cream discs with a crescent mark — 12 of them.

## D. ACTION
Lift the dome-and-plate off the tray; the discs are inside. Set the discs out on the marked tiles — three rows each end, twelve a side, two empty rows in the middle (playing/top_el90). Play: move discs around the grid. When a disc earns something, stack a second disc of the same colour on it (kings/*). At the end, sweep the discs back into the tray and put the lid back on. The dome and its tube do not appear to do anything during play — they are the closed-state face of the object.

## E. RELATIONSHIP
The tray is the container and the board at the same time; the lid plate is the ceiling of the storage void and the pedestal of the dome. The four corner posts locate the plate and set its height, so the discs' thickness sets the gap. The discs sit on the tiles one-to-one, and a second disc stacks coaxially on a first. The dome is purely decorative cargo riding on the lid — nothing on the tray engages with it.

## F. THE LID / TOP PIECE
First word: **observatory** — a domed observatory building on a square base slab.

Yes, something sticks out: a short cylindrical tube, angled upward out of the slot in the dome, held on a small bracket. It is **hollow** at its end — image 8 (roof/telescope-close_az-90_el8.png) shows an unmistakable annulus: a pale ring wall around a dark circular bore. It protrudes past the dome's silhouette in image 6 (side-on) as a thin cantilevered stub.

## G. THE TWO MARKS
- Orange disc: a ring of eight short marks — alternating squares and diamonds — arranged around a plain central circle. It is a picture of the **sun**, a stylised sunburst.
- Cream disc: a single narrow crescent, sitting left of centre rather than centred. It is a picture of the **moon**, a crescent moon.

Both marks are **raised out of** the disc face, not cut into it. The discs read as: a raised outer rim ring, a recessed annular field inside it, and the symbol standing proud from that recessed floor, its top roughly level with the outer rim. Image 11 (counters/sun-oblique_az-90_el22.png) made me sure for the sun — each ray block has visible vertical side walls and throws a shadow down-right onto the floor beneath it. Image 13 (counters/moon-oblique_az-90_el22.png) made me sure for the moon — the crescent is a thick slab with lit top and shaded side walls, clearly standing above the pocket floor.

Worth flagging: the straight-down views contradict this. In image 10 (sun-plan) the rays read as *engraved*, and in images 12 and 14 (moon-plan, moon-symbol-plan) the crescent almost vanishes into a hairline outline. Because the symbol top and the rim top are coplanar, an overhead view gets no shading difference at all.

## H. BOTH SIDES
The two flat copies look **the same as each other** — identical mark, identical orientation, in both images 15/17 (sun) and 16/18 (moon). So either both faces carry the same mark, or the intended flip was never applied to the second copy; from the images alone I cannot separate those.

The copy on its edge tells me almost nothing about the two faces. In the oblique images (15, 16) its one visible face is marked and its outer edge is a plain cylindrical band, nothing more. In the plan images (17, 18) the edge-on copy projects as a bare rounded rectangle with a chamfer — it carries no information at all in that view. If the intent of that third copy was to prove the reverse face, it fails: the reverse face is never shown in any of the 22 images.

## I. THE DOUBLED PIECE
Two discs of the same colour stacked one on the other, on a single cell. I would assume immediately that a stacked pair is a **king** — a piece that has reached the far row, been crowned by putting a second captured piece of its own colour on top, and now moves both forwards and backwards. That is exactly how draughts/checkers is played, and the board (8 x 8, twelve discs a side on alternating cells, two empty middle rows) matches draughts precisely.

## J. SIZE AND LEGIBILITY
Image 19 (close, 45°): yes, easily — and both cues work there, colour and symbol.

Image 21 (iso, at anything like sitting distance): only **colour** separates them. Orange-brown vs pale cream is instant. The symbols are not doing the work — the sunburst is a smudge and the crescent is effectively gone, because both marks are flat-topped and level with the rim, so they only show up under raking light. A colour-blind player, or a version cut in one material, would have nothing to go on. This is the single biggest legibility weakness.

Secondary: in image 21 the discs are so close to the cell width that adjacent counters visually merge; a row of neighbouring singles reads as a stack from a low angle, which is exactly the cue that is supposed to mean "king".

## K. ANY DEFECTS
1. **The box does not close.** In images 1, 3 and 4 the lid plate rests on the four corner posts and stands clear of the tray wall, leaving an open slot right around the perimeter — you can see the discs straight through it in closed/az0_el8.png and closed/az-90_el10.png. Tip the closed object and the discs fall out.
2. **Unsupported lid plate.** The plate is thin, contacts only four corner posts, and carries a large dome at its centre — the middle of the span is loaded and unbraced (images 1, 3).
3. **Counters oversized for the cells.** In image 22 the disc diameter is essentially equal to the tile width; discs overhang their tiles and, on the edge columns, reach or cross the board's inner margin. Diagonal neighbours nearly touch.
4. **The checkerboard has no contrast.** Only alternating cells exist as tiles, all in one colour, distinguished by a shallow engraved outline (images 19, 22). At normal viewing distance the grid is faint; in image 20 the floor reads as random staggered brickwork rather than a board.
5. **Telescope tube joint.** In images 7 and 8 the tube and its two thin vertical struts appear to pass through / merge into the flat band bridging the slot, with no clean fillet or shoulder — the tube reads as embedded in the panel rather than mounted to it. In image 9 the tube and gusset intersect the dome's cut face at a hard, ambiguous edge.
6. **Fragile protrusion.** The tube is a thin, unsupported, upward-angled cantilever standing proud of the dome silhouette (image 6). It is the first thing that would snap, and as an overhang it is the hardest feature on the object to make.
7. **The dome slot is inconsistent between views.** Image 5 shows the slot as an open, empty, dark canyon; images 7 and 8 show most of that same slot filled by a flat panel. If those are the same geometry from different angles, the panel does not visibly connect to either dome half — it butts against them with a step and a visible gap line.
8. **Moon mark near-invisible from directly above** (images 12, 14, 18) — that is the very angle a seated player uses.
9. **Marks and relief styles are not a matched pair.** The sun is a fine, fussy ring of eight small blocks; the moon is one big thick slab. Different visual weight, different line thickness, and the crescent is off-centre on its disc while the sun is centred.
10. **Faceting.** The dome is visibly low-poly — quadrilateral facets across the whole surface in images 3, 6, 7, 9, and a stepped, polygonal outer contour on the discs in images 10 and 12. Whether that is the render or the model, it would print as visible banding.
11. **The edge-on demo disc in images 17 and 18 is a wasted view** — from plan it is an unreadable rectangle.

## L. IS IT DESIRABLE
Partly. The core idea is genuinely good — a draughts set where the box lid is a little domed observatory, and the board is the inside of its own storage tray, is charming and I would pick it up. But as shown it is not something I would buy: the lid does not actually shut, the counters are too big for the squares, the board pattern is nearly invisible, and the sun and moon marks — the whole point of the theme — disappear at the only angle anyone will ever look from.
