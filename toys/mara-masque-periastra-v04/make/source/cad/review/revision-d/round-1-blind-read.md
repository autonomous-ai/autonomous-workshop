# Round 1 — Blind Read

Written from the 41 PNGs only. I have not opened any other file and I have not looked for a brief, a spec or source. I have used the folder names (`roof/`, `counters/`, `kings/`, `board/`) only to know which picture is which — where a folder name implies a function (e.g. "kings", "foot"), I flag it rather than assume it.

---

## 1. HELD OBJECT

A small lidded game box: a square grey tray with a chequered 8×8 field and 24 loose discs in it, closed by a flat teal square plate that carries a big teal half-sphere on top, and the half-sphere has a vertical slot cut in it with a thin tube poking out through the slot. In the hand it would be a chunky paperweight-sized thing — you lift the domed plate off like a cloche and the game is underneath.

## 2. VOLUMETRIC FORM

Four masses, stacked bottom to top.

1. **Tray.** A flat grey slab with a raised rail on all four sides, about one counter-height deep (`board/empty-oblique_az-45_el35`). Four square posts stand proud at the corners, rising maybe 1.5× the rail height above it. Inner floor is pale blue-grey with 32 slate-blue squares laid into it as shallow inlays, each with a visible seam and a small chamfer around it (`board/inlay-seat_az-60_el22`).
2. **Lid plate.** A flat teal square, thinner than the rail, notched at its four corners to drop over the corner posts (`iso.png`). It sits *above* the tray floor with a clear open gap all the way round — the counters are plainly visible in that gap in `closed/az0_el8` and `closed/az-90_el10`.
3. **Drum + dome.** On top of the plate: a short cylindrical collar (two stacked steps, the lower slightly wider, separated by a dark parting line) and on that a hemisphere. Diameter of the dome is roughly 70% of the board width; its height is about half the board width. It dominates everything — closed, you mostly see a big blue-green ball on a tray.
4. **Tube.** A thin hollow cylinder inside a vertical slot cut through the dome, running from low inside the dome up and out through the dome skin at roughly 45°, so a short stub projects beyond the outer surface.

Proportionally the thing is top-heavy: the tray is a thin biscuit and the dome is a boulder.

## 3. SUBJECTS

- **Counters, 24 of them,** in two colours: 12 terracotta/orange and 12 pale cream/ivory (`playing/top_el90`, `board/populated-plan_el90`). Thick low cylinders with a raised outer rim and a sunken field.
- **Orange counter mark:** a central circular boss ringed by eight separate blocks — four squarish "brackets" on the cardinal axes and four diamonds on the diagonals (`counters/sun-plan_el90`). It reads as a stylised sun/starburst, though the ray shapes are lumpy brackets rather than rays.
- **Cream counter mark:** a single crescent, opening to the right, drawn as a very thin ribbon and sitting left of the disc centre (`counters/moon-plan_el90`, `counters/moon-symbol-plan.png`). A moon.
- **The dome with the slot and tube:** the slot plus the tube unmistakably reads as an observatory with a telescope, though the "telescope" is a featureless pipe.
- **Board squares:** plain slate-blue rounded squares, no letters, numbers or border marks.
- **Two stray arcs** scratched into the board field at the top and bottom edges, same x, visible in `board/empty-plan_el90`, `board/populated-plan_el90` and `playing/top_el90`. I cannot tell what they are meant to be.

## 4. ACTION (across `signature.png`, left → right)

- **Left frame:** shut. The domed plate is seated on the tray. A thin horizontal band of orange and cream counter-edges is visible under the lid plate, in the gap between plate and board.
- **Middle frame:** the domed plate has been lifted straight up and hovers above the tray. Underneath, the board is revealed with all 24 counters in their starting rows — 12 orange near, 12 cream far.
- **Right frame:** the lid is gone entirely. Only the tray, with the same 24 counters in the same starting layout.

So the action is a lid being taken off, in three steps. Nothing else happens: the board layout is identical in frames 2 and 3, no counter moves, nothing opens, the dome does not rotate and the slot does not change. It is a single event — undress the box — not a story.

## 5. RELATIONSHIP

- The **corner posts** register the lid plate; the plate's corner notches drop over them, so the lid can only sit one way and cannot slide (`iso.png`).
- The **plate** carries the drum; the **drum** carries the dome; the **dome** is bored through by the **slot**; the **tube** lives in the slot. Lift the plate and the whole observatory goes with it as one piece — dome, drum, slot and tube are all part of the lid.
- The **counters** live in the tray, under the plate. They are the reason the plate stands off the tray floor. The stand-off is what creates the open perimeter gap.
- Causally the only real chain is: lid up → board available. The dome is pure superstructure; it does nothing to the board, holds nothing, latches nothing.
- The tube is supported by nothing I can see (see 7b). Nothing causes the tube to stay where it is.
- The two stacked discs in `kings/board_az-90_el45` and `kings/low-oblique_az-110_el10` show that counters stack on each other — one disc of each colour is doubled up while its neighbour is single. So "double the disc" is the state change the pieces support.

## 6. ANTI-GENERIC SIGNATURE

What's specific:

- The **sun/moon pairing** as the two sides, in place of plain black/white, and carried as relief on the discs rather than paint.
- The **cloche form**: the lid is not a box lid, it's an observatory dome with a real slot cut through it, and the slot goes all the way from the crown down the flank.
- The **corner-post registration** — four little L-posts that make the lid self-locating.
- The **inlaid square field** (recessed tiles with visible seams) rather than a printed or painted chequer.

What is not specific, and undercuts the above:

- The telescope is a plain tube with a flat cut end. No eyepiece, no focuser, no bands, no dew shield, no mount. It is the one part that *had* to be characterful and it is stock pipe.
- The dome is a bare hemisphere: no ribs, no panel lines, no shutter doors, no finial, no base detail.
- The board has no edge marks, no grain, no signature, no orientation cue.

## 7. THE FOUR DIRECT QUESTIONS

### a. What does the thing sticking out of the dome look like?
**Frames: `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0`, `roof/side-elevation-close_az0_el0`.**

First three words: **snapped-off pipe**, **chimney**, **straw-end**.

At camera level it is a small blunt parallelogram sticking out of the dome's upper-left flank at about 10 o'clock, with a flat oblique end face and no features at all — in the close-up it is unmistakably a cut length of tube, dark on the underside, meeting the dome skin in a hard un-filleted line with no collar, no flange, no shroud. It reads as damage or as an exhaust stub, not as an instrument.

### b. What is holding the tube up?
**Frames: `roof/into-slit_az-75_el24`, `roof/into-slit_az-105_el24`, `roof/foot-in-slit_az-75_el24`, `roof/down-slit_az-90_el14`.**

**Nothing.** There is no mount of any kind visible. What I actually see: the tube runs down into the dome, narrows continuously into a **cone/needle**, and its point disappears into a **grey speckled, dithered smear** on the dome's inner shell (clearest in `roof/foot-in-slit_az-75_el24` at the centre of the frame, and in `roof/telescope-close_az-90_el8` at the bottom). That speckle is coincident-surface noise — two surfaces occupying the same place. So the tube is a spike driven into the inner wall of the dome and fused there.

There is no fork, no yoke, no trunnion, no pier, no pedestal, no tripod, no bracket to the slot walls, no ring, no counterweight. The tube does not touch the slot's side walls either — there's clear air on both sides of it in `roof/down-slit_az-90_el14`. The folder calls one view "foot"; in `roof/foot_az-60_el18` and `roof/foot-close_az-60_el18` there is no foot in frame at all — only the slot walls, a flat shelf at the slot's bottom, and the base ring.

### c. Is there a gap, notch or break in the base ring?
**Frames: `roof/drum-ring_az0_el6`, `az90_el6`, `az180_el6`, `az-90_el6`.**

**No true gap or notch.** In all four directions the ring reads as one continuous two-step collar running the full width of the silhouette, with a fine dark parting line between the upper and lower step, unbroken from left edge to right edge.

Two qualifications, both from the pixels:
- In `drum-ring_az-90_el6` (the slot-facing view) the slot's two flat cut faces come all the way down and **land on top of the ring**, so the ring's upper edge line is hidden behind them for roughly 15% of the width, between about x=775 and x=1030. The ring body itself still runs through, unbroken.
- In `drum-ring_az90_el6` there is a faint short lighter mark at front-centre of the ring (around x=900, y=1218), about 50 px long. It reads as a shading seam rather than a physical notch, but it is the only interruption anywhere on the band.

So: continuous, but on the slot side the slot's walls terminate on it rather than resolving into it.

### d. Does anything in the slot look raw, sawn off, unfinished, or like stock material?
**Frames: `roof/foot-close_az-60_el18`, `roof/three-quarter_az-135_el28`, `roof/into-slit_az-75_el24`, `roof/telescope-close_az-90_el8`, `roof/slit-plan_el90`.**

**Yes — four separate things.**

1. **The slot itself is a straight rectangular boolean cut.** Its two side walls are dead-flat planes and its ends are square. In `roof/slit-plan_el90` the slot reads from above as a plain rectangle laid over the sphere. No shutter, no doors, no runners, no radiused ends.
2. **The bottom of the slot is a raw flat shelf.** In `roof/foot-close_az-60_el18` there is a pale flat quadrilateral ledge at the slot's lower end (roughly x 350–670, y 620–790), and the same ledge shows in `roof/three-quarter_az-135_el28` and `roof/into-slit_az-75_el24`. The cut simply stops in mid-flank and leaves a horizontal table of material. That is exactly what a saw cut that ran out of travel looks like.
3. **The tube's mouth is a flat cut.** `roof/telescope-close_az-90_el8` shows a plain annulus — constant-diameter thin-wall pipe, cut square, no bevel, no lip, no lens seat.
4. **The tube's base is unresolved geometry** — the dithered speckled cone described in (b). That isn't even a raw detail, it's a modelling error made visible.

## 8. DESIRABILITY

Partly. The silhouette is genuinely good — the cloche-over-board idea is charming and the moment in `signature.png` where the dome lifts and the whole game is sitting there is a nice reveal. The inlaid board and the sun/moon relief would feel good in the hand.

What puts me off:

- The telescope. It's the hero element and it is a length of grey pipe with a flat cut end, stuck into the dome wall with no mount. Every close-up makes it worse. As a customer I'd read it as "unfinished" or "broken in the box".
- The closed state leaks. There's a visible open slot all round between the lid plate and the board, with the counters showing through it (`closed/az0_el8`, `closed/az-90_el10`). Pick it up and tip it and pieces come out. Dust goes in.
- The moon glyph is so thin it barely exists. Next to the bold eight-block sun, the cream side looks blank.
- The dome is too big. It buries the board it's meant to serve.
- The dome surface is visibly faceted — you can count the polygon bands in `drum-ring_az90_el6` and the silhouette in `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0` is polygonal, not circular.

## 9. DEFECTS

Concrete, per frame.

1. **Telescope has no support and its lower end is corrupt geometry.** The tube tapers to a needle and terminates in a grey dithered speckle on the dome's inner shell — coincident surfaces. `roof/foot-in-slit_az-75_el24` (centre of frame), `roof/telescope-close_az-90_el8` (bottom centre), `roof/down-slit_az-90_el14`, `roof/slit-plan_el90` (the smudged dark oval at the top of the slot rectangle). Worst defect in the set.
2. **Tube/dome intersection is unresolved.** The stub exits the dome skin as a hard, un-filleted, un-collared cut. `roof/side-elevation-close_az0_el0`.
3. **The slot bottom is a raw flat ledge** hanging in mid-flank above the base ring. `roof/foot-close_az-60_el18`, `roof/three-quarter_az-135_el28`.
4. **The slot is a box cut, not a shutter opening.** Flat planar walls, square ends. `roof/slit-plan_el90`, `roof/slit-plan-close_el90`, `roof/foot_az-60_el18`.
5. **Closed state does not close.** A continuous open gap runs round all four sides between lid plate and board floor, with counters visible in it. `closed/az0_el8` (band of orange/cream/blue slivers under the plate), `closed/az-90_el10` (an entire row of orange counters exposed), `iso.png`.
6. **Two unexplained arc marks scratched into the board field**, at the top and bottom edges on the same x, offset about one square's width right of the board centre — i.e. asymmetric. `board/empty-plan_el90` (≈x1070,y160 and ≈x1075,y1590), `board/populated-plan_el90`, `playing/top_el90`. A matching stray white tick appears at ≈(895,495) in `board/empty-oblique_az-45_el35` and at ≈(505,370) in `kings/low-oblique_az-110_el10`. These look like leftover boolean residue or mis-placed locating features.
7. **Moon glyph far too light, and off-centre.** The crescent is a hairline ribbon sitting left of the disc centre with square-cut horns; against the deep eight-block sun the two sides have wildly mismatched visual weight. `counters/moon-plan_el90`, `counters/moon-symbol-plan.png` vs `counters/sun-plan_el90`. In `counters/moon-oblique_az-90_el22` the crescent's right-hand tips run up against and merge into the raised inner ring.
8. **Counters are dished on one face.** The reverse is a deep circular pocket with the motif sitting at the bottom of it. `counters/sun-flip_az-90_el18` (third item), `counters/moon-flip_az-90_el18` (third item), `counters/moon-faces-oblique_az-90_el14`. In `kings/low-oblique_az-110_el10` the top disc of the left cream stack reads hollow-side-up, so a stack presents a cavity to the sky.
9. **Sun ray shapes are clumsy.** Four cardinal rays are square "L" brackets, four diagonals are diamonds — they don't read as one family of rays. `counters/sun-plan_el90`.
10. **Possible size mismatch between the two colours.** In `kings/board_az-90_el45` the cream disc measures wider on screen than the orange one directly below it (≈185 px vs ≈175 px at the same depth); the same tendency shows in `board/populated-plan_el90`. Two pieces of the same game reading as different diameters would be wrong.
11. **Coarse tessellation.** Visible polygon banding and a faceted silhouette on the dome. `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0`, `roof/drum-ring_az90_el6`, `roof/three-quarter_az-135_el28`.
12. **The lid plate is a bare slab.** Sharp square corners, no chamfer, no edge relief, no draft, and it overhangs the drum by a large flat margin with nothing on it. `roof/foot_az-60_el18`, `iso.png`.
13. **No orientation cue anywhere.** The board has no ranks/files, no edge marking, and nothing ties the slot's azimuth to the board's axes, so the dome can be set down facing any way with no "right" way. `iso.png`, `board/empty-plan_el90`.
