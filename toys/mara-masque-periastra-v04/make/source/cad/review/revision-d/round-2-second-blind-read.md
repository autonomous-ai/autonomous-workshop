## 1. FRESH OBSERVATIONS

Looking straight down the slot's own axis, this is what is at the bottom of it.

A **straight, constant-diameter tube** comes down the middle of the slot. It does not narrow. Near its lower end it **flares outward in a smooth cove**, like the base of a wine glass or a trumpet mouth, and that flare lands on a **flat floor** (`roof/foot-macro_az-90_el28`, `roof/foot-in-slit_az-90_el14`). The floor is a single evenly-lit plane that runs from the flare outward to both slot walls and continues some way below the tube before it stops. Its shading is flat and even across that whole area, its boundary against the slot walls is one clean straight line on each side, and there is **no doubled edge, no second silhouette, no overlapping outline** anywhere on it.

So what I see is: a **plate across the bottom of the slot, with the tube socketed into it through a filleted collar**. The tube is not floating and it is not stabbed into a curved wall. It sits in a hole in a floor, with a cove blend between the two. That is a joint.

In `roof/into-slit_az-90_el28` the same thing at full-object scale: the slot band runs from the crown down; the tube occupies the upper half of it; the flare is a small pale bell at around two-thirds height; and the slot band continues as flat pale material below the flare down to where it meets the base ring.

What is *not* good down there: the flare's own surface still renders as a **mottled, scaly fan** rather than a clean sweep — banded and grainy on its shaded left side in `roof/foot-macro_az-90_el28`, with a set of scalloped arcs just outside its perimeter that a surface of revolution should not produce. It is much better than before but it is not clean, and the noise sits exactly in the zone where the cove goes tangent to the floor plane. I read that as a **near-tangent blend shading badly**, not as two solids in the same place — but I want to be explicit that by eye I cannot fully separate "tangent" from "coincident", and whichever it is, it is still visible.

## 2. THE FOUR DIRECT QUESTIONS AGAIN

### a. Side elevation only — what does the thing sticking out look like?
**Frames: `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0`, `roof/side-elevation-close_az0_el0`.**

First three words: **stubby barrel**, **mortar**, **broken pipe**.

At camera level it is a short, blunt, thick-walled cylinder emerging from the upper-left flank of a smooth armoured-looking dome at roughly 45°, cut off square with a flat oblique face, no collar, no fillet, no taper, no muzzle detail — it projects about one-eighth of the dome's radius and then just ends.

**Is it a spout, a cannon, a mortar or a gun? YES.** Specifically it reads as a **mortar or short cannon barrel poking out of an armoured cupola**. That is the honest first read at this angle and the sharper re-render has made it *more* gun-like, not less, because the flat cut face and the hard edges are now crisp. Nothing in this view says "telescope" — there is no eyepiece end, no tube rings, no dew shield, no mount, and the dome has no ribs or shutter to place it as an observatory. The only competing read is "spout on a kettle", which is no better.

### b. Looking down the slot — what is holding the tube up?
**Frames: `roof/foot-macro_az-90_el28`, `roof/foot-in-slit_az-90_el14`, `roof/into-slit_az-90_el28`, `roof/down-slit_az-90_el14`, `roof/telescope-close_az-90_el8`.**

**A flat floor plate spanning the bottom of the slot.** The tube runs down to that plate and is joined to it by a **flared cove fillet** — a trumpet-shaped skirt that spreads from the tube's outside diameter onto the plate surface. The plate is flat, evenly shaded, continues past the tube on all sides and out to both slot walls, and extends below the tube's base before terminating near the drum.

That is what it is resting on and joined to. To say it plainly: **my round-1 answer of "nothing" was wrong.** I could not see the plate at 15 and 30 degrees off axis because the near slot wall covers it; on axis it is obvious.

What is still *absent*, and I want this on the record separately: there is no fork, no yoke, no trunnion, no altitude bearing, no pier, no counterweight and no visible way for the tube to move. It is fixed in a socket. So the mechanism is real but it is a **bonded pipe in a bulkhead**, not an instrument mount.

### c. Any gap, notch or break in the band at the base of the dome?

**No.** In all four directions the band reads as one continuous two-step collar running unbroken from one edge of the silhouette to the other. The sharper mesh has not exposed anything: the lower step's outline is a clean uninterrupted curve in every frame, and the fine dark parting line between the two steps runs right round.

### d. Anything in the slot that looks raw, sawn off, unfinished or like stock material?
**Frames: `roof/telescope-close_az-90_el8`, `roof/quarter-close_az-60_el18`, `roof/slit-plan_el90`, `roof/slit-plan-close_el90`, `roof/foot-macro_az-90_el28`.**

**Yes — two things, and both are about the tube, not the floor.**

1. **The tube is stock pipe.** `roof/quarter-close_az-60_el18` and `roof/slit-plan-close_el90` show it clearly now: a constant-diameter cylinder, cut dead square, with a **thick wall and a small bore** — the annular end face is roughly as wide as the hole through it. Nothing is turned, stepped, bevelled, banded or seated. It is a length of tube with a flat cut on the end, twice.
2. **The slot is still a straight rectangular cut.** `roof/slit-plan_el90` from directly overhead shows it as a plain rectangle laid across the sphere, square-ended, with dead-flat parallel side walls. No shutter, no doors, no runner, no radius at the ends.

**What no longer reads as raw:** the bottom of the slot. In round 1 I called it a saw run-out ledge. On axis it is plainly a **deliberate floor plate with a socket in it** — evenly shaded, boundaries clean, the tube properly blended into it. That was my misread. It is still an undetailed flat plate with square corners, but it is a designed face, not stock.

## 3. ROUND-1 FINDINGS — VERDICTS

- **F1 — tube tapers to a needle and ends in dithered coincident-surface speckle → WAS WRONG.** The taper was the V-shaped gap between the two slot walls converging at 15° off axis, not the tube; on axis the tube is constant-diameter and lands in a socket with a cove fillet. A lesser shading artefact does remain at the blend (see 5), but "spike into a wall with two solids overlapping" was a misreading of an occluded view.
- **F2 — tube/dome exit is a hard un-filleted, un-collared cut → STILL PRESENT.** The geometry did not move and the sharper render confirms it: `roof/side-elevation-close_az0_el0` shows a flat-cut oblique cylinder meeting the sphere along a hard unrelieved edge with no collar, flange or fillet.
- **F3 — the slot bottom is a raw flat ledge → WAS WRONG.** It is the floor of the slot with the tube socketed through it, single plane, evenly lit, clean boundaries (`roof/foot-in-slit_az-90_el14`, `roof/foot-macro_az-90_el28`). I read a partially-occluded sliver of it as a run-out.
- **F4 — the slot is a box cut, not a shutter opening → STILL PRESENT.** Flat planar side walls, square ends, no shutter, no doors, no track. `roof/slit-plan_el90`, `roof/quarter-close_az-60_el18`, `roof/three-quarter_az-135_el28`.
- **F11 — coarse tessellation / faceted dome → RESOLVED.** This is the one thing that actually changed and it shows: the silhouette in `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0` is now a clean curve and the diamond faceting has largely gone. Residual banding is still faintly visible on the shaded side in `roof/drum-ring_az90_el6` and `az180_el6`, but it no longer reads as a low-poly object.

## 4. WHAT IS STILL THE WEAKEST THING

The hero feature only works in extreme macro, and at every distance a human will actually look at this thing it reads as damage. The root is fine — I was wrong about that, there's a proper socket and a proper fillet down there — but nobody sees the root. What they see is `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0`, `roof/side-on_az0_el6`, `iso.png` and `signature.png`, and in all of those the object is a smooth featureless blue-green helmet with a **short blunt barrel bolted through its skull at 45°** and, on the opposite skyline, an unexplained square nick. The dome has no ribs, no panel lines, no shutter, no finial and no base moulding, so it carries no information that says "observatory"; the tube has no eyepiece, no rings, no focuser and no mount, so it carries no information that says "telescope". Two featureless primitives intersecting at an angle will always read as the crudest available interpretation, and here that is a gun in a pillbox. Making the mesh finer has sharpened that read rather than softened it. Fixing the exit — a collar, a shroud, a socket ring, anything that says the tube *belongs* to the dome — would do more for this object than anything else on the list.

## 5. NEW DEFECTS VISIBLE IN THE RE-RENDERED FRAMES

1. **The same tangency problem exists at the tube's *exit*, not just its root.** `roof/slit-plan_el90` viewed from directly overhead shows a **dark mottled, smeared lozenge** where the tube passes out through the dome skin (around x 555–665, y 455–590). This is a grazing intersection between the tube's outside and the outer sphere and it renders as noise at 0.01 mm just as the root blend does. I did not call this out in round 1.
2. **A dotted/stippled line along the bottom edge of the slot.** `roof/slit-plan_el90` (a broken dotted row at about y 1020–1030, x 520–680) and the corresponding bottom edge of `roof/slit-plan-close_el90`. A dotted rather than solid edge means two faces meeting at a near-zero dihedral — the floor plate going tangent to the dome's inner surface. It survived re-triangulation.
3. **A square nick in the dome's right-hand skyline in straight side elevation.** `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0` at around (770, 368) — a small rectangular step in the crown profile, which is the slot's far end breaking the silhouette. Combined with the stub on the left skyline, the hero elevation has two unexplained interruptions in an otherwise perfect curve, and this one reads as a chip.
4. **The tube's wall-to-bore ratio is now legible and it reads as plumbing.** `roof/quarter-close_az-60_el18` and `roof/slit-plan-close_el90`: the end face annulus is about as wide as the bore it surrounds. At the old tessellation this was mush; sharpened, it reads unmistakably as thick-wall pipe rather than an optical tube.
5. **The root flare is not rotationally clean.** `roof/foot-macro_az-90_el28`: the cove is banded and scaly on its shaded side, with scalloped arcs outside its perimeter that a surface of revolution should not produce. Much reduced from round 1, but present at 0.01 mm and therefore not purely a mesh-density issue.
6. **The mark on the band at `drum-ring_az90_el6` is real.** It survived the re-render, so it is not a triangulation artefact. Small, but it is now the only thing on that band and someone should look at it on the solid.
