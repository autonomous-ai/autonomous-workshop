# Round 6 — final judgements for the sealed record (PRIMED)

Primed round. The correction's requirements stand settled at 13/13 with an empty blocking list
(round 5). These nine are judgements about the object, not about the correction, and they are mine.
Eight are YES. One is NO, and I have said so plainly rather than let a disclosure launder it.

---

## 1. HELD OBJECT UNMISTAKABLE — **YES**

I named it in the first sentence of a blind read, before I knew anything: *"A two-hands-sized boxed
draughts set... Lid off, it is a checkers board; lid on, it is a paperweight-ish model of a domed
observatory."* I did not work for it and I did not hedge it.

The qualification, for the record: the draughts set is unmistakable from any frame, the observatory is
unmistakable only from a frame where the shutter shows. From `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0.png` alone I
wrote *"the telescope does not exist"*. Across the image set as delivered, it is unmistakable. From one
bad bearing, it is a cloche.

## 2. FORM MATCHES — **YES**

All three claims are in my blind read. Lift-off dome lid: *"a big teal hemisphere on a square flange
that drops over the whole tray like a cloche"* (`iso.png`). Telescope standing on the dome's floor:
*"The tube simply widens at the bottom into a smooth conical flare that dissolves into the flat floor
of the slot"* (`roof/foot-macro_az-90_el28.png`). Full-height shutter is the one I got wrong blind and
which round 5 settled by measurement and by `roof/slit-elevation_az-90_el0.png` — the channel breaks
the skyline at the crown with symmetric shoulders. Form matches the claim.

## 3. SUBJECTS MATCH — **YES**

Cold, I found the dome, the tube in its slot, the inlaid board — *"slate-blue squares sunk very
slightly below the pale field"* with *"the squares sit in shallow pockets"* (`board/inlay-seat_az-60_el22.png`)
— and both factions, which I described in section 6 as *"the two sides of the game identified as sun
and moon"* without being told.

**The gear-hub divergence does not break the subject match.** My own sentence says why: *"the sun
reading arrives only because there are eight of them in a ring."* It arrives. It arrives weakly and
late, through a mark that also reads as a bearing race, and the moon is the thinner offender — a
hairline crescent pushed left with *"right 40% of the face empty"*. The subjects are the right
subjects, poorly drawn. That is a quality complaint against a frozen part, not a mismatch.

## 4. ACTION MATCHES — **YES**

Blind, from `signature.png` alone: *"Frame 1: closed... Frame 2: the dome and flange have been lifted
as one piece and float above the tray; the full board is revealed set up for play. Frame 3: the dome is
gone entirely."* And: *"lid off, game out."* Exactly the claimed action, read without prompting.

## 5. RELATIONSHIP MATCHES — **YES**

One lid that leaves together: *"The tube is fused to the dome's inner floor. It is therefore part of the
lid, not of the base"*, and the three-frame lift shows dome, drum, shutter and tube departing as one
mass. Counters in the tray beneath: *"counters glint through the gap under the flange"*
(`closed/az0_el8.png`). Nothing but the tube in the slit: *"There is no fork, no yoke, no pier, no
trunnion, no pivot pin, no bearing block, no ring, no strap, no clamp, no base plate, no fork-arms
either side."* All three relationships as claimed.

## 6. ANTI-GENERIC SIGNATURE VISIBLE — **YES**, at the bar and not far above it

My section 6, written blind: *"a draughts set whose box lid is an observatory dome, with the two sides
of the game identified as sun and moon rather than black and white — that is a real idea, and it makes
the otherwise generic checkers pieces mean something (day side, night side)."* I reached for that
unprompted, which is the test.

It clears. It does not clear by much, and my own next sentence is why: *"Cover the slot and this is a
hemisphere on a box."* The specificity lives in the concept, the shutter and the sun/moon pairing — not
in the dome's surface, which carries no ribs, panels, seams or door frame and is not permitted to.

## 7. SIGNATURE EXPERIENCE UNMISTAKABLE — **YES**

`signature.png` delivers it in three frames with no caption needed, and my blind read reconstructed the
moment exactly: the dome comes off as one piece and a board set out ready to play is underneath. I had
no brief, no labels and no context, and I still wrote *"an unpacking, in three steps, of a single
lift-off lid."* That is the promised moment landing.

## 8. FINISHED PRODUCT DESIRABLE — **NO**

I am holding round 3. *"I like the idea and I would pick it up... I would not buy this one."*

I accept the faceting is a preview artefact and I have struck it. I accept the other four are disclosed
in the notes and the README. **Disclosure changes the contract, not the object.** A buyer who is told
in advance that the lid does not close still owns a box that does not close, and two of the four
disclosures are functional rather than cosmetic:

- The lid does not shut over the set — *"an open gap all round between flange and tray rail with
  counters plainly visible through it"* (`iso.png`, `closed/az0_el8.png`, `closed/az-90_el10.png`).
  The one thing the signature moment depends on is a lid closing over a game, and it rests on the rim
  instead of dropping in.
- The counters have no margin in their squares — *"discs overhang the blue squares onto the light field
  on every side; diagonally adjacent pieces nearly touch. No margin for fingers"*
  (`board/populated-plan_el90.png`, `playing/top_el90.png`). That is a playability problem in a thing
  whose purpose is to be played.

On top of which the product states it is not print-ready and that no physical print, fit, strength or
play test has happened. An object that has never been printed, fitted or played is a desirable
*design*; it is not yet a desirable *product*, and I will not sign that it is.

**Is this about this correction? No. It is entirely about the frozen set it inherited and the untested
status.** The roof correction is the best-resolved part of this object: it passed 13/13 with an empty
blocking list, it killed the cannon reading, and the one thing still weak at az0 is a photography
choice I have already told you is free to fix. If the frozen base, inlays and counters were reopened —
lid seating inside the rail, counters sized down inside their squares, the moon mark centred and
weighted — my answer flips to YES without anything on the roof changing.

## 9. LARGEST RISK

**Nothing has ever been printed, and the most fragile thing on it is the newest: a 15 mm free-standing
tube whose only attachment to the model is a cove fillet on the floor of a 30 mm slot, required to
print support-free at 45 degrees — so the first physical copy is where the real defects will be found,
and the telescope is the part most likely to arrive snapped, drooping or fused to the slot wall.**

# Addendum — question 8 re-asked in the field's own terms

Appended, not edited. My original NO above stands on the record as given.

**The field as actually worded: "the product looks finished and desirable" — a resolved object rather
than an unfinished one, with `print_ready_claim: false` and
`verification_status: digitally-verified-not-print-ready` sealed beside it rather than behind it.**

## Answer: NO

**Which claim I am making:** *this object is unresolved.* Not "I would not buy it". I am withdrawing
the purchase-intent framing entirely, and I am withdrawing the untested/never-printed leg of my
original NO — you are right that it is recorded elsewhere, by me and by the notes, and it has no
business being counted twice. Strip both of those out and a NO still remains, on its own feet.

**What remains, and why it is a resolution failure rather than a taste failure.** Two things I found
cold, as defects, in the delivered frames — not as preferences:

- *"Lid does not close."* `iso.png`, `closed/az0_el8.png`, `closed/az-90_el10.png`, `signature.png`
  frame 1: the flange rests on top of the tray rail with an open gap all the way round and the counters
  visible through it. And immediately after it: *"Flange and corner posts coincide. `iso.png`: the
  flange's corners meet the four grey posts with no clearance and appear to intersect them; nothing
  shows how the lid is located or retained."* An interface where nothing shows how the two halves
  register, in a two-part object whose entire idea is that one half lifts off the other, is the
  definition of an unresolved detail. It is also in the hero frames, not buried in a diagnostic view.
- *"Counters are as large as or larger than their squares."* `board/populated-plan_el90.png`,
  `playing/top_el90.png`: discs overhang onto the light field on every side. A grid and the pieces that
  sit in it not being dimensioned against each other is an unresolved proportion, visible from directly
  overhead, in the frame that shows the game set out.

Those two read as unfinished to a viewer who is given no notes. That is the test this field applies,
and they fail it.

**This is not an anti-unprinted position, and I want that on the record explicitly.** The field can
absolutely be answered YES for an object that has never been printed. If the lid seated inside the rail
with a visible register, and the counters sat inside their squares with a margin, I would answer YES on
these same images today, with `print_ready_claim: false` unchanged and no physical test performed. My
NO is about two specific geometric relationships, both measurable, both fixable, neither requiring a
printer. Round 5 is the proof that I score resolution rather than provenance: I passed the roof 13/13
with an empty blocking list on renders alone.

**Attribution, unchanged from above.** Both unresolved items are in parts this correction is forbidden
to touch and both are disclosed. The roof work is resolved and I have said so four times. If this field
is hard-gated and its NO finalises the *roof correction* as not-passing, then the record is attributing
an inherited fault to the one part of the object that was actually corrected — that is a flaw in the
structure of the record, and I will say so as plainly in public as I have here. **The fix is not to
re-ask me: it is to unfreeze two dimensions.** Seat the flange inside the rail, and size the counters
down inside their squares. That is a small, bounded change to frozen parts, and on the day it is made I
will flip this field to YES without a single thing on the roof changing.

# Addendum 2 — the two grounds measured

Appended. Nothing above is edited. Both the original NO and the narrowed NO stand on the record as
given, and this is the third and final statement of the field.

I made two specific, falsifiable claims and asked, in effect, for them to be checked. They were checked
on the built solids. Both are wrong, and — this is the part that decides it for me — both are wrong in a
way that explains what I actually saw, rather than merely contradicting it.

## Ground 1 — counters overhanging their squares: **WITHDRAWN**

Pitch 22.0, inlay tile 21.5 in a 22.0 pocket at 0.25/side, counter Ø20.0 — so 0.75 mm of dark tile
showing round every counter, enforced not by arithmetic but by a boolean in `measure/check_fit.py` that
asserts a Ø20.0 extrusion minus the built inlay leaves under 1e-6 mm³, with the chamfer line at
14.4957 mm against a 10.0 mm counter radius. A containment assertion that fails the build is stronger
evidence than my eye on a flat-shaded frame.

What I got wrong, and why it looked the way it did: in `board/populated-plan_el90.png` and
`playing/top_el90.png` the counters stand several millimetres above the board, so in a perspective
camera a raised Ø20 disc images larger relative to a 21.5 mm tile lying flat — the disc grows, the tile
does not. Add a flat-shaded renderer and an inlay top coplanar with the board floor, so the tile edge
carries almost no tonal step from directly overhead, and the ring of dark tile that is physically there
simply does not render. That is the same failure mode as B2: I judged a three-dimensional relationship
from the one projection that flattens the evidence needed to see it. My own oblique frames were the
check I did not run — in `board/populated-oblique_az-45_el35.png` the blue tile reads around the discs.

## Ground 2 — lid resting on the rim, nothing locating it: **WITHDRAWN**

Opening 184.0, plate 182.4, so the plate drops *inside* the opening on a 0.8 mm-per-side spigot and
lands on four integral 3.0 x 3.0 mm corner ledges at z21.0, probed on the built base. The 4.0 mm gap I
called a failure to close is the side walls deliberately cut to z17.0 between corner piers that stay at
z24.0 — a reveal, with the plate carried at the corners.

This one I should have got, and the cue was in a frame I described. My round-3 defect 10 read the
flange corners meeting the four piers as *"no clearance and appear to intersect them"* — that is the
0.8 mm spigot fit, sub-pixel at that render scale, i.e. I was looking directly at the locating feature
and calling it an error. And in `iso.png` the piers plainly stand proud of the plate while the side
walls sit below it, which is what "inset and carried at four corners" looks like; I read a plate below
pier tops as a plate perched on a rim. With the seat established, the reveal reads as a deliberate
sightline into the board rather than a lid that will not shut, and the absence of retention is correct
for a lift-off cloche — my own blind reading of the action was *"lid off, game out"*, which needs no
catch.

## Answer: **(a) YES**

`finished_product_desirable` — **YES.** Both grounds my NO rested on were misreadings of flat-shaded
projections, measured and explained, and with them withdrawn I have no remaining ground for
"unresolved": every other complaint I hold is either a disclosed frozen characteristic, a render
artefact I have already reclassified, or the aperture trade I recorded as an objection rather than a
failure. I am not taking option (c). It would be available to me only if I still had a substantive NO
that the record was mis-mapping onto the roof work, and I no longer do; refusing to sign on structure
alone, having lost the substance, would be stubbornness dressed as rigour.

Three things I attach to the YES, none of them conditions:

1. **I have not verified these numbers myself** — I accept them as I accepted the B2 volumes, on the
   strength of their specificity, their falsifiability and their agreement with what the pixels show
   once explained. If any of the three measurement sets I have now accepted is wrong, my YES is wrong
   with it, and I would rather that be on the record than implied.
2. **The reveal still needs a frame that sells it.** A cold viewer of `closed/az0_el8.png` sees a gap,
   because the piers that carry the plate are at the corners and that frame is shot square to a side.
   A three-quarter or corner-level frame showing the plate inset between the piers would make the seat
   legible. Same class of fix as my round-5 hero-frame recommendation, and free.
3. **The structural note stands as a recommendation, not an objection.** One boolean field spanning a
   frozen inherited set and the correction under review is a weak instrument — it nearly failed a roof
   I scored 13/13 over two things on the board that I had misread. Split the field, or scope it to the
   correction. Carry that forward as process feedback; it is no longer a reason to withhold anything.

My standing objection is unchanged and unrelated: the aperture dies on an unbroken rotation ring and I
think viewers read it as an aperture that cannot open. Recorded, deliberate, not scored, not a gate.
