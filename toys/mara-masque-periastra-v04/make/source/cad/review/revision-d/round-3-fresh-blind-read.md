# Round 3 — fresh blind read (pixels only)

Nothing was read except the 42 PNGs listed. Folder names are treated as filing labels, not as claims:
`roof/` contains a dome, `telescope-close_*.png` contains a plain hollow cylinder, `drum-ring_*` is a
collar at a dome base, `kings/` shows two-high stacks of counters. Where I disagree with what a folder
name implies, I say so.

---

## 1. HELD OBJECT

A two-hands-sized boxed draughts set: a shallow grey tray with a blue-and-white 8x8 chequer floor
holding 24 round counters, closed by a big teal hemisphere on a square flange that drops over the
whole tray like a cloche. Lid off, it is a checkers board; lid on, it is a paperweight-ish model of a
domed observatory.

## 2. VOLUMETRIC FORM

Four masses.

1. **Tray** (`board/empty-oblique_az-45_el35.png`): a flat light-grey slab with a low rail on all four
   sides and a square post standing proud at each of the four corners. Rail height is roughly one
   counter-thickness; the posts rise maybe double that.
2. **Chequer floor**: a pale blue-white plate inset inside the rail, carrying 32 slate-blue squares
   sunk very slightly below the pale field (`board/inlay-seat_az-60_el22.png` shows the squares sit in
   shallow pockets, with a thin bright land between them).
3. **Lid flange**: a thin square teal plate, noticeably thinner than the tray rail, spanning about the
   same footprint as the tray (`iso.png`).
4. **Dome**: a hemisphere sitting on that plate, standing on a stepped collar — a taller cylindrical
   skirt plus a thin proud rim below it, with a hard black seam line between dome and collar
   (`roof/side-elevation_az0_el0.png`, `roof/drum-ring_az0_el6.png`). Dome diameter is about 70% of
   the plate's width and its height is roughly four to five times the tray's total height. In the
   assembled object the dome dominates completely; the game underneath is a plinth.

The masses stack, they do not interlock. In `iso.png` and `signature.png` frame 1 the flange rests on
top of the tray rail and there is a continuous open gap all the way round beneath it, through which
you can see the counters lying inside.

## 3. SUBJECTS

- **24 counters**, 12 orange-terracotta and 12 pale cream (`board/populated-plan_el90.png`).
- **Orange counter face** (`counters/sun-plan_el90.png`): a plain centre circle ringed by **eight**
  small marks. The marks are not rays or triangles — they are little hollow corner-brackets, four of
  them square "⌐"-shaped and four of them diamond outlines, alternating. Read cold it is closer to a
  bearing race, a gear hub or a camera-aperture icon than a sun; the sun reading arrives only because
  there are eight of them in a ring.
- **Cream counter face** (`counters/moon-plan_el90.png`, `counters/moon-symbol-plan.png`): a single
  hairline crescent **outline** — an open "C" with blunt-cut tips, not a filled crescent. It sits well
  left of the disc centre, leaving the right 40% of the face blank. Much lighter in graphic weight
  than the sun, and the two symbols do not read as a matched pair.
- **Both counters are hollow-backed pans** (`counters/sun-flip_az-90_el18.png`,
  `counters/moon-flip_az-90_el18.png`): one face is flat with the symbol engraved, the other is a deep
  circular cavity with a thick wall, the symbol standing proud on the cavity floor. They look like
  bottle caps or moulded jar lids seen from underneath.
- **Kings** (`kings/board_az-90_el45.png`): two counters stacked. Two of the four pieces shown are
  doubled, two are single.
- **A tube in a slot** (`roof/telescope-close_az-90_el8.png`): a plain dark cylinder with a square-cut
  muzzle showing a clean annular wall, sitting in a straight-sided rectangular channel cut into the
  dome.
- **Two small semicircular nicks** in the chequer floor at the north and south mid-edges only
  (`board/empty-plan_el90.png` at top centre and bottom centre; same in
  `board/populated-plan_el90.png`, `playing/top_el90.png`, and again as a white sliver in
  `kings/low-oblique_az-110_el10.png`). Unexplained, unmirrored on east and west.

## 4. ACTION across `signature.png`, left to right

Frame 1: closed. The dome and its flange sit down over the tray; counters glint through the gap under
the flange.
Frame 2: the dome and flange have been lifted as one piece and float above the tray; the full board is
revealed set up for play.
Frame 3: the dome is gone entirely; only the loaded board remains.

So: **lid off, game out.** It is an unpacking, in three steps, of a single lift-off lid. Nothing
rotates, nothing hinges, nothing opens in place. The dome does not do anything except come off.

## 5. RELATIONSHIP

- Flange carries dome; flange lands on the tray rail; the four corner posts sit outboard of the flange
  and nominally locate it (`iso.png`). But the flange rests on the rail rather than dropping inside it,
  so the enclosure is not closed — there is a full-perimeter slot you can see the pieces through
  (`closed/az0_el8.png`, where cream and orange counter edges and a blue square are plainly visible in
  the gap; `closed/az-90_el10.png`, where a whole row of orange counters shows).
- The tube is fused to the dome's inner floor (`roof/foot-macro_az-90_el28.png`). It is therefore part
  of the lid, not of the base. Rotating the lid would swing the tube; the tube cannot move relative to
  the dome and the slot cannot move relative to either. There is no shutter, no bearing, no track.
- Causally: almost nothing causes anything. The only real causal chain is lift-flange → dome, tube and
  slot all leave together → board is exposed. The counters, the squares, the slot and the tube are
  four independent decorative facts sharing a footprint.

## 6. ANTI-GENERIC SIGNATURE

What is specific: a draughts set whose box lid is an observatory dome, with the two sides of the game
identified as **sun** and **moon** rather than black and white — that is a real idea, and it makes the
otherwise generic checkers pieces mean something (day side, night side). The stepped collar with its
thin proud rim, the sunken-square inlay with visible lands between squares, and the four corner posts
give the tray a little machined character.

What is generic: the dome itself is an untouched hemisphere with no ribs, panels, seams, bolt circles
or door frame; the chequer plate is the default checkerboard; the counter edges are plain cylinders.
Cover the slot and this is a hemisphere on a box. The slot and the tube are the only things claiming
"observatory", and both are executed so faintly that from the front the object is a cloche.

## 7. FOUR QUESTIONS

### a. What does the thing sticking out of the dome look like?

**Frames used:** `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0.png`, `roof/side-elevation-close_az0_el0.png`.

**First three words: snapped-off peg.** (Runners-up: broken tab, sawn stub.)

It is a tiny dark quadrilateral tab, about 4% of the dome's width, poking out of the upper-left slope
at roughly 45–55 degrees, with a flat blunt end and no visible bore, taper, ring, lens, ferrule or
mount; it reads as the stump of something that broke off, or as a casting tab someone forgot to file.
The close-up makes it worse, not better — at 10x it is a dark trapezoid welded to a bald sphere, and
its dark grey is a different value from everything else in the frame, so it reads as a shadow or a
chip rather than as a part. On the right of the same silhouette there is a matching nick in the dome
edge, which adds to the "this thing is damaged" reading.

**What I think it is for:** honestly, from these two frames alone, my first answer is a lifting nub or
a lug for a strap — something you catch a finger under. My second guess is a vent or chimney. Only
after seeing other frames do I learn it is the muzzle of a tube inside a slot. In the straight side
elevation — the view that will be photographed for a box, a listing or a shelf — the telescope does not
exist. That is the single biggest failure in this set of pictures.

### b. What is holding the tube up?

**Frames used:** `roof/into-slit_az-90_el28.png`, `roof/foot-in-slit_az-90_el14.png`,
`roof/foot-macro_az-90_el28.png`, `roof/down-slit_az-90_el14.png`, `roof/into-slit_az-75_el24.png`,
`roof/into-slit_az-105_el24.png`.

**Nothing that reads as a mechanism.** The tube simply widens at the bottom into a smooth conical
flare that dissolves into the flat floor of the slot, like candle wax run down onto a plate, or an
injection-moulding sprue, or a soldered fillet. There is no fork, no yoke, no pier, no trunnion, no
pivot pin, no bearing block, no ring, no strap, no clamp, no base plate, no fork-arms either side. The
flare is also the worst-rendered surface in the whole set: in `foot-macro_az-90_el28.png` and
`foot-in-slit_az-90_el14.png` it is visibly triangulated, with concentric banding and mottled shading
artefacts that look like two surfaces fighting for the same space. It does not look designed; it looks
like an automatic blend applied to hide a join.

**How much is inside versus outside:** essentially all of it is inside. In the straight-down-the-slot
views the whole tube — flare, shaft and muzzle — sits within the channel, below the dome's outer
profile. The only part outside is the stub in the side elevation, which is roughly a tenth to at most a
fifth of the tube's length. Call it **85–90% swallowed, 10–15% showing**, and the showing part is the
part that reads as a broken peg.

### c. Any gap, notch or break in the drum ring?

**Frames used:** all four of `roof/drum-ring_az0_el6.png`, `az90`, `az180`, `az-90`.

**No. The band is continuous through 360 degrees.** From all four directions it is an unbroken
two-step collar — a taller skirt with a thin proud rim below it — running right round with no notch,
no joint, no rebate, no keyway and no door. Specifically at az-90, where the slot faces the camera,
the slot's two parallel walls run down and simply die on the top edge of the collar; the collar's dark
upper line passes straight across in front of them without interruption.

I will state the consequence bluntly, because it is what the four frames prove: **the slot terminates
at a solid unbroken rim.** A real dome slit runs down to, and through, the base ring so the aperture
opens to the horizon; here the aperture stops in mid-air at a closed collar. The ring being perfect is
itself the defect.

### d. Does anything in the slot look raw, sawn or like stock material?

**Frames used:** `roof/telescope-close_az-90_el8.png`, `roof/slit-plan-close_el90.png`,
`roof/quarter-close_az-60_el18.png`, `roof/foot-macro_az-90_el28.png`, `roof/into-slit_az-90_el28.png`.

**Yes, most of it.**

- The **muzzle** is a dead-flat crosscut showing an even-thickness annulus. That is a length of tube
  cut off with a saw. No taper, no chamfer, no lip, no eyepiece, no ring, no optic, nothing occupying
  the bore.
- The **shaft** is a constant-diameter dark cylinder with no barrel sections, no focuser, no bands,
  no draw tube — bar stock.
- The **slot** is a straight-sided rectangular channel with square-cut ends and hard 90-degree
  internal corners and no radius, lip, rebate, runner or frame. The back of the channel is a flat
  rectangular panel (clearly visible as a lighter rectangle in `foot-in-slit_az-90_el14.png`), so the
  inside of the "dome" behind the aperture is a flat plate, not a curved shell.
- The **plan view** (`roof/slit-plan_el90.png`) confirms it: the aperture is a plain rectangle laid on
  a sphere, clipped where it runs off the edge — a milled slot, not a shutter opening.
- The **foot flare** is the Boolean-artefact blob described above.

The only thing in there that looks deliberately made is the bore of the muzzle, and that is a
by-product of using a tube.

## 8. DESIRABILITY

I like the idea and I would pick it up. A checkers set that lives under an observatory dome, with sun
counters against moon counters, is a genuinely good small gift object, and the tray, the inlaid
squares and the corner posts are pleasant.

I would not buy this one.

- On the shelf, closed, it is a featureless teal hemisphere with a broken-looking nub on one side. The
  "observatory" only appears if you stand at one particular bearing.
- The dome's surface is visibly faceted everywhere — quad-grid tessellation across the whole hemisphere
  in `iso.png`, `closed/az0_el8.png` and all four drum-ring views. It looks like an unsmoothed render
  or a coarse print, not a finished surface.
- Counters have hollow backs with thick rims; picked up, they will feel like bottle caps and will
  rattle in the tray.
- The lid does not sit down onto the box. You see the pieces through a slot all round it.
- The moon mark is a thin scratch of a crescent, off-centre, while the sun is a bold eight-mark ring.
  The pair does not balance in the hand or in a photo.

## 9. DEFECTS

Each with the frame it is visible in.

1. **The telescope is invisible in the hero view.** `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0.png` and
   `roof/side-elevation-close_az0_el0.png`: a ~4%-of-diameter dark stub, blunt-ended, reading as
   breakage. Also `roof/side-on_az0_el6.png`, `roof/drum-ring_az0_el6.png`,
   `roof/drum-ring_az180_el6.png`.
2. **Matching nick in the dome silhouette** opposite the stub, at the same height, upper right of
   `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0.png` — reads as a chip out of the dome.
3. **No telescope mount at all.** `roof/foot-macro_az-90_el28.png`, `roof/foot-in-slit_az-90_el14.png`:
   a smeared conical fillet where the tube meets the floor, with triangulation banding and mottled
   shading — an unresolved surface, not a detail.
4. **Aperture dies into an unbroken base collar.** `roof/drum-ring_az-90_el6.png` versus the other
   three drum-ring frames: the ring is continuous 360 degrees, so the slot cannot reach the horizon.
5. **Slot is a milled rectangle on a sphere, with a flat back wall.** `roof/slit-plan_el90.png`,
   `roof/foot-in-slit_az-90_el14.png`, `roof/quarter-close_az-60_el18.png`: square-cut ends, hard
   corners, flat rectangular panel behind the tube where a curved shell should be.
6. **Aperture is off-axis and stops short of the apex.** `roof/slit-plan_el90.png`: the rectangle sits
   right of the dome's centre in plan and does not reach the zenith, so the "slit" is neither
   meridional nor symmetric.
7. **Visible black seam between dome and collar.** `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0.png`,
   `roof/drum-ring_az0_el6.png`: a hard dark line all round, reading as a gap rather than a joint.
8. **Whole dome is visibly faceted.** `iso.png`, `closed/az0_el8.png`, all four `roof/drum-ring_*`:
   quad tessellation across every highlight.
9. **Lid does not close.** `iso.png`, `closed/az0_el8.png`, `closed/az-90_el10.png`,
   `signature.png` frame 1: an open gap all round between flange and tray rail with counters plainly
   visible through it.
10. **Flange and corner posts coincide.** `iso.png`: the flange's corners meet the four grey posts with
    no clearance and appear to intersect them; nothing shows how the lid is located or retained.
11. **Counters are as large as or larger than their squares.** `board/populated-plan_el90.png`,
    `playing/top_el90.png`: discs overhang the blue squares onto the light field on every side;
    diagonally adjacent pieces nearly touch. No margin for fingers.
12. **Two unexplained semicircular nicks in the chequer floor**, north and south mid-edge only, absent
    east and west. `board/empty-plan_el90.png`, `board/populated-plan_el90.png`, `playing/top_el90.png`,
    and as a stray white sliver in `kings/low-oblique_az-110_el10.png`.
13. **Counters are hollow-backed pans with thick rims.** `counters/sun-flip_az-90_el18.png`,
    `counters/moon-flip_az-90_el18.png`, `counters/sun-oblique_az-90_el22.png`,
    `counters/moon-oblique_az-90_el22.png`. The two faces of a single piece are completely different
    objects — one engraved and flat, one a deep cavity.
14. **Moon symbol is off-centre and under-weighted.** `counters/moon-plan_el90.png`,
    `counters/moon-symbol-plan.png`: a hairline crescent outline with blunt cut tips, pushed left,
    right 40% of the face empty — against the sun's bold centred eight-mark ring in
    `counters/sun-plan_el90.png`.
15. **Sun mark does not read as a sun.** `counters/sun-plan_el90.png`,
    `counters/sun-faces-plan_el90.png`: eight hollow corner-brackets and diamonds around a plain disc
    read as a bearing, a hub or an aperture icon.
16. **Kings are just two loose counters stacked.** `kings/board_az-90_el45.png`,
    `kings/low-oblique_az-110_el10.png`: no nesting, spigot or register between the discs; the stack is
    visibly free to slide, and at low angle the top disc of the cream stack reads as a washer with a
    hole through it rather than a moon counter.
