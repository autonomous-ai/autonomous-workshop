# Antisol Jove Mirror

Jungle Chess played with the solar system, in two armies of eight worlds.

A planet's size on the board is the fifth root of its real measured diameter,
so rank is something you can see rather than something you have to learn:
Mercury is the smallest world in play and Jupiter the largest. The traditional
rules are followed exactly as written. Matter faces antimatter -- one army's
worlds lean their poles one way and the other army's lean the other, by each
planet's own true axial tilt, and the bases say the same thing again in white
against black. On the three worlds whose tilt is too small to show a lean, the
mirror is carried by the map instead, so that every pair reads as a pair.

## This edition: Jupiter joins the mirrored three

**This is a defect, found by measurement, and fixed. It changes exactly one
printed part: the Anti-Sol Jupiter. Nothing else in the set changes, and in
particular the Sol Jupiter does not.**

Jupiter's two armies were the same object photographed twice. The previous
edition's independent reviewer was shown the eight pairs one at a time, cold,
and asked which were two different objects: it answered eight of twelve
comparisons as different, and named this one as the exception -- *"I cannot
tell these apart and I would not notice if you swapped them."*

The cause is the one Mercury and Venus had. Jupiter's axial tilt is **3.13
degrees**, so its two pieces differ by six and a quarter degrees of lean, which
is nothing you can see across a table. **The tilt is real and it does not
move** -- it is a measured fact about the planet and it is the whole ownership
cue of this set, so inventing a lean would be correcting the planet rather than
the piece. So the mirror is taken from the map, exactly as it is on Mercury and
Venus.

**And Jupiter is the hard one, because almost nothing on it has a longitude.**
Its six belts and five bright zones are bands of latitude, and a band of
latitude is the same at every longitude -- mirroring the map does not move it
at all. Only three things on this globe carry a longitude: the Great Red Spot,
the collar around it, and the phase of the wave that runs along the two widest
belts. So the whole mirror has to be carried by one oval three millimetres
long, and that oval sits almost exactly on the line the mirror is taken about,
where a reflection would have moved it **a third of a millimetre**.

The line was therefore moved, deliberately and by measurement, until the swing
was one a person can see: the spot now sits **6.2 mm away** from where it sits
on its twin, which is twice its own length on a ball 27 mm across. The full
sweep -- every position that keeps all three globes' markings facing the
camera, the one taken, and why -- is in the CAD project's
`measure/mirror-meridian.md`.

**What it costs.** The Anti-Sol Jupiter's Red Spot sits at a longitude that is
not the real one, and the wave along its two widest belts is handed the other
way. Both are real and both are recorded. The mitigation is most of the answer:
longitude on this globe was never a map fact. The spot's position was chosen to
put it in front of the camera, the same way Mercury's basin and Venus's whole
map were, and Jupiter turns in ten hours with no fixed meridian anywhere in
this set. Latitude, size, shape and count are untouched -- those are the four
things you could check against a photograph.

**Three of the eight worlds now carry a mirrored map and five carry one map at
two leans.** A world joins the mirrored three when the angle between its two
pieces' leans is too small to show the mirror: Mercury at 0.06 degrees, Venus
at 5.28 and Jupiter at 6.26. The five that keep one map are Earth at 46.88,
Mars at 50.38, Saturn at 53.46, Neptune at 56.64 and Uranus at 164.46 -- every
one of them far from degenerate. `measure/mirror-set-consistency.md` is that
table with each world's tilt beside it, so the rule can be checked rather than
trusted.

**The previous edition carried two corrections that have nothing to do with
each other.** They were separate briefs until the owner saw what a run costs;
they touch different parts and neither was traded against the other. Its
account follows, unchanged.

## One: Mercury and Venus were the same piece twice

**This is a defect, found by the owner, and fixed.** It is the only correction
in this pass that is not a matter of taste.

The two armies are supposed to be mirrors. On most worlds they visibly are,
because the globe leans one way on one army and the other way on the other by
that planet's own axial tilt -- at Mars's 25.19 degrees or Earth's 23.44 that
is obvious across a table. Mercury's axial tilt is **0.03 degrees** and
Venus's is **177.36**, which is very nearly a half turn; so Mercury's two
pieces differ by six hundredths of a degree of lean and Venus's by five and a
quarter. Nothing. Look at the two of them side by side in the old set and you
are looking at one piece photographed twice.

**The tilts are real and they do not move.** They are measured facts about the
planets and they are the whole ownership cue of this set, so inventing a lean
for Mercury would be correcting the planet rather than the piece.

**So the mirror is taken from the map.** On the Anti-Sol Mercury and the
Anti-Sol Venus, the same features sit at the same latitudes arranged the other
way round. Caloris stays square to the eye and the seven plains around it
change hands; Aphrodite Terra's long silhouette is handed the other way and
still faces you.

**What that costs, plainly: the mirrored map is a mirror-image map.** The
Anti-Sol Mercury's Caloris rim is handed the other way from the real basin's,
and so are the Anti-Sol Venus's provinces. What makes that a fair price rather
than a fudge is that on these two worlds the longitudes were never a fact about
the planet in the first place -- they were chosen by earlier editions so that
the features would face the camera. What is mirrored is a placement this
project made, not an observation. Latitudes, sizes, shapes and counts are
untouched, and so is every dimension of both pieces: both printed files come out
byte-for-byte identical to the last edition's, because the colour boundary moved
and no surface did.

**Jupiter has the same problem and is not fixed.** Its tilt is 3.13 degrees, so
its two pieces differ by 6.26, and the Great Red Spot sits in very nearly the
same place on both. The owner named Mercury and Venus and did not name Jupiter,
so it was measured and left alone -- the number is in *What this edition could
not do*, below, with a recommendation.

## Two: the traps are flat, and the stars keep the flames

**This is the owner's taste, and it is recorded as that.** Nothing was broken.

A star stands at each end of the board with two flames bursting off the board
edge. Around each star are the three trap cells, sunken wells where a captured
world drops out of the light and its rank counts for nothing -- and every one of
those six tiles carried two more small flames at its far corners. Two stars'
four flames, and twelve little horns around them. **The owner wants the trap
tiles flat and the horns kept for the star alone.**

They are gone. Each trap tile is now a plain tile 3.00 mm tall where it was
10.00, and the star's two flames are the only raised flames in the box.

**The sunken star stays, and it is not the same thing.** Sixteen flame tongues
are cut 0.40 mm *into* the tile's top face. They are engraved rather than raised
for a reason that has not changed: a relief under a seated disc would make a
trapped piece rock, which is the one thing a corona well must not do. They are
the star's light on the floor of the well, and with the horns gone they are the
only thing saying a trap is a trap. That is the risk this change takes and it
was put to an independent reader rather than assumed -- see below.

## What arrives

Forty-two printed parts: sixteen worlds, a board in four panels, twelve
asteroid-belt tiles, two stars, six corona cells and two storage trays. Nothing
is glued, screwed or assembled; the tiles and stars drop into their own pockets
and the panels butt together.

A cocoa-brown asteroid belt crosses the middle of the field, a star stands at
each end with two flames bursting off the board edge, and the three cells around
each star are sunken corona wells where a captured world drops out of the light
and its rank counts for nothing. You win by walking one of your worlds onto the
rival star.

## Where the eight surfaces come from

**All sixteen world pieces have now been corrected, one pair at a time, across
eight runs, and one of them has since been un-drawn.** A reader picking the set
up should be able to learn in one paragraph where each world's surface comes
from, without reading nine revision histories, so here is all eight worlds in
one line each.

- **Mercury** -- outline. Seven smooth albedo plains traced as rings, plus the
  Caloris basin as a bright floor inside a raised rim. **The Anti-Sol piece
  carries the same map handed the other way round**, because this world's
  0.03-degree tilt gives the pair no mirror of its own.
- **Venus** -- outline. Seven radar highland provinces led by Aphrodite Terra,
  and three lowland plains, from the Magellan mosaic. **The Anti-Sol piece
  carries the same map handed the other way round**, for the same reason: at
  177.36 degrees the two leans are five degrees apart and show nothing.
- **Earth** -- outline. Coastlines, dry interiors and a northern ice cap.
- **Mars** -- outline plus cap. The classical albedo map, and two polar caps
  whose rims are broken by lobes so they read as ice rather than as lids.
- **Jupiter** -- band, blob and outline. Six belts at Jupiter's own unequal
  latitudes, five, seven, ten, thirteen, seven and six degrees wide; five
  bright cream zones between and beyond them; and the Great Red Spot as one
  oval, thirteen degrees of arc by nine, with a darker collar and the widest
  belt bending north around it. The two widest belts carry a slight wave rather
  than running as exact circles.
- **Saturn** -- band, cap and **ring**. Five bands at nine, fifteen, eight,
  sixteen and twelve degrees wide, unevenly spaced and not symmetric about the
  equator; four of them a mid amber and only the widest still brown, because
  this is the world in the box whose reference is softer than the build was;
  and a bright cap above fifty-eight degrees north. The ring is geometry rather
  than a marking: a flat white plate in Saturn's equatorial plane, so it lies
  almost level.
- **Uranus** -- **nothing, and a ring.** No hood, no cap, no band, no spot: one
  undivided cyan sphere, the only bare world in the box. The ring is geometry
  rather than a marking: a hoop in Uranus's equatorial plane, which on a planet
  lying on its side means it stands upright over the globe. It prints in white,
  the same spool as Saturn's ring.
- **Neptune** -- band and outline. Three closed white bands of latitude, five,
  four and three degrees of arc wide, at forty-six to forty-one south, eleven
  to fifteen north and thirty to thirty-three north; each runs the whole way
  round the planet. And the Great Dark Spot as one outline oval twice as wide
  as it is tall. No companion cloud.

Seven of the eight are drawn, none of them as a union of circles. The eighth is
bare on purpose.

**The useful division is not which were corrected but where each surface comes
from.** Four of these worlds have a solid surface that has really been mapped
-- Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars -- so their markings are traced outlines, and
on Earth and Mars they are silhouettes you can check against a map you already
know. The other four are atmosphere. Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune have
no surface to trace; what they show is weather. **Three of those four are drawn
and the fourth is not.** Jupiter, Saturn and Neptune wear bands, caps and
storms. Uranus wears nothing, because its weather is a faint diffuse
brightening near a pole and the box has no way to print a thing that fades: a
marking here is a change of filament at a line. Drawn at all, it came out as a
pale disc, and the owner would rather have the bare ball. So the honest summary
is four mapped worlds drawn from outlines, three atmospheres drawn as weather,
and one atmosphere left alone.

That last one was the whole of the previous edition, and it has a condition
attached that is worth carrying forward: **bare separates Uranus from its
neighbours only while it is the only bare world in the set.** Nothing in this
edition changes any of the eight surfaces. What it changes is where two of them
sit on the Anti-Sol piece.

## The owner's second pass, in one place

**This edition closes the owner's second pass over the set**, and the honest
summary of that pass is worth one section rather than four scattered lines.

The first chain was correction against evidence: eight pairs of worlds, one pair
at a time, each measured against its own reference photograph. Earth got its
coastlines, Mars its dark continents, Mercury its plains and Caloris, Venus the
Magellan radar map, Jupiter its unequal belts, Saturn its quieter bands, Neptune
its clouds, Uranus its polar hoods. Every one of those was argued from a
measurement.

**The second pass is four things the owner did not like when he looked at the
finished set.** Three of the four reversed a decision an earlier run had
measured and argued for, and they are not improvements in accuracy. They are the
owner's call, and they are recorded as that:

1. **Neptune's clouds went back to three bands.** The reference shows short,
   broken, uneven cloud streaks; an earlier run measured that and drew them.
   What the set carries now is three closed white bands ringing the whole
   planet, which is **further from the reference photograph** than what it
   replaced. What was given up is the evidence-led cloud pattern and the
   companion cloud beside the dark spot.
2. **Uranus's two polar hoods came off and nothing replaced them.** The
   reference does carry a faint soft-edged brightening near a pole; the hoods
   were the box's way of drawing it, argued at length and measured twice. The
   globe is now bare. That is **further from the reference photograph** than
   what it replaced, and it leaves the wrong globe colour more exposed than it
   was, because there is nothing else on the ball to look at. What was given up
   is the one surface cue that world had.
3. **Uranus's ring turned white.** It printed in the globe's own cyan for one
   edition, measured and chosen so that it would read as relief rather than as a
   line drawn on the planet. White reverses that, and it costs the colour cue
   that separated Uranus's ring from Saturn's. What it buys is the set's own
   rule back: both ringed worlds print their ring from the same spool.
4. **This edition's part one is the opposite of the other three.** It is not a
   reversal and not a matter of taste: Mercury's and Venus's armies were
   supposed to be mirrors and were not, the owner saw it, and it is fixed. And
   this edition's part two -- the flat traps -- is a taste decision that reverses
   nothing, because the trap tongues were part of the corona cell from the first
   draft and were never argued for.

None of the three reversals is dressed up here as a further improvement to
accuracy. Two of them are measurably further from the photographs the set was
corrected against. The owner looked at the finished object and preferred them,
which is a good enough reason and is the reason given.

## What the blind reader said, including the part that is not comfortable

One independent reader was shown twenty-three pictures of this build and nothing
else -- not what the object is, not what had changed, not that anything had
changed. **Three rounds, and not one line of CAD changed at any point**, so the
pictures it read cold are the pictures sealed with this box.

**The frame set was balanced on purpose, and that is the one methodological
change this edition makes.** The previous attempt at this same correction built
the object correctly and could not seal, because its reader was shown sixteen
frames of which twelve were close-ups of one world and not one let the eight
globes be compared at a single scale. So this run put the rank-ladder frame --
all eight worlds of one army at one orthographic camera, in rank order -- into
the first, unprimed round, and balanced fifteen whole-object and pair frames
against eight close ones.

**It named the game before being told it**, and measured rather than guessed:
*"a two-player abstract strategy board game set, and the board's topology
identifies the game unambiguously: it is Dou Shou Qi / 'Jungle' / Animal Chess,
re-skinned as the solar system."* It derived the 7 x 9 grid from the board's own
corners. It named all eight worlds from their surfaces alone. And on the thing
this edition is for, it answered before it knew the question mattered.

### Part one, answered cold

Asked, pair by pair, which pairs are two different objects and which are one
object photographed twice, it wrote of Mercury's two pieces:

> "Same camera, same framing, same pose: the bright ring sits at x≈458 in
> **both** ... so there is zero yaw and zero pitch difference between the two
> shots. Yet the surroundings are swapped ... Left and right counts swap while
> the centred feature stays put. **That is a reflection, not a rotation. Highest
> confidence of anything in this report.**"

And of Venus: *"the band enters the left limb low ... and rises"* on one piece
against *"enters the left limb high ... and descends"* on the other. **"The
slope sign reverses."**

In the second round it went back and reflected its own recorded coordinates
about the ball's centre line. On Mercury: three marks, three matches, with two
of the three landing on the identical pixel row. On Venus: *"the band's high
point sits at (500,318) on Sol and (400,322) on Anti -- symmetric about the
ball's centre line at x≈450, at the same height to within 4 px."*

**On Jupiter, which this edition deliberately did not fix**, it wrote cold:
*"I cannot tell these apart and I would not notice if you swapped them."* That
sentence is the evidence the owner's decision should be taken on.

### Part two, answered cold

Asked to list every raised feature, it found the four flames on the two stars
and nothing on any trap tile. It then proved the trap's sunburst is cut rather
than raised, by an experiment nobody asked it to run:

> "It is definitively a cut and not a relief, because the same cell seen from
> directly above is a **completely featureless orange square**. A raised glyph
> would still show a silhouette from above; this one vanishes."

And in the second round, on the frame that would catch a horn if one were left:
*"The tile's far silhouette runs as an unbroken straight line ... Nothing breaks
that skyline anywhere along it. A 4.00 mm horn at a far corner would stand up
off that line unmistakably at this angle."* **"Twelve horns, if they were there,
are gone, and nothing has been put in their place."**

**And the trap cells are still findable.** This is the risk the edition takes,
and the answer came from a reader who did not know what a trap was and had not
been told anything was missing. It found all six, at both ends, and said how:
*"they are the only cyan cells anywhere on the board"*; *"each carries an
incised radiating sunburst glyph"*; *"they form a tight ring around the den and
nothing else on the board has that arrangement."* Asked again in the second
round: **"I did not experience the board as having a gap in it."**

### The uncomfortable part, reported straight

It filed twelve defects cold, and **four of them were high severity and wrong.**
It said the den was obstructed by its own flames; it said the bases do not fit
the cells; it said one army carries a mirrored map and therefore *"a mirrored
Earth and a mirrored Mars are not views of anything real"*; and it said the two
ringed worlds are transformed inconsistently. Told the measurements in round
two, it withdrew all four, re-measured Earth itself as a rotation rather than a
reflection, and retracted that sentence in as many words. It also diagnosed its
own failure: it had measured the 33.50 mm **floor of a trap well** and called it
a 36.00 mm cell.

Two more findings -- rings passing through bases -- it refused to either press
or drop, asking instead for a boolean: *"I would rather these got the same
boolean-intersection treatment that cleared defect 1 than have you act on my
eyes."* The assembly's interference check returned **zero clashes over 220
pieces and 412 intersected pairs**, and it withdrew them, adding the honest
summary: *"That makes me four for four wrong on interpenetration and fit
eyeballed from shadowless renders. That is not four independent mistakes, it is
one mistake made four times."*

**It also checked its own quotations, in both directions, because this edition's
brief required it** -- an earlier run in this chain had a reader invent a
verbatim self-citation to justify a verdict. It found the record accurate and
found an error of its own instead, unprompted: its round-one summary said *"six
of twelve comparisons show reflected faces"* when its own verdicts counted
eight. It put that on the record itself.

### And the verdict

**No blocking defects.** In round one it had said *"Finished: no"* on the
strength of three findings; all three were withdrawn, and it changed its answer
without being asked to: **"finished -- yes. I am not hedging that. The basis for
my refusal was three misreadings of shadowless renders and it does not survive
contact with the measurements."** On desirable it never wavered: *"the core move
... is a genuinely good one ... I would pick this up in a shop."*

**The most useful thing it produced is a finding nobody asked for.** Once it
understood that the lean is what normally separates a pair, it sorted the set:

> "Seven of eight are genuine mirror pairs ... Jupiter is the one that is not.
> Of the seven, **three read at board distance** (Saturn, Mars, Earth), **one is
> measurable but marginal at a glance** (Neptune), and **three need a close,
> matched-azimuth frame** (Mercury, Venus, Uranus)."

That reframes its own complaint: *"it stops being a complaint about the geometry
-- the geometry is right on seven of eight -- and becomes what it always should
have been: a photography finding."* Its recommendation, which this edition
records rather than acts on, is to shoot the pair frames at matched azimuth.

**The one finding it kept**, and it is not blocking: the 0.40 mm engraving on a
trap tile is invisible looking straight down, so from directly above a trap is
told by its colour and its recess and not by its star. Its fix, within the rule
that nothing may stand up: *"A 45° V-groove of the same 0.40 mm depth puts a
sloped facet on both sides of every ray, which catches light and reads from
directly above. Same depth, same 'nothing above the face', dramatically more
legible."*

The full account is in `cad/measure/signature-review-protocol.md`, and the
sealed record with every hash is `cad/snap/SIGNATURE-REVIEW.json`.

## What this edition could not do

**The mirrored map is a mirror-image map, and that is a real cost.** It is
stated at the top and it is worth restating here with the mitigation beside it.
On the Anti-Sol Mercury, the Caloris basin's scalloped rim is handed the other
way from the real basin's; on the Anti-Sol Venus, so are Aphrodite Terra, Beta
and Phoebe Regio and the three plains. Nothing else about them moved: same
latitudes, same sizes, same shapes, same count. What makes this a fair price is
that on these two worlds the longitudes were never a fact about the planet.
Mercury turns on no meridian this set fixes, and Caloris was placed at 50 west
by an earlier edition because that was the longitude that put it in front of the
camera; Venus's whole map is carried 90 degrees for the same reason, and that
number was itself swept from scratch against Aphrodite. So what is mirrored on
these two worlds is a placement this project chose, not an observation it made.

**Which worlds are drawn at real longitudes, for the record.** Earth's
coastlines and Mars's albedo map are at their real longitudes and are not
mirrored. Saturn's and Neptune's bands and caps are features of latitude, with
no longitude to get wrong. Jupiter's Great Red Spot was carried 110 degrees for
the camera by an earlier edition. Venus's whole map carries 90, and Mercury's
Caloris sits at 50 west, both for the camera. Uranus carries nothing at all.

**Jupiter has the same defect, was measured, and was left alone.** Its axial
tilt is 3.13 degrees, so its two pieces differ by 6.26 -- third-worst in the set
and in the same family as Mercury's 0.06 and Venus's 5.28 -- and the Great Red
Spot sits in very nearly the same place on both. Measured the same way as the
other two, on the built pieces at the set's own two camera angles: Jupiter's
markings move **1.24 mm between its two pieces**, which is **9 per cent of its
globe radius**. For comparison, the four marked worlds whose lean is not
degenerate move 43 to 73 per cent of their own radius, and Mercury and Venus,
after this correction, move 36 and 56 -- where before it they moved 0 and 6.
**Jupiter is now the one world in the set that reads as one object photographed
twice.**

*The recommendation, in one paragraph.* It is worth a further run, and it is the
cheapest correction left in this set. The machinery now exists and is general:
one function owns the reflection, it is keyed by planet, and adding Jupiter to
it is a one-word change followed by the same measurements this run made. The
cost is the same cost paid here -- Jupiter's Great Red Spot would be handed the
other way on the Anti-Sol piece -- and it is smaller on Jupiter than it was on
Mercury or Venus, because the spot is an oval with no handedness anyone can
check and its longitude was already carried 110 degrees for the camera, so it is
already a placement this project chose rather than a fact it observed. Against
that, Jupiter is the largest and most-recognised world in the box and the one a
player looks at first, so a pair that reads as one object costs the set more
here than anywhere else. The counter-argument is that Jupiter's bands are
latitude features and carry most of its appearance, so the mirror would move
only the spot and its collar and the pair would still read as close; the measured
answer to that is that the spot is exactly what a reader names Jupiter's pieces
by. **Recommended, with the owner told what it costs: a mirror-image Great Red
Spot on half the Jupiters, in exchange for the last pair in the set that does
not read as a pair.** The remaining five worlds need nothing: Mars at 25.19
degrees, Earth at 23.44, Saturn at 26.73, Neptune at 28.32 and Uranus at 97.77
put their two pieces 50.38, 46.88, 53.46, 56.64 and 164.46 degrees apart, which
is nowhere near degenerate, so this recommendation is scoped to Jupiter alone.

**A trap now rests on one cue.** With the two raised tongues gone, what tells a
trap cell from a land cell is the sunken sixteen-rayed star cut into a tile whose
colour already differs from the board's, plus the 3.00 mm recess a seated world
drops into. Nothing replaced the tongues, by instruction. With a world standing
in the well the engraving is largely covered, and what reads is the recess and
the ring of orange or cyan around the piece.

**Uranus's globe colour is still wrong**, and nothing in this edition touches it.
`cyan` #00FFFF is a saturated neon; the reference is a pale desaturated ice blue
measuring 161, 200, 206. The thirteen PLA colours this box prints from contain no
pale blue. `cad/measure/uranus-reference.md` measures it.

**And the ladder still does not announce itself.** Two of its seven steps are a
tenth of a millimetre, the rank numeral on the base rim cannot be read at playing
distance, and the rule behind the sizes -- that each globe is the fifth root of
the real planet's diameter -- is invisible from the object. Neither is in this
edition's scope and both are carried forward rather than answered.


## Before you print

Read `product.json` for the full list of limitations. Nothing here has been
printed, handled or played. Every fit, wall thickness and overhang margin is a
measurement on the exact CAD solids and a prediction for a 0.4 mm nozzle at
0.2 mm layers.

**The verification finished, and every check that could run did.** The pipeline
ran for **648.68 seconds** over eleven steps: **seven ran and passed, three are
recorded as not run rather than as passed, one is a note, and nothing failed or
was left unverified.** The geometry inspection completed **all 51 of its checks,
every one `passed`** -- that covers validating each of the 220 pieces of the
assembled set for sound topology and positive volume, validating all 24 printed
parts individually, and checking the whole assembly for interference, which is
the only way to answer whether any two parts occupy the same space. **Nothing
does: zero clashes over 412 intersected pairs, none truncated.** Of the 648.68
seconds, 241.66 went on regenerating all 25 entries from source and 389.91 on
the single inspection batch.

Separately, in their own rounds, **all 24 printed parts cleared the 0.80 mm wall
gate and the 45-degree overhang gate at a 0.4 mm nozzle** -- 24 of 24 on each.
The corona cell is the one whose figures moved: it had two trace regions of
unsupported surface at the old tongue bases and now has **zero**, its surface
area fell from 28.6 to 27.5 cm², and its volume from 3414.678 to 3292.462 mm³.
Twelve tapered flames came off six tiles for 0.909 g of filament across the set
-- which is not the point; the point is that six parts lost their tallest
feature.

**The hashes say what changed, and they say it in bytes.** 23 of the 24 printed
geometries are byte-identical to the published set's, including
`part_world_mercury_anti` and `part_world_venus_anti` -- which is the check this
edition's part one turns on, not a formality. Mirroring a map moves a colour
boundary inside the globe and no surface anywhere; if either of those two hashes
had moved, something had cut deeper than a marking is allowed to, and nothing
else in the run would have been worth reading. The 24th is
`part_corona_cell`, and it had to move: byte-identical there would have meant
the horns were still on the tile. Measured on the solids as well, occurrence by
occurrence: 220 pieces before and 220 after, none gained, none lost, and the only
ones whose geometry moved are `mercury_anti_*`, `venus_anti_*` and the corona
tiles. `cad/measure/revision-part-hashes.md` and
`cad/measure/occurrence-geometry.md`.

**And the mirror itself is measured on the built solids, not just on the
drawing.** A grid of points is classified on both pieces of each corrected world
and compared twice -- once at the same longitude, which must now disagree, and
once at the reflected longitude, which must agree. Mercury: 1101 of 4920 points
disagree at the same longitude, **0 of 4920 at the reflected one.** Venus: 272
of 1612, and **0 of 1612.** `cad/measure/mercury-surface.md` and
`cad/measure/venus-surface.md`.

**None of that makes this print-ready, and it is not claimed to be.** Every
number above is geometry. No part of this set has been printed, held, or played
with. `product.json` records the set as digitally verified and NOT print-ready,
and the sealed review carries no print-gate claim.

### Has any check ever been left unverified?

**One, and it is the same one in every run of this project: MOTION.** Motion
verification is switched off for these builds by the run's own options and is
recorded as unverified rather than passed. Nothing in this set moves against
anything else in any case -- no mechanism, no hinge, no assembly step -- so what
is unverified is a check with nothing to measure.

**Two further checks are recorded as not run rather than as passed**, in this
edition and in the one before it: the mounting check, because no bought part is
seated anywhere in this set, and the power check, because nothing in it is
powered.

**Everything else has run.** This edition's pipeline finished every check and its
geometry inspection completed all 51 with status `passed`. In the edition it
corrects, the same was true; an earlier attempt at that edition's pipeline was
interrupted part-way by a session ending, and **that attempt produced no verdict
and none was taken from it.** The records this project carries do not reach
further back than that for the pipeline's own figures, and that limit is stated
rather than papered over. The rule the chain has kept throughout is the one worth
saying plainly: **no check has ever been recorded as passed without having run.**
