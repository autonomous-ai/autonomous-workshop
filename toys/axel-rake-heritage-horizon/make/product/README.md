# Heritage Horizon

An unofficial imagined 2050 evolution of the Heritage Classic touring cruiser, represented at 1:12 scale. A low saddle, deep fenders, swept bars, windscreen and paired luggage surround a speculative electric package with V-shaped cooling shoulders. This is an original future interpretation, with no manufacturer affiliation.

The assembled model measures approximately 204.4 × 77.5 × 118 mm including its removable cradle. It contains 22 printed pieces and six purchased hardware instances. The wheels are intended to turn on stationary M3 axles while you guide the motorcycle upright. Steering and suspension are fixed. The screen and lamp are opaque, inert printed parts; the model has no electronics.

Read [assembly and printing guidance](heritage/README.md) for print quantities, prepared orientations, assembly order and the future-technology ledger. Print the 18 distinct `heritage/part_*.step` variants in that table's quantities. Purchase two M3 × 30 socket-head screws and four ordinary M3 nuts; do not print the hardware models.

`assembled.step` shows the complete placement. `parts/` includes named occurrence files for addressing the assembly and component-role copies required by the build groups. These are alternate addresses for the same pieces, not extra print quantities. Use the quantity table, not the number of STEP files, to prepare a set. The CAD project contains editable parametric source and numeric verification reports.

The signature image shows three samples of cradle withdrawal from the exact CAD model; the motorcycle must be held when away from the cradle. The animation shows sampled wheel rotation at fixed axle centers. Digital clearances and print checks do not establish physical rolling, thread friction, adhesive strength or print fit. No physical print or human-use test has been performed.

The proposed full-size 24 kWh battery package and its 2050 performance assumptions are speculative. No roadworthiness, range, production or delivery claim is made.
