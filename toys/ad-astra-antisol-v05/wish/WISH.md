# Wish

Jungle Chess played with the solar system. Every piece is a planet, and a planet's size on the board is the fifth root of its real measured diameter, so rank is something you can see rather than something you have to learn: Mercury is the smallest world in play and Jupiter the largest, and the traditional rules are followed exactly as written. Matter faces antimatter. One army's worlds lean their poles one way and the other army's lean the other, by each planet's own true axial tilt, and the bases give the same answer again in white against black. Four of the eight worlds wear the pattern a telescope shows, as circles and bands. The other four wear a real map: Earth its own coastlines, Mars its own dark continents, Mercury its smooth plains with the Caloris basin in them, and Venus -- alone in the set -- a surface no eye has ever seen. Venus used to wear its clouds and now wears the ground underneath them, from the Magellan radar map: a golden amber ball with the long sweep of Aphrodite Terra running most of the way round its equator and three dark lowland plains around it, and still upside down, because Venus's axis is tipped 177 degrees and this is the one world in the set whose map reads inverted. A cocoa-brown asteroid belt crosses the middle of the field, a star stands at each end with two flames bursting off the board edge, and the three cells around each star are sunken corona wells where a captured world drops out of the light and its rank counts for nothing. You win by walking one of your worlds onto the rival star.
