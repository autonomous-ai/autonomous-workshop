- Bounded searches for the exact title 'Rainward Sun', 'backgammon coronal rain', and nearby circular/solar/lunar backgammon found no direct same-combination product. This supports only the claim **an unusual original combination**, not universal novelty or exclusive rights.

## Complete playing instructions in original prose

Take the15 drops of one silhouette and a cup. Orient the Sun so both people can reach its face. A uses the printed point numbers from24 toward1; B uses the same points in reverse, so B's point q is printed point25-q. Place2,5,3,5 drops on your own24,13,8,6. Each six-point group is one bank; your own1–6 is your home. There is no occupied-point cap below the full15-drop inventory. Lay out rows and stacks so the count remains visible.

Choose whether the opening contest uses one die per person or two dice per person, keeping the same contest method for both people. With one die each, the higher face wins first play and that player may use the pair just shown or roll both again. With two dice each, compare totals; the higher total wins first play and may keep their own pair or roll both again. Equal comparisons decide no higher player, so repeat the contest. This preserves Britannica's one-or-two-dice option; it is not replaced with the compulsory single-die modern opening. The method and equal-comparison treatment spell out the ordinary meaning of a fair higher-throw contest rather than adding an asymmetric choice advantage.

After the opening, alternate rolls of both dice. On each die, move a drop exactly that many points toward your home and exit. One drop can use both dice, but it must make a legal landing for each die. On doubles, use the value four times. Play the entire throw whenever there is a legal sequence doing so, including choosing its order to avoid unnecessarily losing a move. If only part is possible, play every possible portion. Britannica does not state the modern rule compelling the higher die when either of two values but not both can be played; do not import it.

A point with two or more enemy drops cannot receive yours. A point containing exactly one enemy drop can: move it to the flare and replace it with yours. On your turn, every drop on the flare must enter before any of your other drops moves. A die1 enters your own24,die2 your23,and so forth through die6 your19, provided that entry point is not blocked. Entry can hit an enemy single. Continue the legal remainder of a roll after the last barred drop enters. If entry is blocked for the available dice, the unusable portion is lost.

You may bear off only when every drop still in play is in your own points1–6. A die can remove a drop on its matching point. You may make a legal move within the home instead. If no drop is on the die's point and any drop is farther from the exit, use the die to make a legal internal move. When all occupied points are below the die value, remove a drop from your highest occupied point. Continue to obey the whole-throw requirement. A hit stops further bearing until that drop has entered and returned home.

The first person to remove all15 wins immediately. Award1 when the other person has removed any drop; otherwise award3 if any enemy drop remains on the flare or in the winner's home; otherwise award2. These are single, gammon and backgammon results, respectively. There is no doubling device. For a series, reset the setup after every game. After a single, the winner has the first roll of both dice. After a gammon, each person casts a single die for first play, repeating ties; the winner retains the historical opening choice to use that pair or reroll. The same contested restart after a backgammon is the period-supported reading explained below.

During a roll, touching a playable drop commits you to play it when possible. Releasing it on a point completes that move. An impossible touched move causes no penalty. An accidentally omitted drop incurs no penalty; that does not grant an extra scoring benefit or change the setup inventory. Restore drops borne prematurely while a hit drop still awaited entry. Once the opponent has rolled after an incorrectly played throw, change that completed play only if both people agree. If either die is cocked, make a fresh two-dice roll. Apply these historical laws together with legal movement; no tournament clock, prescribed modern correction procedure, resignation rule or match threshold is added.

## Historical abbreviations resolved transparently

Britannica's wording is concise: it does not spell out an opening tied contest, comparison of two-die throws, or the restart following a triple backgammon. It also lists2 as a gammon and3 as a backgammon separately but speaks only of 'a gammon' in its restart sentence. The comparable period primary text [Hoyle's Games Modernized](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/39445/39445-h/39445-h.htm),1909, explicitly repeats tied opening casts and contests the next start after either gammon or backgammon. Its [Gutenberg record](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/39445) establishes the edition and public-domain status. We use this only to corroborate the interpretation of the abbreviated restart and tie language. We **do not adopt** that book's divergent option of a quadruple backgammon, its single-only opening description, or other different rules. Two-die contest total comparison and retention of the winning pair are transparent conventional readings of Britannica's stated option, not falsely quoted clauses.

Rule-equivalence audit: every explicitly stated ordinary1911 rule above has the same setup, legal actions, public information, transitions and result; no modern rule is substituted. The abbreviated cases are disclosed as interpretations instead of claiming the1911 paragraph expressly resolves them. No physical construction decision requires a rule change. Deterministic transition examples and geometric checks must still be implemented and checked by Make; this source-level ledger alone does not prove completed CAD or physical play.
