## Observed digital evidence

- Rabbit is unmistakable in its indexed view and all four distance corners:
  long ears, muzzle, haunch, feet, and tail survive projection.
- Fox remains ambiguous without its caption. Its rounded head, long upright
  ears, and very large rising tail can also read as a squirrel/rabbit hybrid.
- Owl preserves the round-two cheek lobes and two pointed tufts, but remains a
  solid cat/bear/pawn-like mass. Beak, eyes, wings, feet, and perch do not
  survive as distinct cues. It therefore does not meet the Wish's exact
  “unmistakable” threshold.
- The 12 distance cases cover all three indexed states at source-to-mask 400
  and 500 mm crossed with mask-to-wall 600 and 900 mm. Projected portal
  diameter spans 95.975–141.479 mm. Within-state dark-fraction variation is at
  most 0.004859, so scale response is stable; the stable ambiguous silhouettes
  do not become unmistakable.
- The five alignment cases challenge only owl at centered and ±2° yaw/pitch.
  Owl dark fraction remains 0.629096–0.632875, but rabbit and fox have no
  equivalent alignment coverage.
- The 72 poses at 5° increments honestly expose an eclipse wipe and indexed
  endpoints. Dark fraction spans 0.260471–0.633458 and maximum adjacent change
  is 0.047653. This is not evidence of continuous between-detent animal
  recognition.
- The product render exposes the large circular wheel, but the unique double-V
  reset is not visually discoverable. Source inspection finds cardinal relief
  ticks and bilateral portal bites, but no conspicuous fixed home pointer or
  phone-facing/beam-direction arrow, and no setup-side render demonstrates one.

## Result

**FAIL — implementation improvement in Make.** Strengthen the fox's snout and
ear language and rebuild the owl with unmistakable printable beak/eye openings
or stronger exterior beak, wing, feet, and perch separation. Regenerate exact
projection evidence and include all three indexed states at centered and ±2°
yaw/pitch. Add a conspicuous fixed rabbit-home pointer plus phone/beam arrow and
a setup-side render that makes both cues legible.

This is native-agent digital visual judgment, not a human play session. It
does not prove recognition by children, delight, normal-room contrast,
finite-phone-emitter performance, first-use behavior, physical fit, print
success, durability, or safety.
