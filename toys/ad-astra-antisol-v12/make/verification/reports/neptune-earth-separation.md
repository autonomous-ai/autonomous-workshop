# Neptune against Earth at the product's own frame

Ranks 5 and 4, Ø21.89 and Ø16.70 mm -- 5.19 mm apart in a set whose
ranks are told apart by size. Measured on the canonical side-by-side
renders at the hero frame (azimuth -55, elevation 22), one per army.

This revision changed Neptune's surface, so this pair is asked again
on this run's own renders rather than inherited from the archive.

## The size cue

| | Neptune | Earth | apart |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| rank | 5 | 4 | 1 |
| globe diameter mm | 21.89 | 16.70 | 5.19 |
| piece height mm | 24.89 | 19.70 | 5.19 |
| obliquity deg | 28.32 | 23.44 | 4.88 |

5.19 mm is 23.7 per cent of Neptune's own diameter and 13
nozzle widths, and the pieces stand 5.19 mm apart in height.
**Size separates this pair on its own**, which is what the size
ladder is for: they are 1 ranks apart and they look it.

## The colour cue

| | sealed hex | luma of 255 |
|---|---|---:|
| Neptune globe `blue` | #004EA8 | 67.9 |
| Earth globe `blue` | #004EA8 | 67.9 |

**They are the same spool.** Both globes are `blue` #004EA8, so colour
separates them by exactly nothing and cannot be asked to. This is
one of the two colour collisions this set records rather than
hides, and it is why the two globes that share a spool are put
four ranks apart in the ladder.

As rendered, measured on the pixels of the two globes themselves:

| army | Neptune mean RGB | Earth mean RGB | luma apart | Neptune marked | Earth marked |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sol | 15,128,174 | 13,146,149 | 10.9 | 9.0% | 5.5% |
| Anti-Sol | 20,129,174 | 4,124,133 | 9.6 | 11.2% | 13.6% |

The `marked` columns are the share of each ball that is not its
own globe colour, which is the surface cue reduced to one number.
Neptune's three bands cover 9.0 to 11.2 per cent of its visible
face; Earth's markings cover 5.5 to 13.6 per cent of its.

## The silhouette cue

| | Neptune | Earth |
|---|---|---|
| silhouette | a bare sphere on a disc | a bare sphere on a disc |
| piece height mm | 24.89 | 19.70 |
| widest, ring included | Ø21.89 | Ø16.70 |

Neither piece has anything but a ball on a disc, so the only
silhouette difference is SIZE -- and here that is a real one:
5.19 mm of globe and 5.19 mm of piece height, 24 per cent.
**The pair separates on silhouette, by size alone.**

## The surface cue

| world | what it wears |
|---|---|
| Neptune | three closed white latitude bands, 0.96, 0.76 and 0.57 mm wide, running the whole way round the globe, plus one dark_gray oval 5.35 by 2.67 mm south of the equator |
| Earth | the world's coastlines as green land with beige dryland inside it and a white ice cap, an outline map a reader can check against an atlas |

These are not the same KIND of marking, which is the cue a reader
uses once they are close enough for paint. **The pair separates on
surface.**

One honest caveat about Neptune's half of it, and it is the whole
subject of this revision: a closed latitude band is the most
generic marking a sphere can carry. It separates Neptune from these
two worlds because neither of them wears bands -- but it is the
reason the blind review of this build is asked, in so many words,
whether any piece in the set reads as a beach ball, and the answer
it gives is recorded in `snap/SIGNATURE-REVIEW.json` beside the
owner's decision rather than argued with here.
