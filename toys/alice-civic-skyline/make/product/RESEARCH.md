## Object and piece language

Make the set a city assembled from its skyline and shared daily life. Both armies use the same original architectural forms, with warm limestone and midnight slate colors. A repeated stepped octagonal plinth, broad vertical piers and recessed detail unify every role. Distinguish sides through high-contrast material color plus one versus two broad recessed plinth bands. Avoid applying names, faces or logos as the personalization.

The Manager's concrete baseline is accepted: 42 mm squares, 384 mm outer board split into four 192 mm quadrants, 24 mm frame, 5 mm board base, and 32 separate dark 41.5 × 41.5 × 2.6 mm square inlays with a 2.4 mm seat. Inlays are gravity seated; neither locking nor carrying the assembled board is promised. The board's checker pattern must remain flat and continuous across the four seams. No bolts or purchased parts are required.

| Role; total quantity | Baseline height / base | Required silhouette and chess cue |
|---|---|---|
| King; 2 | 86 / 30 mm | Empire State: tall rectangular shaft, four legible setbacks, vertical pier grooves, stout cross-shaped mast. The cross is an original chess identification adaptation, not a claim about the building's antenna. King remains the tallest and most vertically emphatic role. |
| Queen; 2 | 76 / 30 mm | Chrysler: broad ascending crown of nested arch bands, triangular recessed window marks, short robust central peak. Preserve the curved fan silhouette; an ordinary stepped pyramid will not read as Chrysler. No delicate projecting eagles. |
| Bishop; 4 | 64 / 28 mm | Flatiron: visibly triangular plan, rounded narrow prow, horizontal belt courses and assertive upper cornice. Add one broad shallow diagonal mitre groove to its upper crown as the conventional bishop cue. The groove is a chess translation, not a historic facade feature. |
| Knight; 4 | 58 / 28 mm | A clearly horse-shaped side silhouette built as continuous masonry-like planes: forward muzzle, sloped breast, strong neck, two blunt joined ears, stepped mane. Horse recognition takes priority over abstract architecture. Use a solid 11–14 mm thick profile, neck at least 10 mm wide and no separate reins, teeth or legs. |
| Rook; 4 | 52 / 28 mm | Brooklyn Bridge stone tower: paired Gothic portal recesses below a broad cornice and three strong capstones. The capstones form familiar castle crenellations. Keep openings recessed or self-supporting; no thin suspended bridge cables. |
| Pawn; 16 | 40 / 25 mm | New York rooftop water tank: short banded cylinder, low conical lid, stout closed support on the same plinth. Sixteen identical forms preserve the pawn family. No narrow freestanding stilts. The squat cap and shortest height must read as pawn at a glance. |

Maintain a comfortable primary grasp below each signature crown. Spires and ears need blunt ends and approximately 2.4–3 mm minimum local section, subject to the actual material/nozzle and gate results. Chamfer underside projections toward 45 degrees from vertical where practical. Keep substantial mass in the plinth and lower shaft. The 86 mm king on a 30 mm base is the stability-critical part: calculate its actual center of mass and tipping margin before acceptance; these dimensions alone are not stability evidence.

## Five cultural passages in one border

Use one continuous low relief route through the 24 mm frame. Give each passage a large primary form, then one supporting detail. Keep relief approximately 0.8–1.2 mm high with strokes at least 1.2–1.6 mm wide, away from playable squares and seams. These are deliberate interpretations of researched aspects of each community, not exhaustive portraits or decorative pseudo-writing.

| Placement | Exact recommended relief composition | Evidence and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| South: Chinatown | A sharply bent lane between two compact shopfront masses, with two wide recessed horizontal galleries, stout vertical mullions and shallow roof lips. Make the lane bend unmistakable before adding facade grooves. | Doyers Street's sharp middle bend is documented by MOCA. NYC Archives describes tong lau galleries, including a recessed second-floor gallery at 47–49 Mott Street. This honors local adaptation and association life without a generic pagoda or invented Chinese characters. |
| West: Williamsburg | Three tall round-headed masonry bays under a single strong roofline, followed by one broad rectangular chimney. The arches and stack should occupy one coherent industrial building silhouette. | NYC Planning identifies the former sugar refinery's round-arched openings and Williamsburg waterfront industrial history. This represents an industrial layer of Williamsburg; omit commercial signage and current developer logos. |
| North: Bronx | Two large concentric record circles on a common rectangular deck; a short stout tonearm mark for each, and three stepped rhythm bars between/above them. Make the paired decks dominant rather than tiny circles in generic trim. | Smithsonian connects early Bronx hip-hop innovation to DJs working two turntables. This is original geometry referring to a documented creative practice; no artist likeness, copied graffiti, label or record sleeve. |
| West/south: Staten Island | A long commuter ferry body with two separated horizontal deck bands, a simple upper wheelhouse and a strong symmetric bow/stern; three broad parallel wake lines beneath. Preserve the air gap between deck bands through shallow recessed channels. | NYC DOT documents the ferry link between St George and Whitehall and city operation since 1905. This represents daily harbor connection; it is an original abstraction, not a scale claim for a named vessel class. |
| East: Queens | A horizontal elevated railway girder supported by three broad piers, a short train silhouette on top, and unequal shopfront bays beneath. Continue the common border route through the piers. | NY Transit Museum describes the 7 line as the International Express through diverse communities, with transportation and migration shaping neighborhood life. Elevated transit and varied street bays interpret that connection. Omit MTA route bullets and copied maps. |

The relief supplies community detail while the pieces supply long-distance skyline recognition. Both sides share all five passages; no borough is assigned the antagonist army. The border must stay visually subordinate to the alternating playing field. Labeling in the guide may explain each passage, but its geometry must carry the distinctive primary cue.

## Standard game, inventory and identification

This is orthodox chess, with no invented moves, powers, capture conditions or board spaces. The source is [FIDE Laws of Chess](https://rcc.fide.com/fide-laws-of-chess_fulltexthtml/), effective 1 January 2023. Use the usual 8 × 8 alternating board, light square at the player's near right, light army moving first, and queen on her own color. Back ranks are rook, knight, bishop, queen, king, bishop, knight, rook; eight pawns occupy the next rank for each side. The complete starting inventory is 2 kings, 2 queens, 4 bishops, 4 knights, 4 rooks and 16 pawns: 32 pieces.

The identification guide should show an actual rendered silhouette of all six roles with both role and building/type names, plus a correctly oriented initial setup. Explain standard castling, en passant, check/checkmate and promotion using faithful paraphrase. Promotion may select queen, rook, bishop or knight of the same color and is not limited to previously captured pieces. Provide the existing role CAD/print files for additional promotion pieces; do not introduce a rule requiring a captured piece or inverted rook.

For storage, provide a photographed/rendered inventory layout and separate protected places for the six signature profiles if a carrier is built. Keep pressure off crown tips. Storage geometry is not yet part of this handoff's verified baseline and must not be represented as complete until all 32 actual pieces fit. A simple separate tray is preferable to creating an unverified folding or retention mechanism.
