# False Lantern

A two-player firefly signaling duel for age 14+. Target play time: 5–12 minutes; the nine-exchange limit is proven by the rules, but human play time is not yet physically verified.

## Inventory

Six printed parts:

- 1 night-garden tray/board/storage case
- 1 sliding moon-screen lid
- 2 reversible firefly selectors: crescent owner and notch owner
- 2 three-tip score/route leaves: crescent owner and notch owner

No cards, dice, magnets, electronics, batteries, glue, paint, or loose decoration are required. Color is optional and never carries a rule.

## Read the geometry

- Fern = one pulse; Arch = two pulses; Reed = three pulses. Each route dock and each leaf tip repeats this 1/2/3 pip grammar.
- A selector's two raised wing strokes are the **Flight** face for the Caller. Its single recessed eye is the **Reply** face for the Watcher.
- The crescent-edge selector belongs with the **one-stud leaf**. The diamond/notch-edge selector belongs with the **two-stud leaf**. These pairings, not color, define ownership.
- A leaf's position on its six-post vine is score 0–5, from the single recessed center pit toward the five-pip end. The score-0 leaf covers that pit, so use the visible five-pip end as the direction check.
- The leaf tip aimed inward toward the moon spine is that owner's last route.
- On the screen, separated center clusters of 1/2/3 pips remind you of the routes. The isolated end clusters remind you: Echo = 1 spark, Mimic = 2 sparks. This manual defines the rules.

## Setup

1. Slide the lid out through the open end. Put the tray between the players with one mirrored dock bank facing each player.
2. Stand the lid on its keyed long edge in the central moon slot. It is now the intended privacy screen and pulse/stake reference.
3. Give the crescent-edge selector and one-stud leaf to one player. Give the notch-edge selector and two-stud leaf to the other.
4. Put both leaves on score post 0. Rotate each leaf so its one-pip Fern tip points inward. Both players start at score 0 and last route Fern.
5. For the first game, the crescent owner is Caller and the notch owner is Watcher. Reverse the opening Caller in an immediate rematch.

## One exchange

1. **Read the forbidden perch.** The inward-pointing tip on the Caller's leaf is their previous route. The Caller may fly to, and may claim, only one of the other two routes.
2. **Secret Flight.** Behind the screen, the Caller puts their selector Flight face up in either legal route dock and says **set**. It may not move afterward.
3. **Public flash.** The Caller says either legal route and its pulse count. The claim may name the actual route (truth) or the other legal route (bluff); it may not repeat the forbidden perch. Optional matching taps may accompany speech, but speech controls.
4. **Secret Reply.** After hearing the claim, the Watcher puts their selector Reply face up in one of the same two routes other than the Caller's previous route and says **locked**. This predicts the Caller's actual route and may not move afterward.
5. **Reveal.** Lift the screen straight up by its center. Leave both selectors where they are and compare their route docks.
6. **Set the stake.** If prediction equals claim, the reply is an **Echo** worth 1 spark. If prediction differs from claim, it is a **Mimic** worth 2 sparks.
7. **Name the scorer.** If prediction equals actual, the Watcher scores. Otherwise the Caller scores.
8. **Execute the two leaf updates in this order:**
   1. Move the scoring player's own leaf along their own vine by the stake, stopping at post 5. Keep that leaf's existing inward-pointing route tip during the move.
   2. Rotate the Caller's leaf on its current score post until the tip matching the revealed actual route points inward. Do this even when the Watcher scored and even on the final exchange. Do not rotate the Watcher's leaf merely because the Watcher predicted correctly.
9. If either leaf is on post 5, that owner wins immediately. Otherwise clear both selectors, reseat the screen, and swap Caller and Watcher.

## Complete resolution and update example

Crescent owns the crescent selector and one-stud leaf. Crescent is Caller, has 2 sparks, and their leaf points Fern inward. Notch owns the notch selector and two-stud leaf, has 3 sparks, and their leaf points Reed inward.

Fern is forbidden for Crescent, so Arch and Reed are legal. Crescent secretly flies Reed but publicly claims Arch. Notch predicts Reed. Prediction differs from claim, so this is a two-spark Mimic. Prediction matches actual, so Notch scores.

First move Notch's two-stud leaf from score post 3 to post 5 while preserving its Reed-inward orientation. Then rotate Crescent's one-stud leaf on post 2 so Reed points inward, because Crescent was the Caller and actually flew Reed. Notch wins immediately at 5; the final Caller-route rotation still remains part of the recorded state.

## Ending and bound

The first player to 5 sparks wins. Exactly one player gains 1 or 2 sparks on every scoring exchange. A finished game therefore takes at most nine scoring exchanges, cannot tie, and cannot loop through legal play.

## Irregular play: check in this order

Use the first applicable line; do not alter either last-route orientation on a forfeit. The first applicable fault ends the exchange and replaces normal Echo/Mimic scoring, so only the stated one spark is awarded.

1. **Caller Flight is forbidden:** reveal enough to determine the Caller committed to their previous perch. Caller forfeits; Watcher gains 1 spark. Ignore the Watcher selector when deciding this fault.
2. **Caller claim is forbidden:** if the spoken claim repeats the Caller's previous perch, Caller forfeits immediately; Watcher gains 1 spark. Reset selectors without revealing more than necessary.
3. **Watcher Reply is forbidden:** if the first two checks are clear and the Watcher predicted the Caller's previous perch, Watcher forfeits; Caller gains 1 spark.
4. **Moved after commitment:** moving a selector after its owner said **set** or **locked** makes that owner forfeit 1 spark.
5. **Screen knock before both locks:** the first knock by a player resets both commitments with the same roles and no score. Keep that player's warning. A second knock by the same player before the next scoring exchange makes that player forfeit 1 spark.
6. **Screen falls after both locks:** treat the fall as the reveal and evaluate unchanged commitments. If a responsible player moved a selector so its route cannot be determined, that player forfeits 1 spark.
7. **Speech and tap disagree:** the spoken legal route controls. A tap alone is not a claim; say one legal route before the Watcher locks.

For every forfeit, move the opponent's leaf exactly one score post while preserving its orientation; rotate neither leaf; end at 5 or otherwise clear, reseat, and swap roles. Standard speech is limited to commitment words, the pulse claim, resolution, and rules questions.

## Pack away

Return both selectors to the middle route docks and both leaves to score 0/Fern. Hold the lid broad face level, insert its left edge under the two side rails through the open end, and slide until it meets the closed end stop. Never force a warped lid.

## Print the six-part set

Print only the six `cad/part_*.stl` component files from the sealed design package, each separately in its exported orientation at Z=0. Do not print `assembled.stl`; it is a digital inspection assembly. The tray prints garden-side up, the lid on its broad outer face with the screen key upward, and selectors and leaves on a broad face.

The digitally validated starting process is generic PLA on an OrcaSlicer 2.3.2 Ender-3 profile: 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.20 mm layer height, three wall loops, 15% infill, supports disabled, and brim disabled. Across six separate jobs, the engine reported zero out-of-bounds parts, zero slicer errors, 0.0 g support material, 44.33 g / 35.75 cm3 estimated material, and 12,645 seconds estimated time. These are digital estimates, not a Bambu-specific result or proof of a successful print.

Before printing the game, use the supplied clearance coupon to evaluate 0.20, 0.25, 0.30, 0.35, and 0.40 mm clearance per side on the intended printer and material. A physical coupon and measurement are required before treating any nominal clearance as calibrated. If fit requires a CAD change, regenerate every dependent output and repeat Make and Playtest rather than altering a single mesh in isolation.

## Assemble for storage or play

No glue, magnets, screws, or permanent assembly are required. For storage, place the selectors in their matching middle route docks and the leaves at score 0 with Fern inward. Hold the lid broad face level, insert its left edge from the tray's open end under both rails, and slide to the end stop. For play, withdraw the lid fully, rotate it upright, seat its keyed long edge in the central slot, and lift vertically by the center during reveal. Never force a warped or binding lid.

## Prepare, inspect, and care

Let parts and the build plate cool before removal. Remove stringing and burrs without sharpening an edge. Before every use, inspect for cracks, delamination, sharp burrs, loose fragments, warping, or an unstable lid. Confirm the lid slides from the open end without force, remains retained when closed, seats upright without rocking into the docks, and clears both commitments during a vertical reveal. Stop using any damaged or uncertain part.

Store all six pieces in their counted recesses with the lid closed. Keep the set dry and away from flame, high heat, food, pets, and children who may mouth components. Do not force a warped lid, throw pieces, or put any piece in the mouth. Physical fit, wear, opacity, stability, and durability remain unverified until hands-on evidence exists.

## Safety

Contains small parts. Choking hazard. Not for children under 3 years. Designed for age 14+ tabletop use; this is not an age-suitability or toy-safety certification. Keep faces and hands away from the lid edge while inserting or lifting the privacy screen. Follow the printer and filament manufacturers' ventilation, hot-surface, moving-machine, and material-handling instructions.
