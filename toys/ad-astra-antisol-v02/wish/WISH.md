# Wish

Jungle Chess played with the solar system. Every piece is a planet, and a planet's size on the board is the fifth root of its real measured diameter, so rank is something you can see rather than something you have to learn: Mercury is the smallest world in play and Jupiter the largest, and the traditional rules are followed exactly as written. Matter faces antimatter. One army's worlds lean their poles one way and the other army's lean the other, by each planet's own true axial tilt, and the bases give the same answer again in white against black. Seven of the eight worlds wear the pattern a telescope shows; Earth wears its own coastlines, so the one world you already know by heart is the one you can name in the hand. A cocoa-brown asteroid belt crosses the middle of the field, a star stands at each end with two flames bursting off the board edge, and the three cells around each star are sunken corona wells where a captured world drops out of the light and its rank counts for nothing. You win by walking one of your worlds onto the rival star.
