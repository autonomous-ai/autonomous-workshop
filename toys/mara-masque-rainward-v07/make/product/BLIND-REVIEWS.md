# Blind reviews - Rainward Sunflare

One independent critic, two unprimed reads and one primed comparison, recorded
verbatim. In rounds 1 and 2 it was shown only renders and told nothing about
the object, the Wish, the concept or the maker; round 2 disclosed only that one
property of the rim shapes had been altered, without saying which property or
in which direction, and is presented as a rereview, not as a naive read. Round
3 is the requirement comparison: by then it had the brief, so nothing in it is
a naive read and none of it is presented as one. No review from the set being
corrected is reused here as evidence, and the reads below are of this build's
own images.

The critic is a native subagent of this Make session, given file paths, a fixed
answer schema and nothing else. It could read the images and write its own
answer; it could not read the source, the spec, the Wish or any report.

The images each round was shown are listed with their sha256 in
`cad/snap/SIGNATURE-REVIEW.json`.

## Round 1 - naive read of the first build of this revision

Shown `iso.png`, `top.png`, `setup-top.png`, `rim-detail.png`,
`rim-unwrapped.png`, `hub-detail.png`, `side-profile.png` and `signature.png`
of the first build. No brief, no concept, no prior context. The forced
sun-or-wheel question was put last, after every free-form answer was already
written.

**A. HELD OBJECT**

It is a flat circular game board - a sun-shaped wooden disc about the size of a
dinner plate, cut into pie wedges, with a plain round hub in the middle, a ring
of carved flame/fin shapes standing out all around the edge, and two sets of
small teardrop-shaped playing pieces (cream and dark brown) sitting loose on
the wedges.

**B. VOLUMETRIC FORM**

It is overwhelmingly a plate, not a solid. In the side view the body spans
roughly 1045 px of image width against a slab about 30-35 px thick, so the
thickness is on the order of 1:30 of the diameter - a flat disc you would hold
the way you hold a record or a serving tray. The profile is essentially a
constant-thickness slab with no doming, no dish, no chamfer that I can detect:
top face flat, bottom face flat, rim cut square. The only relief above the top
face is the playing pieces (short extruded prisms with a teardrop footprint,
standing maybe 25 px, i.e. under a tenth of the disc radius) and the only
relief in plan is the flame fringe, which the unwrapped sheet labels as living
between radius 0.86 and 1.22 of the disc - so the flames are rooted 14% inside
the edge and project 22% beyond it.

Surfaces are flat-shaded facets with hard edges and no fillets anywhere; the
wedge seams are drawn as thin raised orange slivers rather than as grooves, so
the top face reads as marquetry (inlaid veneer strips) rather than as carved
relief. The hub is a slightly proud flat circle, about 0.30 of the disc
diameter. In the iso view the whole thing reads as laminated sheet goods -
plywood or thick card - not as a turned or moulded part.

**C. SUBJECTS**

Four distinct kinds of thing:

1. **The wedge field.** Roughly 24 radial sectors (I count about 12 pale cream
and 12 darker ochre alternating), each a true pie wedge running from the hub
circle to the outer rim, separated by thin lighter-orange seam lines. They are
identical in shape and differ only in the two-tone alternation.

2. **The hub.** One peach-orange disc at the centre carrying four short dark
tick marks arranged roughly at 12, 3, 6 and 9 o'clock. They are not a matched
set: the 12 and 6 ticks are longish and near-vertical, the 3 and 9 ticks are
shorter, thicker, tilted, and sit at different distances from centre and at
different heights. Read as a face, they are two eyes, a nose and a mouth; read
as engineering, they are registration marks. Either way they are the crudest
element on the object.

3. **The rim flames.** About 24 crescent/shark-fin shapes around the border,
all the same family but wildly different in scale - in the unwrapped strips the
tall ones stand about twice their base width, the stubby ones under
three-quarters of it, a roughly 3:1 height range within a single ring.

4. **The pieces.** Small teardrop tokens in two colours, cream-white and dark
grey-brown, laid out in single-file radial rows on the wedge faces. In the
setup view I count about eight rows of 2, 2, 3, 3, 5, 5, 5 and 6 - roughly 15
dark against 16 cream, which is not an obviously symmetric or balanced starting
position.

**D. ACTION**

It reads as a two-player board game played on a circular track: you set the
cream pieces and the dark pieces into the radial lanes, and move them wedge by
wedge around the hub. The hub's ticks would be a start/quarter marker. A person
would sit across the board from an opponent, pick up a teardrop token by its
fat end and step it along. Nothing here rotates - there is no axle hole, no
bearing, no bore through the hub - so it is not a spinner or a wheel in the
working sense. The flames are pure ornament; they carry no teeth, no notches,
no detents, nothing a finger or a pawl would engage.

**E. RELATIONSHIP**

Concentric, in three bands. The hub sits at the centre and owns the inner 30%.
The 24 wedges radiate from the hub edge out to the rim and form the playing
field. The flames are rooted underneath/behind the wedge field at r=0.86 and
stick out past the rim to r=1.22, so their bases are buried under the disc and
only their outer halves are visible - which is why their visible widths vary at
random. The pieces sit loose on top of the wedge faces, resting on them,
aligned nose-to-tail along a wedge's long axis. Nothing is inside anything;
nothing is joined; the only real assembly relationship is disc-and-fringe, and
even that is unresolved (see G).

**F. THE EDGE**

In the close crop (image 4) I count eight flames across roughly the top 140
degrees; in the three unwrapped strips (image 5), each covering 120 degrees, I
count about eight per strip - so about 24 around the full circumference, one
per wedge nominally, though they do not register to the wedge seams.

Where they meet the body they are wide: in the unwrapped strips the bases
measure roughly 70-90 px, and the visible lengths range from about 50 px (the
stubs) to about 150 px (the tall ones). So the tall ones are about 2:1
long-to-wide and the stubs are shorter than they are wide, under 1:1. The
rhythm is irregular - tall, medium, stub, tall, stub, medium - not a clean
alternation and not a monotonic gradient.

They end in points, but not sharp points: every one terminates in a small
rounded tip, like a blunted claw. There is bare background between most of them
- a clear notch of pale background dips right down to the rim line between
neighbours in the unwrapped strips. But not all of them: in image 4 several
bases run together where a stub is tucked under its taller neighbour, and in
the full top view the flames at 11-1 o'clock overlap one another so that the
intervening background is closed off entirely. So the fringe is intermittently
separated and intermittently fused, with no rule.

Lean: the great majority lean the same way. Every silhouette is asymmetric in
the same sense - one edge is a steep convex leading curve and the other is a
longer, shallower, slightly concave trailing edge, and I never see that
asymmetry mirrored. At 12 o'clock in image 4 the tall central flame rakes to
the left, and its neighbours on both sides rake the same direction, which means
a consistent counterclockwise sweep around the disc, like a pinwheel. That is
how I can tell: the convex/concave pairing is constant, so it is a rotational
rake and not random wobble. The exception I'd flag: one or two flames near the
right edge of image 4 read to me as leaning the other way, which may be
foreshortening or may be a genuine mirror; I can't resolve it, and that itself
is a defect - a uniform rake should be unambiguous from any crop.

**G. WHAT LOOKS WRONG**

Plenty.

- **The fringe does not sit flush with the disc.** In the side profile the
flames appear as an orange scalloped skirt whose top surface is clearly below
the disc's top face, and the disc's front edge overlaps them. Rather than one
coplanar plate, this reads as two plates at different heights, or as a fringe
that interpenetrates the body. It is the single most structurally confusing
thing in the set.

- **The flames do not register to anything.** There are about 24 wedges and
about 24 flames, but the flames do not sit at wedge centres or at wedge seams;
several straddle a seam in the unwrapped strips. A thing with 24-fold symmetry
that fails to line up its two 24-count features looks accidental.

- **The size variation in the flames looks like noise, not design.** A 3:1
length range in no discernible pattern, with a couple of stubs so short they
barely clear the rim, reads as a parameter jitter that nobody reviewed.

- **The hub ticks are sloppy.** Four marks that should be at 90-degree spacing
are at unequal lengths, unequal radii, unequal thicknesses and slightly wrong
angles. The left tick sits below centre, the right tick above. This is the part
a hand would touch first and it is the least resolved.

- **Pieces cross wedge boundaries.** In the iso view several tokens sit half on
a cream wedge and half on the ochre neighbour, and in the top row two dark
pieces overlap each other. If the wedges are the cells of the game, pieces
straddling cells is a setup error.

- **The three "identical" thumbnails in image 8 are not identical.** Piece
counts and positions shift between them, and in the third panel a stray dark
teardrop sits directly on the hub, on top of the tick marks, where no other
view has a piece at all. That is a floating/misplaced part.

- **The silhouette is not round.** In the top view and in the thumbnails the
body is measurably elliptical rather than circular, and the thumbnails are
squashed differently from each other.

- **Seam rendering is dirty.** In the unwrapped strips the wedge boundaries
render as ragged dotted lines rather than clean edges, which suggests
coincident or near-coincident faces at those seams.

- **The piece sets are unbalanced.** ~15 dark against ~16 cream, in rows of
2/2/3/3/5/5/5/6 with no visible symmetry, so the "setup" view does not depict a
legal-looking starting position.

**H. SUN OR WHEEL**

**Sun.** The flames are soft, tapering, rounded-tipped and swept - they are
licks of flame or rays, not teeth; a wheel's edge features would be square,
uniform and load-bearing, and a gear's would register exactly, which these
conspicuously do not. It also has no axle, no bore, no hub bushing and no rim
tread, so there is nothing to rotate on. The face-like ticks at the centre seal
it: this is a sun disc that someone has pressed into service as a game board.

### What round 1 decided

Round 1 answered the forced question **sun**, and read the edge as licks of
flame rather than teeth. That is the defect this Wish exists to fix, and it was
already fixed by the three changes.

But part F was a fail against acceptance clause 1, which requires the mixed
handedness to be visible: the critic reported "a consistent counterclockwise
sweep around the disc, like a pinwheel" and could not resolve the one or two
flames it thought might be mirrored. Measurement said the split was a correct
14 against 18. The repair is recorded in `DESIGN.md`: a flame's visible rake
scales with its length, only the eight tall flames carry it at a glance, and
under the first walk constant those eight fell into same-sign pairs. Only the
walk constant moved.

Every other item in G was checked against the geometry rather than accepted or
dismissed; the results are in round 3 below.

## Round 2 - rereview of the repaired build

Shown regenerated `iso.png`, `top.png`, `rim-detail.png` and
`rim-unwrapped.png`. The critic was told that the object had changed, that it
was therefore no longer a naive viewer, and that exactly one property of the
rim shapes had been altered - not which property, and not in which direction.
It was given no other context and still had not seen the Wish. Fresh
observations were required before any comparison with round 1.

**A. FRESH EDGE READ**

A ring of curved, fin- or flame-shaped blades standing proud of a flat two-tone
wedge disc, rooted (per the strip labels) at radius 0.86 and reaching to 1.22
of the disc.

**Count.** In the top strip of `rim-unwrapped.png` (96-216 degrees, exactly 120
degrees) I resolve ten complete shapes plus one cut off at the left frame edge
- bases at roughly x = 125-200, 260-320, 405-480, 565-655, 700-765, 845-905,
985-1060, 1155-1230, 1300-1365, 1450-1520, mean pitch about 145 px. The second
and third strips give the same pitch and the same count. Eleven per 120 degrees
x 3 = **about 32-33 around the full circle**, allowing one double-count at a
strip seam. That does not divide the wedge count: the same strips show eight
wedge rectangles per 120 degrees, i.e. 24 wedges. So 33 blades against 24
wedges - the two rings cannot register, and in each strip you can watch the
blade bases drift steadily across the wedge seams from left to right, several
sitting directly astride a seam.

**Width vs length.** Bases run 60-95 px. Heights run from about 40 px (the
stubs) to about 160 px (the tall ones). So the tall blades are roughly 2:1
length-to-base-width and the stubs are roughly 0.6:1 - shorter than they are
wide. A 4:1 height range within one ring, in the repeating-but-not-quite rhythm
medium / stub / medium / tall / medium / stub / tall / medium / stub / medium.

**How they end.** Every one ends in a single rounded tip - a blunted point,
never a sharp spike, never a square end, never a fork. Tip radius looks
constant across all sizes, which makes the stubs read as squashed versions of
the tall ones rather than as a different feature.

**Gaps.** In the unwrapped strips there is bare background between every
neighbouring pair, and the background reaches all the way down to the rim line
in each gap - the fringe is fully separated when you look at it flat. But in
the assembled views it is not: in `top.png` at about 10-11 o'clock and again at
about 5 o'clock, neighbouring blades overlap in projection and close the gap
completely, while at 2 o'clock and 4 o'clock a blade is almost entirely hidden
behind the disc edge and shows only a nub. So: separated in fact, intermittently
fused in appearance.

**B. LEAN**

Judged against radial, which in the unwrapped strips is simply vertical.

**`rim-unwrapped.png`, top strip (96-216 degrees), left to right,** taking apex
position against base midpoint:

right, straight, left, right, left, straight, left, right, straight, left

(the eleventh, at the left frame edge, is cut and unresolvable).

**`rim-unwrapped.png`, third strip (336-456 degrees), left to right:**

straight, left, right, right, left, left, straight, straight, left, left, right

**`rim-detail.png`, along the visible arc, left to right.** Here radial is not
vertical, so I fitted the rim: the arc passes through about (730, 400) at the
crest, (180, 600) left and (1280, 600) right, giving a centre near (730, 1256),
R = 856. Measuring each blade's axis against its own radius:

right (+21 deg), right (+25 deg), left (-8 deg), right (+19 deg), left (-23
deg), right (+11 deg), right (+6 deg), left (-23 deg)

**They do not all lean the same way.** Roughly half rake one way and half the
other, in no repeating order, with magnitudes from about 5 to about 25 degrees.

The evidence I would point at, because it needs no geometry at all: in the
third unwrapped strip there are two tall blades of near-identical height, one
with its base at x = 355-440 and one at x = 790-860. The first has a long
sloping left edge and a steep right edge, apex at x = 432 - well right of its
base midpoint at 398. The second is the exact mirror: steep left edge, long
slope to the right, apex at x = 805, left of its base midpoint at 825. Two
neighbours of the same size in the same flat strip, where vertical *is* radial,
with mirrored silhouettes. A uniform rake cannot produce that. The same test in
the top strip gives the tall blade at x = 565-655 leaning right and the tall
blade at x = 985-1060 leaning left - again mirrored, again in one strip.

**C. WHAT IT READS AS**

The edge treatment reads as **flame** - tapering, rounded-tipped, curling licks
of fire, now flickering in inconsistent directions the way real flames do,
rather than the raked pinwheel vanes I took them for before.

**SUN.** Blades with mixed, unrelated rake angles cannot be teeth, vanes or
blades in any working sense - a saw, a ratchet, a turbine or an impeller all
require a single consistent rake, and mixed rake is the one thing that
positively rules those out. Add a hub with no bore, no axle, no bearing and
four face-like ticks on it, and a fringe that is pure silhouette with nothing
to engage, and the only coherent reading left is a decorative sun disc.

**D. AGAINST MY FIRST READ**

**Clearly false.** Last time I wrote that the great majority leaned the same
way in "a consistent counterclockwise sweep around the disc, like a pinwheel",
with one or two I could not resolve. That does not survive these images, and it
is not a marginal call: in a single unwrapped strip, where vertical is radial
by construction, I can point at two tall blades of matched height whose
silhouettes are exact mirrors of each other. That is not one or two unresolvable
outliers at the edge of a crop - it is roughly a fifty-fifty split, in both
strips I measured and in the assembled crop.

What changed my mind was the method as much as the pictures. My first read
judged lean in image space - "the blade at 12 o'clock rakes toward the left" -
which silently treats vertical as radial everywhere, and that is only true at
12 o'clock. Once I fitted the rim circle in `rim-detail.png` and measured each
blade against its own radius, the tidy sweep dissolved: the crest blade is +19
degrees clockwise of radial while its immediate left neighbour is -8 degrees
counterclockwise, and they were the two I would previously have called "both
leaning left" because both tips point up-left in the picture. They do, and it
means nothing.

So I have to hold two things at once and say so plainly. The lean is mixed now;
I am confident of that and it is measurable. I cannot cleanly separate "the rake
was altered" from "my first reading of the rake was sloppy", because the first
reading was sloppy enough that it would have reported a uniform sweep either
way. What I can say is that the count, base widths, heights, height rhythm, tip
treatment and root radius all look unchanged to me from the previous set - so
if exactly one edge property was altered, rake direction is the only candidate
I can see any room for, and the object as it now stands has no consistent rake.

I will also correct an error of my own: I said last time there were about eight
blades per 120 degree strip and 24 in total, matching the wedge count. That was
an undercount. There are eleven per strip and about 33 in total, and they have
never matched the 24 wedges.

**E. ANYTHING NEW THAT LOOKS WRONG**

- **The mixed rake reads as noise, not as design.** Randomized heights I could
accept as flicker; randomized heights *and* randomized rake, with no pairing,
no mirror symmetry about any axis and no alternation, looks like two
independent jitter parameters left switched on. Nothing in the object is
symmetric about anything.

- **33 blades against 24 wedges.** Visible directly in the unwrapped strips: 11
silhouettes over 8 wedge rectangles. The bases precess across the seams, and in
the top strip the blades at x = 405-480 and x = 1155-1230 straddle a wedge
boundary outright.

- **The wedge seams still render as ragged, stippled dotted lines** in all three
unwrapped strips - a stitched, non-clean edge where two faces meet. It is the
only dirty linework in the set and it appears at every seam.

- **The fringe still does not sit flush with the disc in `iso.png`.** Along the
near (lower) edge the disc's square rim face clearly stands above the blades,
and the blades pass behind and below it; there is a visible step at every
junction rather than a continuous surface. Two blades at about 7-8 o'clock are
half swallowed by the disc's side wall.

- **The body silhouette in `top.png` is measurably elliptical, not circular** -
taller than wide. Since the blades are specified at a constant radius fraction,
an elliptical body means their tips reach different absolute distances at
different clock positions, and you can see it: the blades at 3 and 9 o'clock
stand off much further than those at 12 and 6.

- **Spacing around the assembled disc is visibly uneven** even though the
unwrapped pitch is regular - at 10-11 o'clock three blades crowd and overlap,
at 2 o'clock and 4 o'clock a blade nearly disappears behind the rim. That is
the ellipse and the projection fighting each other, and it makes the fringe
look accidental at the very places a viewer looks first.

- **Two blades in `top.png` contribute nothing.** The ones at roughly 2 o'clock
and 4 o'clock show as small orange nubs barely clearing the edge. They are
geometry that is present, costs material, and reads as a chip in the rim rather
than as a ray.

## Round 3 - the requirement comparison, primed

The Wish was revealed. Every requirement, positive and negative, was put
separately, and the critic was asked to answer each against its own recorded
observations from rounds 1 and 2 rather than by looking again. Measured values
were supplied as data, with the instruction to say if any of them contradicted
what it had actually seen. Nothing below is a naive read.

Verdicts: requirements 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 12 **MATCH**; requirement
11 is **NOT VISUALLY DETERMINABLE**; negative requirements 13 to 19 record **no
violation**. Requirement 2 was first answered DOES NOT MATCH against the wrong
baseline - the fault of the question, not the answer - and was re-answered
**MATCHES** once the correct baseline was supplied. All seven of round 2's new
findings were re-judged **non-blocking**, five of them retracted outright.

Selected verdicts, verbatim.

**1. Thirty-two flames - MATCHES.** "I counted eleven silhouettes per 120 degree
unwrapped strip ... giving 'about 32-33 around the full circle, allowing one
double-count at a strip seam.' 32 is inside that. Note for the record that my
first read said 8 per strip / 24 total; I retracted that in the second read as
an undercount, and the measured 32 confirms the retraction was the right call."

**4. Separate tongues, no fused skirt - MATCHES.** "Second read, verbatim:
'there is bare background between every neighbouring pair, and the background
reaches all the way down to the rim line in each gap - the fringe is fully
separated when you look at it flat.' My competing observation of blades
'overlapping and closing the gap' at 10-11 and 5 o'clock in top.png is a
projection effect; with the flat strip as the unambiguous evidence and the
measured 7.275 mm minimum air gap, I take the flat strip as correct."

**5. Lengths span at least 4 to 1; tall ones about one in four, uneven, never
every fourth - MATCHES.** "I recorded heights from about 40 px to about 160 px
- 4:1 exactly - and a rhythm of 'medium / stub / medium / tall / medium / stub
/ tall / medium / stub / medium.' The talls fall at positions 4 and 7 of 10 in
the top strip and 3 and 6 of 11 in the third strip: roughly one in four, at
irregular intervals, and never on a four-beat."

**6. Width at half length at least 45 per cent of root - MATCHES.** "Everything
I described is a broad triangular fin with a fat mid-section ... tips reached
by a long continuous taper, not by a shoulder-then-stalk. Nothing in either
read resembles a filament." The critic added a caveat - that it could not
corroborate the *previous* build's filaments from its own record - and then
withdrew that caveat in the item 2 re-answer below, having been shown the old
strip.

**8. Every tip blunt and rounded; nothing terminating in a point - MATCHES.**
"Both reads independently. First read: 'they end in points, but not sharp
points: every one terminates in a small rounded tip, like a blunted claw.'
Second read: 'Every one ends in a single rounded tip - a blunted point, never a
sharp spike, never a square end, never a fork.' No exceptions in any of the
four views."

**9. At least one flame in three leans the opposite way, runs of one to three,
never strictly alternating, no repeating pattern - MATCHES.** "My recorded
sequences, unprompted and before I knew this was a requirement - top strip:
right, straight, left, right, left, straight, left, right, straight, left.
Third strip: straight, left, right, right, left, left, straight, straight,
left, left, right. That gives runs of one to three, no strict alternation, and
no correspondence between the two strips. The minority direction runs around 40
per cent of each strip, consistent with 14 of 32. My 'straight' entries are
small-sign cases I could not resolve at that pixel scale, not a third
handedness."

**10. The mixed handedness is visible in the unwrapped strips - MATCHES.**
"This is precisely the evidence I gave: in the third strip, two tall blades of
near-identical height at x = 355-440 and x = 790-860 with exactly mirrored
silhouettes ... In an unwrapped strip vertical *is* radial, so that comparison
needs no geometry and admits no other reading."

**11. Turn 37-44 degrees, about 45 per cent in the inner half - NOT VISUALLY
DETERMINABLE.** "My rim-detail figures ... are chord deviations of the flame
axis from its own radius, not root-to-tip turn, and they sit at roughly half
the stated total - which is what you would expect if the turn accumulates along
the blade with the tip deviating further than the chord. So: consistent, not
confirming."

**13-17, 19. No violation.** On 14: "Nothing in any of the four views encloses
background. Every flame is a simple tongue attached along one root; the only
other closed outline on the part is the hub circle, which is a flat face, not a
loop." On 16: "Measured valleys of roughly 50-85 px against roots of 60-95 px:
every gap is at or below root width." On 17: "All 32 tips rounded, cap size
visibly constant across the 4:1 length range, no widened or bulbed tip and no
needle in any view."

### The seven round-2 findings, re-judged

**(a) Mixed rake reads as noise rather than design - NON-BLOCKING; retracted
outright.** "This is requirements 9, 10 and 11. I called the object's principal
intended feature a defect because I was reading it as a mechanism. It is the
strongest evidence in the set that the edge is fire and not gear teeth."

**(b) 33 blades against 24 wedges - NON-BLOCKING; retracted.** "32 against 24
cannot register and the brief fixes both counts ... No contradiction with your
data: about 32-33 observed, 32 measured."

**(c) Ragged stippled wedge seams in the strips - NON-BLOCKING; withdrawn.**
"The stair-stepping appears *only* in rim-unwrapped.png and nowhere in top.png,
iso.png or rim-detail, which is the signature of a resampling artifact rather
than of coincident faces in the solid. A 0.70 mm margin nearest-neighbour
resampled at 1.06x produces precisely that dithered edge."

**(d) Fringe not flush with the disc - NON-BLOCKING; retracted.** "Requirement
12 specifies a 5.4 mm root height with the flame top a single plane falling to
a lower tip, so the step is the design."

**(e) Body silhouette elliptical - NON-BLOCKING; retracted.** "My own second
read fitted a circle through three points on the *tile* arc in rim-detail and
it closed cleanly at R = 856 px, which is positive evidence for a circular body
and which I failed to weigh against my impression from top.png. I was measuring
the outer flame silhouette and calling it the disc."

**(f) Blades overlapping at 10-11 and 5 o'clock - NON-BLOCKING; contradiction
accepted.** "32 separate connected regions with a 7.275 mm minimum air gap
cannot be reconciled with blades touching in plan ... The flat strips, where I
saw clean separation everywhere, are the reliable view and I said so at the
time."

**(g) The two shortest flames read as chips in the rim - NON-BLOCKING, not
fully withdrawn.** "Requirement 5 mandates a 4:1 span, so the shortest flames
are *supposed* to be nubs and their existence is correct. What I stand by is
the narrower aesthetic point: at the very bottom of that range ... the shortest
flames read as damage to the rim rather than as short flames." Recorded and not
acted on: the 4 to 1 span and the flame rises are values the Wish carries over
explicitly and forbids this round from changing.

The critic also volunteered three corrections against its own first read: that
the four hub ticks are "one congruent feature at exactly 90.0 degree spacing,
rotated a few degrees as a rigid set ... and I should have seen the rotation
instead of inferring four sloppy features"; that its claim counters straddle
tile boundaries "is contradicted by 0.0 mm2 off-tile and is explained by
extruded counters overhanging their own tile in a three-quarter view"; and the
signature-panel question resolved at item 18 below.

## Round 4 - the three items held open, resolved

Two of these were faults in the questions put to the critic, not in its
answers, and they are recorded as such.

**Item 2 - re-answered MATCHES.** The first question asked whether the roots
were "roughly twice as wide as the build before". The critic reasonably read
"the build before" as its own round-1 images - two builds of the same revision,
both with 32 flames, whose roots are identical by design - and refused to
certify a change it had not seen. It was then shown the actual 48-flame build
at the same arc, window and scale, and told that the Wish's own figures are
5.47 to 8.2 mm, which is 1.5x rather than 2x.

"Judging the two lower bands at the same scale, the difference is obvious and
goes the right way. The old band (Emberfan) carries about sixteen tongues
across the 1600 px third - consistent with 48 - at a pitch I read as roughly
100 px. The current band (Sunflare) carries eleven at a pitch of roughly 145
px. Those two pitch readings land almost exactly on your 10.433 mm and 15.649
mm, which is useful because it independently fixes the scale of the strip at
about 0.104 mm/px ... Against that scale the old roots read as slender ... and
the new roots read as roughly half again as wide. I would not have called it
2x; 1.5x is what it looks like. Your corrected numbers are consistent with the
pictures."

"The shape difference is larger than the width difference, and matters more.
The old tongues lose width almost immediately above the root and then run out
as long, near-parallel slivers with a lot of dead air around each one - exactly
the 'filament' characterisation I said in item 6 I could not corroborate from
my own record. I can corroborate it now: that is what the Emberfan band does,
and it is not what the current band does. The current tongues keep a full belly
to mid-height before tapering, which is what makes them read as flame rather
than as bristle."

Asked whether the build is closer to or further from the concept than the one
it corrects, the critic said closer on balance, and named what it is closer on
- root width, body fullness, mixed handedness and length spread - and what it
is further on:

"**count**. I count roughly sixteen licks per third in the concept band, i.e.
something in the mid-to-high forties around the ring. The 48-flame build
matched the concept's density almost exactly; 32 is a visible thinning ... If
fidelity to the concept were the only criterion, 48 was the better number."

And three things it says neither build captures: "the tips recurve in the
concept - they hook over past vertical into a claw with a concave leading edge,
and several carry a secondary shoulder lobe. Both builds use a simple
single-curvature fin with a blunt cap; the hook is simply absent, and it is the
single most characteristic thing about the hand-made original." Second, the
concept's flames "rise from a continuous undulating collar ... which the brief
deliberately forbids, so that departure is a decision rather than a miss".
Third, "the concept licks carry engraved interior veins and vary in silhouette
from one to the next; both builds are blank flat shapes generated by scaling
and shearing one profile."

None of these three is acted on and none is a defect against this Wish. The
count is fixed at 32 by the Wish. The fused collar is forbidden by the Wish.
The recurved hook and the interior veins would change the approved flame curve
and `CORONA_TURN_EXP`, which the Wish carries over and forbids changing. They
are recorded here as the honest remaining distance to the concept, for whoever
writes the next brief.

**Item 18 - resolved, flag withdrawn.** The critic had held, through two
rounds, that a counter on the hub in the third signature panel was a floating
or misplaced part, and explicitly refused to drop it quietly. Shown that the
three panels are three positions of the game and that the parked counter's base
plane is at Z 6.200 - exactly the bar's top face - at radius 4.0 mm inside a
24.0 mm bar with the ticks out at 17.0 to 23.4 mm:

"Resolved. Flag withdrawn. Three panels as setup / before capture / after
capture makes sense of everything I saw, including the thing I reported
alongside it - that 'piece counts and positions vary slightly' between panels -
which was me treating three game states as three inconsistent renders of one
state. 30 counters in each panel, base plane at Z 6.200 on the bar's top face
... none of that leaves room for a floating or interpenetrating part."

It then raised a separate, narrower defect, which was acted on:

"One thing still looks wrong to me, and it is a communication defect rather
than a geometry defect ... I looked at that sheet cold and read the bar-parked
counter as a dropped piece - the most obvious error in the set, I said so, and
I held the flag through two rounds. Nothing in the image distinguishes the hub
from a named location ... If the point of the third panel is to show a capture,
it currently fails on exactly the viewer it needs to convince. That is worth a
fix in the sheet, not in the solid."

**Item 19 - no violation, and now determinable.** The critic had read the
diagnostic strip's caption "radius 0.86 to 1.22 of the disc" as meaning the
flames are rooted at 0.86 and buried under the disc from there, and had used
that to explain away the variation in visible root width. That caption names
only the crop window; the flames are rooted at exactly 1.00 of the disc radius
and the measured minimum radius of any flame material is 79.7000 mm.

"Yes, it changes both, and I withdraw the claim ... That was inference from
your caption, not from the pixels, and it was wrong ... I should have noticed
that the renders never actually supported burial - in rim-detail and in both
comparison bands the tongues meet the tile-end arc cleanly and no flame
silhouette crosses inside it."

It also volunteered the limit of its own pixel measurements: "my absolute
widths should not be trusted to better than about plus or minus 20 per cent; my
*ratios* - pitch to root, old to new - held up against your numbers and are the
part worth relying on." And on item 19 itself: "With the root at exactly 1.00
that hedge is unnecessary - there is no radius band in which hidden flame
material could exist - and the renders positively agree ... Item 19: no
violation, and now determinable rather than merely unrefuted."

## Round 5 - the signature sheet, primed

Round 4 closed the geometry questions but left one live defect, raised by the
critic itself: the capture panel failed on the viewer it needed to convince.
The sheet was revised twice in response. Only `snap/signature.png` changed:
the three exact-state renders inside it, the geometry, and all twelve other
canonical images stayed byte-identical, so rounds 1 and 2 stand untouched.

First pass, after captions were added: **"Yes as a sheet, no as an image."**

"What carries it is entirely the text ... panel 3's 'It is not lost: it sits on
the bar top and must re-enter before its owner moves again' pre-empts my exact
blind misreading almost word for word. Read the captions and the position is
unambiguous. What still fails is the picture. Cover the captions and panel 3 is
indistinguishable in kind from my first cold read: the hub is drawn identically
in all three panels, nothing marks it as a named location rather than as
decoration, and a lone counter sitting on it still reads as a piece that fell
off ... So the sheet now states the capture rather than showing it."

It listed six non-blocking sheet defects. Four were acted on - caption type
raised to 30 px titles over wrapped 24 px body, the centre bar ringed and
labelled in panel 3, the point numbering disambiguated to "the dark side's
point 1", and panel 3's title brought back into the pattern of 1 and 2. Two
were not, and the reasons were given to the critic rather than left for it to
find:

- **Marking the bar on the object itself.** The critic is right that it belongs
  there and would help a real player mid-game. This correction may change only
  the ring of flames; the hub, the bar and its four ticks are carried over
  untouched and nothing new may be cut into them.
- **Panel 2 depicting imminence.** A static exact-state render cannot show a
  pending roll without drawing product that does not exist. The critic's reply:
  "I accept your reason ... and I would rather have that gap than a fictional
  die on the board."

Second pass, on the revised sheet:

"**Yes.** With the text hidden, panel 3 is now the only panel that has a marked
region on it, and the marking does three things at once: the ring is *closed*,
so it reads as an enclosure rather than as decoration; the counter sits inside
it rather than beside it, so the enclosure is visibly doing something; and the
red is the only colour on the sheet outside the product's three oranges, cream
and brown, so it reads unmistakably as annotation laid over the object rather
than as a feature engraved into it. The words THE CENTRE BAR then name it in
the picture. That is enough - I would no longer call that counter dropped,
because a dropped piece does not get a circle drawn round it."

**"No. Nothing on this sheet is blocking, and nothing in the geometry ever
was."**

Its remaining wants, recorded and not acted on: a knock-out box behind the red
label, which sits on ochre tiles at the sheet's weakest contrast; a matching
ring or ghost on the vacated point so panels 2 and 3 can be compared without
counting counters; and panel 2's imminence. On the set's use of "drop" for a
counter: "it no longer trips, and I would keep it. Caption 1 now defines it in
its first six words ... The one place it still rubs is 'a cream drop is about
to land' - 'drop' and 'land' stack the same falling metaphor ... That is a
preference, not a defect."

## The critic's own summary of the whole review

"**Unprimed reads (1 and 2).** Read blind, the object read as a sun-themed
circular board with 24 radial lanes, a central hub and a fringe of flames,
carrying two sets of teardrop counters. Both unprimed reads land on SUN over
WHEEL, the second more strongly than the first, because mixed flame rake
positively rules out any mechanical reading."

"**The one property that changed at the rim, second read.** My first read
claimed a uniform counterclockwise rake, 'like a pinwheel'. That was retracted
under measurement ... Mixed handedness confirmed. The first read's error was
methodological - I judged rake in image space, which is only valid at 12
o'clock."

"**Requirements check (items 1-20).** All positive requirements MATCH or are
consistent where not directly measurable, except item 2, which I initially
refused as DOES NOT MATCH. That refusal was correct on its own terms and was
later resolved: the baseline I had been given was the wrong build. Against the
true 48-flame predecessor, seen at matched scale, the 1.5x root widening and
the shift from filament to full-bodied flame are both plainly visible, and my
measured pitch readings (~100 px then, ~145 px now) independently confirm the
stated 10.433 and 15.649 mm. No negative requirement is violated."

"**Retractions I owe the record.** Four of my seven 'looks wrong' items were my
errors, not the object's: the elliptical body (my own circle fit in rim-detail
actually supported a circular disc), counters straddling tiles (extrusion
overhang in a three-quarter view), asymmetric hub ticks (one congruent cross
rotated a few degrees), and buried flame roots (I inferred burial from a
caption, not from pixels - flames are rooted at exactly 1.00). The ragged strip
seams were a resampling artifact of a diagnostic image. My root-width estimates
should not be trusted better than plus or minus 20 per cent; my ratios held."

"**What survived as real.** One defect survived all five passes and was fixed:
the capture panel failed on the viewer it needed to convince. I misread the
bar-parked counter as a dropped part blind, and held that flag through two
rounds. It is now closed - the ring plus label carries the reading with the
captions covered."

"**Open for the next brief, not this one.** The bar is still unmarked on the
object itself; this correction was only permitted to touch the flames. And on
fidelity to the hand-made concept: the current build is closer in root width,
body fullness, handedness and length spread, but 32 flames is a visible
thinning against the concept's roughly 48, and neither build captures the
concept's recurved hooked tips, its fused undulating collar, or its engraved
interior veins. The rim still reads as a repeated part where the concept reads
as drawn."

## What this review did and did not establish

It established, from images alone and twice without any brief, that the object
reads as a sun and its rim as fire rather than as a wheel - the single defect
this Wish exists to correct - and that the mixed handedness is visible in the
unwrapped strips, which is acceptance clause 1. It drove two repairs: the
handedness walk constant, and the signature sheet.

It did not, and could not, establish any millimetre value, any print outcome,
any physical handling, tile retention, stack stability, durability or human
response. Those come from the deterministic audits in `cad/measure/` or are not
claimed at all.
