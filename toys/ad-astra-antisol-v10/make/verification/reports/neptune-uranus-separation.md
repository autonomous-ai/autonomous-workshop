# Neptune against Uranus at the product's own frame

Ranks 5 and 6, Ø21.89 and Ø22.02 mm -- 0.13 mm apart in a set whose
ranks are told apart by size. Measured on the canonical side-by-side
renders at the hero frame (azimuth -55, elevation 22), one per army.

This revision changed Neptune's surface, so this pair is asked again
on this run's own renders rather than inherited from the archive.

## The size cue

| | Neptune | Uranus | apart |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| rank | 5 | 6 | 1 |
| globe diameter mm | 21.89 | 22.02 | 0.13 |
| piece height mm | 24.89 | 25.98 | 1.09 |
| obliquity deg | 28.32 | 97.77 | 69.45 |

0.13 mm is 0.6 per cent of Neptune's own diameter and less than
one 0.40 mm nozzle. **Size cannot separate these two pieces**,
and it is the only pair in the ladder of which that is true: the
next closest, Venus and Earth, are 0.17 mm apart.

## The colour cue

| | sealed hex | luma of 255 |
|---|---|---:|
| Neptune globe `blue` | #004EA8 | 67.9 |
| Uranus globe `cyan` | #00FFFF | 200.8 |

Sealed, the two separate **132.9** of 255 luma levels, which is
four times the 9.4 this project accepted as its narrowest on
Saturn, and they separate in hue as well.

As rendered, measured on the pixels of the two globes themselves:

| army | Neptune mean RGB | Uranus mean RGB | luma apart | Neptune marked | Uranus marked |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sol | 18,129,175 | 16,203,203 | 54.5 | 10.1% | 9.5% |
| Anti-Sol | 20,125,169 | 19,198,198 | 54.0 | 14.4% | 13.2% |

The `marked` columns are the share of each ball that is not its
own globe colour, which is the surface cue reduced to one number.
Neptune's three bands cover 10.1 to 14.4 per cent of its visible
face; Uranus's markings cover 9.5 to 13.2 per cent of its.

## The silhouette cue

| | Neptune | Uranus |
|---|---|---|
| silhouette | a bare sphere on a disc | a sphere with an upright hoop |
| piece height mm | 24.89 | 25.98 |
| widest, ring included | Ø21.89 | Ø24.02 |

Uranus carries a ring and carries it standing up: its equatorial
plane is 7.77 degrees past vertical, so the hoop is a band over
the globe rather than a brim beside it. Against Neptune's bare
sphere that is a difference a reader sees before any colour
arrives. **The pair separates on silhouette.**

## The surface cue

| world | what it wears |
|---|---|
| Neptune | three closed white latitude bands, 0.96, 0.76 and 0.57 mm wide, running the whole way round the globe, plus one dark_gray oval 5.35 by 2.67 mm south of the equator |
| Uranus | a broad soft beige polar hood at each pole, boundary at latitude 60, and an upright cyan ring standing pole-over-pole |

These are not the same KIND of marking, which is the cue a reader
uses once they are close enough for paint. **The pair separates on
surface.**

One honest caveat about Neptune's half of it, and it is the whole
subject of this revision: a closed latitude band is the most
generic marking a sphere can carry. It separates Neptune from these
two worlds because neither of them wears bands -- but it is the
reason the blind review of this build is asked, in so many words,
whether any piece in the set reads as a beach ball, and the answer
it gives is recorded in `snap/SIGNATURE-REVIEW.json` beside the
owner's decision rather than argued with here.
