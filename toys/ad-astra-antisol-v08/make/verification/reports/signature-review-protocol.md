# The independent blind review of this revision

`snap/SIGNATURE-REVIEW.json` is the sealed record and its fields are length-bounded.
This is the same account at full length, so that nothing had to be cut to fit a field.

## Who reviewed it

Two independent native critics, in four review rounds, neither given the Wish, the concept, the product name or any file but the nine canonical images. Critic A took rounds 1 to 3 on three successive builds. The Manager's session was then restarted by the host and critic A's transcript did not survive it, so round 4 -- the final review, on the exact shipped images -- was taken by critic B, which had never seen this product. That is disclosed rather than hidden: it cost the continuity the protocol prefers and bought a genuinely unprimed reader for the round that decides, which is the round where priming does the most damage. Critic A had by round 3 told us itself that it could no longer diff builds reliably ('I cannot find a single difference ... I am reporting that plainly rather than manufacturing a change') on files that were then proved byte-for-byte current by rebuilding them from source.

## What happened, round by round

PROTOCOL. Four review rounds, three focused repairs, two critics, neither ever given the Wish, the concept, the product name, or any file other than the nine canonical images; each was told not to read source, README, measurements or JSON, and each confirmed it had not. Round 1 failed: critic A's first words for the streaks were 'painted stripes', 'slashes', 'decals' -- and the correction's own stated failure test is the word 'stripe' or 'beach ball' appearing in the blind text. REPAIR 1: the streaks were given a skewed taper and a bowed centreline, their tilts widened to 3-12 degrees with alternating signs, and their eight longitudes re-solved against all four cameras at once for the widest longitude coverage the facing floor allows. Round 2: the streaks became 'curved tapered sweeps ... they now sit on the ball instead of lying on top of it'; 'stripe' and 'beach ball' were gone and did not return. REPAIR 2: the dark spot's ring went from 22 to 40 vertices and the streak sampling from 0.80 to 0.60 mm per edge, after a faceting report. REPAIR 3: the companion was redrawn as a crescent parallel to the spot's own rim at constant clearance, after it was read as a fragment twice. The Manager's session was then restarted by the host; critic A's transcript did not survive, and round 4 was taken by critic B on the exact shipped images, disclosed above. THREE OF CRITIC B'S FOUR BLOCKING FINDINGS WERE DISPROVED BY MEASUREMENT, NOT BY ARGUMENT, and the fourth is carried in `largest_risk` rather than dismissed: (i) 'the markings are solid raised lenses that break the ball's silhouette' -- every marking point measures at exactly the globe radius, nothing proud to 1e-6 mm, and the critic withdrew the finding in writing; (ii) 'the dark spot is a coarse low-segment polygon' -- worst chord error 0.0305 mm, 8 per cent of a nozzle, and a four-times magnification of the same frame shows a smooth ellipse; (iii) 'possibly more than eight streaks' and 'possible longitude bunching' -- exactly eight, at eight distinct longitudes spanning 133 degrees. Critic B raised the two counts itself as checks against the model rather than as findings, and had already withdrawn two other claims of the same kind when measured. ONE FINDING THE CRITIC RAISED IS NOT A DEFECT AND IS RECORDED AS A REQUIREMENT CONFLICT, in its own framing: the far hemisphere of both Neptunes is bare. 'It is the arithmetic consequence of requirement 2 demanding markings face the camera ... put every streak where the two photographed frames can see it and the opposite side is necessarily empty.' The eight streaks and the spot cover 211 of 360 degrees of longitude, which is close to the most the facing floor permits, and `snap/worlds/neptune-sol-opposite.png` is that picture -- which is also the proof of the correction's central negative requirement, because a closed band would have been there in full. GEOMETRY IS FROZEN AS REVIEWED: no source changed after round 4. Print status is sealed as digitally-verified-not-print-ready with `print_ready_claim: false` and an empty print-gate map, so nothing here is offered as a print-readiness claim, even though every printable part cleared the 0.80 mm wall gate and the 45-degree overhang gate at a 0.4 mm nozzle in its own round. Motion is unverified, never passed: the run-root MAKE-OPTIONS.json sets check_motion false, and nothing in the set moves against anything else in any case.

## The largest risk, in the critic's own words

The critic's, in its own words and not softened. Asked the diagnostic question cold -- does the pale mark beside the dark spot read as a second, brighter cloud belonging to it, or as a stray fragment -- critic B answered: 'It does not read as a companion bright cloud belonging to the spot; it reads as a stray fragment, a detached chip of the same slash material that happened to land nearby.' Critic A said the same thing of two earlier and quite different versions of it. Every stated clause of that requirement is met and measured -- white, north of the spot, half its length, 0.61 mm clear of it, a nozzle and a half -- but the sentence explaining why the correction wants it, that it is what stops the spot reading as a smudge, is not achieved in an independent reader's eye. Three repairs were spent on it (a compact oval, then a long wisp, then a crescent drawn parallel to the spot's own rim at constant clearance) and none of them moved the reading. WHAT I WOULD CHANGE, which is the sentence the owner acts on: the obstacle is contrast, not placement. `dark_gray` #6F6E6D on `blue` #004EA8 separates 42 of 255 luma levels, so the spot itself reads as a grey smudge -- critic B's word -- and a white mark beside a smudge has nothing to belong to. The correction fixes the spot's filament as `dark_gray`, so this run could not test the alternative. Give Neptune `black` for the spot, or a darker globe, and the companion has an object to be the bright edge of. Second, smaller risk, and the same root: critic B's one-line verdict on the streaks was 'Paint -- I'd hand it over saying the white marks look like slashes of paint or stuck-on decals, not weather.' That is the owner's own 1.5 mm width decision working exactly as written -- six to twelve times the reference cloud width, chosen for legibility at the set's viewing scale -- combined with a flush inlay's hard edge, which no world in this set can soften. It is reported, not repaired.

## The two claims that measurement overturned

Both were made from the rendered frames and both were answered by measuring the
solids rather than by arguing. `measure/neptune-flush.md` is that measurement: every
point of every marking body on both armies lies at 10.945000 mm from the globe centre
against a globe radius of 10.945000 mm, and the dark spot's ring departs from the true
ellipse by at most 0.0305 mm, eight per cent of one nozzle. Shown the first of those,
critic B withdrew two verdicts in writing: 'I asserted geometry from a silhouette, and
a measurement of the actual solids beats that.' It then diagnosed the cause itself --
a coincident-surface artefact of a renderer that does not antialias -- and made the one
recommendation this run could not act on without invalidating its own review: that the
review renders would be better evidence with a finer tessellation on the inlays,
'purely so the pictures stop lying'. That is recorded here for the next run rather than
done in this one, because any change after the fourth review would have invalidated it.
