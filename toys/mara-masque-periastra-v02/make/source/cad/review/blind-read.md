## A. What the part in images 1 and 2 reads as

**First read, before thinking:** a computer mouse. A teardrop dome, low and pointed at one end,
swelling to a tall rounded hump at the other, with a curved channel running across the top and a
small round knob sitting in that channel — that is a mouse with a scroll wheel between two
buttons. The side view (image 2) reinforces it: a smooth asymmetric blob on a thin flat base,
with a short stub poking out of the nose at about 45 degrees, which reads like a cable leaving
the front of the mouse.

**Second read:** a squat domed lid or cloche with a spout — a kettle or teapot lid, or a covered
butter dish. The angled cylinder at the narrow end is unmistakably spout-like in the elevation
view.

**Recognisable structure on or in it:** yes. A single broad, deeply sunk **crescent-shaped
channel** is gouged across the dome. It is thin at both ends, fat in the middle, and sweeps from
the upper right of the dome down to the lower left. It cuts the whole way through the shell, so
the dome is visually split into two separate lobes joined only near the nose and via the base
plate. Standing inside that crescent channel is a **short cylindrical peg** with a flat circular
end. In the 45-degree view (image 1) the peg reads as upright; in the elevation (image 2) the
same peg is clearly angled, emerging from the nose of the dome and pointing up and outward. There
is also a thin vertical fin or knife-wall running along one side of the channel down to the base
plate.

So: a crescent moon sunk into a dome, with a peg in it. The crescent is legible as a crescent from
the 45-degree view and from the iso view. From the straight-on elevation (image 2) the crescent
disappears entirely and only the peg is visible.

## B. As a plain black silhouette

As a flat black shape, image 2's part reads as **a computer mouse** — a low teardrop blister with
a small stub off the nose. Secondary readings, in order of how quickly they arrive: a squat teapot
or kettle (stub = spout), a bicycle helmet, a limpet or snail shell, a whale's head. It does **not**
read as a crescent, a sun, a moon, or anything celestial in silhouette — the crescent is an
interior cut and contributes nothing to the outline. The silhouette also does not read as a lid or
a box; the base plate reads as a thin tray the blob is sitting on, not as part of the same object.

## C. The two small discs (images 5-8, plus plan views 9-10)

**Disc 1 (images 5, 6, 9) — orange/tan.**
- Outline: a clean circle. The plan view (image 9) shows a perfect circle with a chamfered or
  drafted rim ring. No tail, tab, notch, slot, spur or flat anywhere on the circumference.
- Mark: a **sunburst / rosette** cut into the face — eight tapered wedge rays arranged evenly
  around a central round pocket. Alternate rays read slightly differently (four broad flat-ended
  wedges at roughly the 3, 6, 9, 12 positions, four sharper arrowhead wedges on the diagonals),
  which may just be facet shading. The central pocket is a circular depression; because only its
  far wall is lit, it renders as a bright crescent inside the circle.
- What it depicts: **the sun** — a disc with rays.

**Disc 2 (images 7, 8, 10) — pale cream/ivory.**
- Outline: also a clean circle. Plan view (image 10) is a perfect circle with a chamfered rim. No
  tail, tab, notch, slot or spur.
- Mark: a single **crescent-shaped groove**, thick near the top right, thinning to a point at the
  lower left, with a sharp horn at each end. Sunk, not raised.
- What it depicts: **a crescent moon.**

**Important observation on images 9 and 10:** both plan views are completely blank. Seen straight
down, neither the sun rosette nor the crescent is visible at all — just a flat circle with a rim
ring. Either these plan renders are of the unmarked underside, or the marks vanish under
straight-down flat lighting. I cannot tell which from the images. If these are the marked faces
seen from the player's own viewpoint, the marks are not doing their job.

## D. Comparing 5 with 6, and 7 with 8

**Sun disc (5 vs 6):** the geometry that differs is a **concentric crown ring**. Image 5 shows the
sun rosette cut into a plain flat face with a simple rim. Image 6 shows the same face with an
extra annular groove cut just inside the rim, leaving a raised circular plateau in the middle that
carries the rosette. The sunk mark itself — the eight-ray sun with the round centre pocket — is
**identical** on both; only the crown ring is added.

**Moon disc (7 vs 8):** here the two faces are **not** consistent with the sun's treatment. Image 7
shows a small, clearly readable crescent, roughly half the disc diameter, sitting in the middle of
a flat face. Image 8 shows no small crescent at all. Instead there is one large, fat, near-full
annular groove running almost all the way round just inside the rim, with a narrow break on the
right side, enclosing a raised blank plateau. The crescent has effectively been inflated until it
is the crown ring. There is no distinct crescent left on that face.

**Net answer:** on the sun disc, the mark is the same on both faces and the king face just gains a
ring. On the moon disc, the mark is genuinely different between the two faces — one face has a
small crescent, the other has a big broken ring that only barely reads as a crescent and reads
much more readily as a "C" or as a plain crown ring. The two pieces therefore do not follow the
same rule, and the moon's king face loses its identity: a blank plateau inside a ring could belong
to either side.

## E. Image 11, the populated board at a distance

At that viewing distance the two sides are told apart **almost entirely by colour** — a warm
orange/terracotta group in the near-left half and a pale cream group in the far-right half. That
separation is immediate and unambiguous.

The **marks** are a different story:

- The sun mark still works. On the orange counters you can clearly see a dark speckled rosette —
  a small flower or cog. It is not readable as "sun" specifically at that size, but it is clearly
  a mark, and clearly a many-pointed one.
- The moon mark does not work. On the cream counters the crescent reads as a thin pale arc hugging
  the top-left of each disc, which is indistinguishable from an ordinary lighting highlight on a
  chamfered rim. Several of the cream counters look simply blank. If the colours were stripped out
  I do not believe I could name the cream pieces from this render at all.

So: **yes, the marks are ambiguous — specifically the moon's.** If the two materials were similar
in tone (both light woods, both natural PLA, a mono-colour print), or in low light, the crescent
would be read as "no mark" and the sun would be the only side with a device. That is a real
confusion risk: a blank-looking disc reads as the unmarked/neutral piece, not as "the other
player". The crescent needs to be deeper, larger, or given a second contrasting element.

A secondary risk: the sun's central round pocket renders as a bright crescent inside its ray ring
(clearest in image 5). At small scale that centre highlight is itself crescent-shaped, so the sun
counter is carrying a crescent in the middle of it. Not fatal, but it muddies the distinction.

I also could not tell from image 11 whether any piece is a "crowned"/ringed variant. All pieces on
the board appear to be the plain-faced versions; none shows the concentric ring from images 6 and 8.

## F. Images 3, 4 and 12 — what I see, and what is stored in what

**Image 3 (iso).** A shallow square grey tray or box with low walls and four small upstanding
posts at its corners. Sitting on top of the tray is a flat square teal plate carrying the big
teardrop dome from images 1 and 2 — i.e. the dome part is a **lid**. Through the gap between the
tray rim and the underside of the teal lid plate you can see a row of four orange discs lying flat
inside the tray. So yes: **the counters are stored inside the tray, and the domed plate closes
over them.**

**Image 4 (signature).** Two arrangements side by side. Left: the closed assembly — lid seated on
the tray, orange counters just visible in the side gap. Right: the same thing exploded — the
domed lid floating above, and below it the open tray with its full contents on show. The tray
floor is a playing surface scored with horizontal zigzag/chevron lines, and 24 counters are laid
out on it: 12 cream in the upper half and 12 orange/tan in the lower half, staggered like a
draughts/checkers opening setup. This image is doing the job of a hero/packshot: closed object and
its contents in one frame.

**Image 12 (closed elevation).** The closed package seen almost edge-on. Grey tray at the bottom,
teal dome lid on top, the angled spout/peg sticking out to the upper left. Through the horizontal
slot between tray rim and lid plate you can see the stored counters in profile: three orange at
the left, three cream at the right, with an empty run in the middle.

**Storage summary:** counters inside the tray, tray closed by the domed lid. The **interior volume
of the dome is not used for anything** — nothing is stored inside the dome itself; the counters
all lie in the thin layer below the lid plate. That is a large empty bubble sitting above a very
shallow storage slot.

## G. Things that look wrong, broken, floating, or out of proportion

Being deliberately picky:

1. **The dome dwarfs the box it closes.** In images 3, 4 and 12 the lid is roughly two to three
   times the height of the whole storage tray, and the storage cavity is a thin sliver. The
   proportion is inverted — a huge empty dome sitting on a wafer-thin box.

2. **The dome's interior volume is wasted.** Nothing lives inside it (image 12 shows the counters
   are all below the lid plate). Either the dome should be shallower or it should hold something.

3. **The angled peg/spout is cantilevered and unsupported.** In images 2 and 12 it projects from
   the nose of the dome at roughly 45 degrees into open air, with its far end floating well above
   the base plate. As a printed feature that is an unsupported overhang coming off a curved
   surface; as a handled feature it is a snap-off risk.

4. **The crescent channel cuts the dome fully in two.** In images 1 and 3 the channel runs right
   down through the shell to the base plate, so the two lobes are joined only at the nose and
   through the plate. The right-hand lobe is effectively a large cantilevered shell.

5. **A thin knife-edge fin inside the channel.** Visible in images 1 and 3 — a tall, very thin
   vertical wall running down the side of the crescent slot to the plate, with an odd stepped
   notch at its base. Fragile, and a print-quality problem at that thickness.

6. **The dome is crowded against the edges of its base plate.** In image 2 the dome's skirt spans
   essentially the full width of the plate — near-zero margin at the mid-edges — while the plate's
   corners have large empty margins. Clearance is very uneven. In image 1 the skirt appears to
   reach or slightly cross the plate edge at the lower right; I cannot confirm overhang from a
   projected view, but it is close enough to want checking.

7. **The dome has an undercut skirt.** A continuous dark groove runs round the base of the dome in
   images 2 and 12, with the shell flaring out above it. That is a re-entrant lip — an overhang
   all the way round, and a place for a lid seal to bind.

8. **Mesh/render artefact on the dome.** Both image 2 and image 12 show a small bright triangular
   sliver on the right-hand slope of the dome, unrelated to any other feature. It looks like a
   stray facet or a non-manifold sliver rather than intended geometry.

9. **Visible faceting.** The dome shows obvious polygonal banding in images 1, 2, 3 and 12 —
   tessellation is coarse for a surface this large and this smooth.

10. **The moon king face has lost its mark** (image 8). Covered above in D. The blank raised
    plateau makes the crowned moon ambiguous against the crowned sun.

11. **The two counter types are not treated symmetrically.** Sun keeps its device and gains a ring;
    moon's device mutates into the ring. Inconsistent design rule across a matched pair.

12. **Plan views carry no information** (images 9 and 10). Straight down — which is exactly how a
    seated player sees the board — both discs are featureless circles.

13. **The board's cells are implied, not defined.** In images 4 and 11 the playing surface is
    scored only with zigzag chevron lines; there are no closed cells, no dimples, no recesses.
    Nothing locates a counter, and several counters straddle a zigzag line rather than sitting
    clear of it.

14. **Counters crowded against the frame.** In image 11, the far-right cream counter in the middle
    band and the lone orange counter on the left both sit hard against the tray wall with visibly
    less clearance than any other piece has to its neighbours. Picking them up would be awkward.

15. **Zigzag line runs are inconsistent.** In images 4 and 11 the chevron rows do not all span the
    same width — some stop short of the left or right frame while others run further. It looks
    like a trimming artefact rather than a deliberate border.

16. **Corner posts look inconsistent.** In images 3, 4 and 11 the near/front corner post reads as a
    chunky solid block while the other three read as slim L-brackets with notched tops. Could be
    perspective, but it does not look like four of the same part.

17. **The stored layout leaves a hole.** Image 12 shows orange counters at the left, cream at the
    right, and a run of empty slots through the middle of the storage layer — the packing does not
    use the tray's width efficiently, and loose counters in a half-empty slot will rattle.

18. **The whole dome reads as the wrong object.** Point A stands as a defect note in its own right:
    at a glance, and in silhouette, the lid reads as a computer mouse or a teapot lid rather than
    as anything belonging to the sun/moon theme the counters establish. The crescent is only
    legible from a raking three-quarter angle and is invisible in elevation.

19. **Nothing indicates a grip or an opening action.** From images 3, 4 and 12 I cannot see how the
    lid is meant to be lifted. The peg is the only graspable protrusion and it is angled, thin,
    and on a curved surface — the object gives no visual instruction for opening.

20. **I cannot tell whether the lid actually registers on the tray.** The corner posts stand proud
    of the lid plate in images 3 and 4, which suggests they locate it, but no matching notches or
    pockets are visible in the lid plate, and the lid appears to rest on the tray rim only
    millimetres above the stored counters (image 12). Whether it clips, drops in, or just sits
    there is not readable from these views.
