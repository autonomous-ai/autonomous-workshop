object, because the angle that produced it is no longer photographed. In image 2 a faint "front of
a mouse with a scroll wheel" reading is still briefly available before the observatory reading
takes over, because the tube foreshortens into a roller.

## A. Image 12 — is anything stored inside anything else?

**Yes.** The domed lid sits on a shallow grey tray, and the tray is full of counters. Along the
full width of the box, between the underside of the lid's plate and the tray floor, there is a
continuous row of orange/terracotta discs — I count eight visible across the opening, seated in
what look like moulded seats or between low ribs. They are lying flat, face up.

**Yes, I can see marks on the stored pieces.** Four of the eight visible discs clearly show the
speckled eight-ray rosette on their upper faces — the sun device is readable through the gap at
this near-eye-level angle. The alternating four read as plain, which I take to be a second,
staggered row seen partly behind the ribs rather than unmarked pieces, but I cannot prove that
from this frame.

No cream/ivory counters are visible in image 12. This camera looks along the rows, so only one
colour's row presents to the opening. In earlier rounds a side-on frame showed both colours with
an empty run between them; that gap is not visible here, and the row that is visible looks fully
occupied.

The dome's own interior is still not holding anything — everything stored is in the flat layer
below the lid plate.

## B. Image 8, looked at carefully

**Yes. There is now a mark on the raised middle plateau, separate from the ring round the edge.**

It is a **crescent** — a slim, sharply tapered sunk groove with a horn at each end, sitting in the
middle of the plateau. It depicts a crescent moon. It is unmistakably a second, distinct feature:
the big broken ring runs round the outside of the plateau, and the crescent sits well inside it.

**Yes, there is a clear unbroken flat band between the crescent and the edge of the plateau.** I
traced it right round: above the crescent's upper horn, outboard of its right tip, and below and
left of its tail, there is uninterrupted flat plateau between the crescent and the inner wall of
the ring groove at every point. Nothing touches, nothing merges, nothing breaks out into the ring.
The plateau is no longer blank, and the crescent is no longer swallowed by the crown ring. This is
the single biggest change in this round.

## C. Comparing 7 with 8, and 5 with 6

**Cream disc (7 vs 8): the sunk mark is now the SAME on both faces.** Both carry the identical
slim tapered crescent, same size, same orientation, same position on the face. The only difference
between the two faces is that one adds the annular crown groove around the outside, leaving the
crescent on a raised plateau. Flip the piece and it is still obviously a moon.

**Orange disc (5 vs 6): the sunk mark is the SAME on both faces.** Both carry the identical
eight-ray sunburst with the round central pocket. The only difference is the added annular crown
groove on one face. Flip the piece and it is still obviously a sun.

The two pieces now follow the same rule as each other. That was a blocking defect in rounds 1
through 3 and it is resolved.

## D. Images 9 and 10 — the near-plan views

**Both now show a mark.** They are no longer blank.

**Image 9 (orange, plain face).** I can see the full sun device from near-overhead: eight rays
arranged round a central circle, plus the round centre pocket. It is legible and it reads as a sun
or a stylised flower. But it renders as **hairline outlines only** — thin dark edge strokes with no
filled or shaded interior. The mark reads as a faint line drawing scratched on the face rather
than as a cut shape with depth. Whether that is the near-plan lighting or a genuinely shallow
recess I cannot tell from an image.

**Image 10 (cream, king face).** Much weaker. From this viewpoint I can see two things: a large arc
sweeping across the **lower half only** of the disc — that is the crown ring, and its upper half is
simply not visible — and a thin, almost horizontal line across the middle with a shallow dip in
it, which is the crescent flattened out. Neither reads as what it is. The combination reads as **a
smiley face**: a straight mouth line above a big curved grin. Seen from overhead, which is a
player's own viewpoint, the crowned moon does not say "moon" and does not say "crowned". This is a
real and visible legibility gap, and it is the one place the crescent still fails.

**Outlines.** Both discs are perfect circles. I looked round the whole circumference of each: no
tail, no tab, no notch, no slot, no spur, no flat. Image 9 shows a clean full rim band; image 10
shows a clean circle with the two-tier king step visible as an offset silhouette at the bottom.

Presentation note: images 9 and 10 are shot at noticeably different tilts and scales, and of
different faces (one plain, one king), so they cannot be compared side by side as a matched pair.

## E. Image 11 at a distance

**Yes, reliably, and by two independent cues.**

1. **Colour** — terracotta/orange group near-left, pale cream group far-right. Instant.
2. **The marks** — every orange counter shows the dark speckled rosette, and every cream counter
   shows a small distinct dark curl. **Neither mark is absent.** Strip the colour out and you could
   still separate the two groups.

Residual, honestly: the crescent is visible but still not *readable* at that distance — it reads
as a comma, hook or curl rather than specifically as a moon, and it is small and sits high and
right of centre, so the cream face looks mostly empty next to the sun's centred, disc-filling
rosette. The two devices are reliably distinguishable from each other but they are not balanced as
a pair. I would call that a finish issue now, not a failure.

Also unchanged and still visible: no crowned/ringed piece appears anywhere on the board in image
11 — all 24 pieces show plain faces.

## F. Images 1 and 2 — the structure on the lid

**Yes.** A **cylindrical tube — a telescope barrel — sitting in the open slot**, its muzzle
projecting toward the camera. In image 1 the circular end face is crisp and the axis is legible;
in image 2 it is dead centre in the slit and reads as the instrument in the opening. It is a solid
object with a definite shape, not a lump.

**Yes, it is held.** In image 1 a chunky stepped block sits hard against the tube on the slot's
near wall. In image 2 the tube is flanked by small cross/plus-shaped brackets — one to the left at
mid-height, one below-right, and a further T-shaped tab above-right — which together read as a
fork or yoke mount. The thin fin still runs down from that area to the base plate.

**Nothing is visibly detached or floating.** I looked specifically for islands. Every bracket
element appears to touch either the tube or the slot wall. Two of them come close to reading as
free-floating tabs at this resolution — the left-hand plus in image 2 sits with a hairline gap
between it and the tube's silhouette, and the lower-right plus sits just under the tube's end —
but I would call those tight abutments rather than gaps, and I cannot resolve which from a render.

Quality caveat: the mount still reads as **small rectangles stuck to the sides of the tube**, not
as a cradle. In image 2 the arms meet the tube at raw right angles with no saddle and no visible
clearance. It holds the tube; it does not yet look like a thing designed to hold a telescope.

## G. Final defect list

Severity is judged against what the object visibly is: a domed observatory-shaped lid closing a
box that holds a two-colour sun/moon draughts-style game. I mark clearly where I am seeing a thing
versus inferring it.

### Resolved — no longer applies
- **Moon king face carried no device.** Fixed and verified in image 8. A distinct crescent now sits
  on the plateau with clear flat margin all round.
- **Sun and moon followed different rules between faces.** Fixed. Both discs now carry the same
  device on both faces, with a crown ring added on one.
- **Plan views carried no information.** Fixed in principle — both images 9 and 10 now show marks.
- **The crescent was absent at board distance.** Fixed and verified in image 11.
- **The tube had nothing carrying it.** Fixed — a mount is present and in contact.
- **Stray facet sliver on the dome.** Still gone.

### Blocking — and I can see it in this set
- **No grip, lip, finger relief or opening affordance anywhere on the closed object.** SEEN, in
  images 3, 4 and 12. The lid presents a smooth continuous dome and a flat plate resting on a tray
  rim; the only protrusion is the thin angled tube, which is the last thing anyone should pull. A
  user looking at this cannot tell how to open it, and the obvious wrong answer is the most
  fragile feature on the part. **BLOCKING.**
- **From directly overhead the crowned moon reads as a smiley face, not as a moon or as a crown.**
  SEEN, in image 10 — the ring shows only as a lower arc and the crescent flattens to a near-
  straight line. Overhead is the player's own viewpoint, so a player cannot identify a crowned moon
  by looking down at it. **BLOCKING**, and the only remaining piece-identification failure.

### Blocking in earlier rounds — cannot be assessed from this set
- **The lid reads as a computer mouse side-on and in silhouette.** NOT SEEN this round, because no
  frame in this set is shot side-on to the slot. All three closed/roof cameras look into the
  opening. I am explicitly not clearing this: the reading is absent from the photographs, which is
  not the same as absent from the object. A low az0 elevation would settle it.
- **The tube is cantilevered and unsupported past the nose of the dome.** NOT SEEN this round —
  every frame views the tube end-on or near end-on, so I cannot judge the projection or the
  overhang angle. This was clearly visible in earlier rounds and I have no evidence it changed, but
  I am inferring, not seeing.
- **Lid-to-tray registration.** Still cannot tell. Posts stand proud of the lid plate in images 3
  and 4; no matching pockets are visible. Unresolved, not asserted.

### Cosmetic — seen, and not failures
- **The dome dwarfs the box it closes, and its interior volume stores nothing** (images 3, 4, 12).
- **The slot cuts the shell in two down to the rim and plate** (images 1, 3, 4); the lobes are tied
  together only by the plate.
- **Thin knife-edge fin in the slot** dropping to the plate (images 1, 2, 3).
- **Continuous undercut groove round the base of the dome** (images 1, 2, 12) — re-entrant all the
  way round.
- **The dome spans essentially the full width of its base plate** (image 2), so clearance is large
  at the plate corners and near zero at the edge midpoints.
- **Coarse faceting** — visible concentric polygon banding across the dome (images 1, 2).
- **The mount reads as plus-shaped tabs stuck to the tube rather than as a cradle** (images 2, 3,
  4). It works; it does not look designed.
- **Bracket arms meet the tube at raw right angles with no visible clearance** (image 2). Flag for
  checking. If that is genuine interference rather than a tight abutment it would be blocking, but
  I cannot determine which from a render and I am not asserting it.
- **Board cells are implied, not defined** — chevron scribe lines only, no cells, no dimples;
  several counters straddle a line (images 4, 11).
- **Counters crowded against the frame** — the far-right cream and far-left orange pieces sit hard
  against the wall with visibly less clearance than any piece has to a neighbour (image 11).
- **Chevron rows are of inconsistent length** — some stop short of the frame, others run further
  (images 4, 11).
- **The four corner posts look like four different parts** — the near one a chunky block, the other
  three slim notched L-brackets (images 3, 4, 11).
- **The sun device renders as hairline outlines in near-plan** (image 9) — legible but weightless,
  reading as a scratched line drawing rather than a cut mark.
- **The crescent is small and sits off-centre**, so the cream face looks mostly empty beside the
  sun's centred, disc-filling rosette (images 7, 8, 11).
- **Images 9 and 10 are shot at different tilts and scales and show different faces**, so they
  cannot be read as a matched pair.

### Previously flagged, not visible in this set
- **The empty run through the middle of the storage layer.** Image 12's row appears fully occupied;
  the old gap was visible only from the other axis. Neither confirmed nor cleared.
- **The rectangular notch in the dome's top silhouette.** Not presented at any of this round's
  angles.
