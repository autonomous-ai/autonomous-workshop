## A. What has visibly changed about the lid

Comparing only the frames shot from identical cameras (images 2, 3, 4 and 12), the changes are
subtle and localised to the slot. What I can positively see:

1. **The slot across the dome is narrower and more parallel-sided.** In the iso view the dark
   channel at the apex is now roughly 60% of the width it was, and its two edges run close to
   parallel instead of fanning open toward the top. It no longer reads as a fat tapering crescent;
   it reads as a straight-walled cut of near-constant width.
2. **The slot now breaks the top silhouette.** In image 2 and image 12 there is a clean rectangular
   step notched into the upper profile line of the dome, on the far side of the apex, about
   two-thirds of the way along. That step was not in the earlier elevation.
3. **A stray triangular facet sliver that used to sit on the right-hand slope of the dome in the
   elevation and closed views is gone.** That patch of surface now renders clean.
4. **The slot's upper end now terminates in a squared-off blunt end just short of the dome apex**
   (clearest in image 1), rather than thinning to a point.

What I can see has **not** changed: the dome's overall teardrop profile, its height, its footprint
on the square base plate, the continuous undercut groove around the skirt, the angled cylinder
emerging at the nose, the base plate itself, and the raised rail and thin fin along the slot.

I cannot compare the new image 1 angle to the old one directly, because the camera moved; anything
I said about geometry at az-45/el45 does not transfer.

## B. As a plain black silhouette

Still **a computer mouse.** Image 2's outline is unchanged apart from the small rectangular notch
now bitten out of the top edge, and that notch does not help — as a pure black shape it reads as a
chip or a casting flaw in the blob, not as an opening. Secondary silhouette readings: squat
teapot/kettle, bicycle helmet, limpet shell.

The silhouette does not read as an observatory. The dome is too flattened and too asymmetric for
that, the slot is invisible in profile, and the tube at the nose is too short and too low to read
as an instrument — in outline it reads as a cable or a spout.

## C. The structure standing in/on the lid

Yes. There is a **cylinder — a tube with a flat circular end face — lying in the slot**, with its
muzzle projecting out past the nose of the dome at roughly 40-45 degrees (image 2 and image 12
show the projecting end; image 1 shows the barrel sitting in the slot with its round end square to
the viewer).

**It reads as a solid object with a definite shape — not a lump.** In image 1 it is unmistakably a
cylindrical barrel: the circular end face is crisp, the axis is legible, and its placement in the
open slot makes the "telescope in an observatory" reading immediate. In image 3 and image 4 it
reads slightly less well — there it presents as an upright circular boss or a knob, because the
foreshortening hides its axis.

Alongside it in the slot are two subordinate features: a **thin raised rail** along one wall of the
slot, and a **thin vertical fin** dropping to the base plate. Neither of those reads as anything
identifiable; they read as structure/scaffolding.

Weakness: the tube is very short relative to the dome. It barely clears the dome surface, so from
most angles it is a stub rather than an instrument. It is the slot's presence that sells the
telescope, not the tube.

## D. The two discs (images 5-8)

**Sun disc (images 5, 6, plus plan 9).** Outline is a perfect circle — no tail, tab, notch, slot or
spur, confirmed by the plan view. The sunk mark is an **eight-ray sunburst / rosette** around a
round central pocket. It depicts **the sun**. Image 5 has it on a plain flat face; image 6 has the
identical mark, but with an extra annular groove cut just inside the rim so the rosette sits on a
raised plateau. **Same mark on both faces**, plus a crown ring on one.

**Moon disc (images 7, 8, plus plan 10).** Outline is a perfect circle — no tail, tab, notch or
spur. Image 7's sunk mark is a **crescent**: a curved groove, thick at the top right, tapering to a
sharp horn at the lower left, about half the disc diameter. It depicts **a crescent moon**.

**Image 8, looked at specifically and carefully: no, there is no crescent on that face separate
from the ring.** What image 8 shows is one single fat groove running around just inside the rim,
almost closing into a full annulus, with a narrow break on the right side, enclosing a raised
plateau. That plateau is **completely blank**. There is no second, smaller crescent anywhere on
it. The only crescent-like quality the face has is that the "ring" is itself slightly incomplete
and varies in width — and that reads as a broken ring or a letter C long before it reads as a
moon.

So: **the sun disc's mark is the same on both faces; the moon disc's mark is different on its two
faces**, and on one of them the moon's identifying device is absent entirely. A player looking at
that face has a blank plateau inside a ring, which carries no side information at all. This is
unchanged from round 1 — images 5 through 10 look identical to the versions I saw before.

## E. Image 11 at a distance

Unchanged from round 1, and the same conclusion.

I can tell the two sides apart reliably, but **almost entirely by colour** — terracotta/orange
group near-left, pale cream group far-right. That separation is instant.

The **marks** do not carry the distinction:
- The sun rosette survives the distance as a visible dark speckled flower/cog. Not readable as
  "sun" specifically, but clearly a mark.
- The crescent does not survive. On the cream counters it reads as a thin pale arc along the
  top-left of the disc, indistinguishable from a lighting highlight on a chamfered rim. Several
  cream counters look simply unmarked.

**So yes, one mark is ambiguous: the moon's.** Print both colours in the same filament, or view in
poor light, and the cream side reads as "blank piece", not as "the other player's piece". The
crescent needs to be wider, deeper, or paired with a second element.

Secondary: the sun's central pocket renders as a bright crescent inside its ring of rays (clearest
in image 5), so at small scale the sun counter is carrying a crescent shape in its middle. It
muddies rather than breaks the distinction.

I still cannot see any crowned/ringed pieces on the board in image 11 — every piece appears to be
a plain face.

## F. Defect list, with round-1 status

### No longer applies (fixed or gone)
- **Stray triangular facet sliver on the dome's right slope.** Gone from images 2 and 12. The
  surface renders clean now.
- **"The crescent channel is only legible from a raking angle."** Partly resolved. At the new
  three-quarter angle (image 1) the slot is highly legible and the whole lid resolves into a
  recognisable object immediately. That is a genuine improvement.

### Still stands
- **The lid still reads as a computer mouse in elevation and in silhouette** (image 2). The
  improvement in image 1 does not carry to the low viewing angle, which is the angle a person
  sees a box on a table from.
- **The dome dwarfs the box it closes.** Images 3, 4, 12: the lid is roughly two to three times the
  height of the tray, and the storage cavity is a thin sliver.
- **The dome's interior volume is unused.** Image 12 shows every counter lying below the lid plate.
  A large empty bubble over a shallow slot.
- **The tube is cantilevered and unsupported.** Images 2 and 12: it projects into open air at ~45
  degrees off a curved surface, far end floating above the plate. Overhang when printed, snap
  hazard when handled.
- **The slot still cuts the dome all the way down to the rim and the base plate.** Images 1, 3, 4:
  the shell is divided into two lobes joined only around the far side.
- **Thin knife-edge fin inside the slot,** dropping to the plate with a stepped notch at its foot.
  Images 1, 3, 4. Fragile, and awkward at print resolution.
- **Undercut skirt.** A continuous groove runs right round the base of the dome with the shell
  flaring above it (images 1, 2, 12). Re-entrant all the way round.
- **The dome is crowded against the mid-edges of its base plate.** Image 2: the skirt spans
  essentially the full plate width. Image 1 shows large clearances at the plate corners and none at
  the edge midpoints — the margin is very uneven.
- **Visible coarse faceting.** Image 1 shows pronounced concentric polygon banding across the whole
  dome; the tessellation is too coarse for a surface this size.
- **Moon king face carries no crescent** (image 8). Detailed in D.
- **Sun and moon do not follow the same rule between faces.** Sun keeps its device and gains a
  ring; moon's device becomes the ring.
- **Plan views carry no information** (images 9 and 10). Straight down — the player's actual
  viewpoint — both counters are featureless circles.
- **Board cells are implied, not defined.** Images 4 and 11: chevron scribe lines only, no closed
  cells, no dimples, nothing to locate a counter. Several counters straddle a line.
- **Counters crowded against the frame.** Image 11: the far-right cream counter in the middle band
  and the lone orange counter on the left both sit hard against the wall with visibly less
  clearance than any piece has to its neighbours.
- **Chevron rows are of inconsistent length.** Images 4 and 11: some rows stop short of the frame,
  others run further. Reads as a trim artefact.
- **Corner posts look like four different parts.** Images 3, 4, 11: the near post reads as a chunky
  solid block, the other three as slim notched L-brackets.
- **Storage packing leaves a hole.** Image 12: orange at the left, cream at the right, empty slots
  through the middle. Loose counters will rattle.
- **No grip or opening affordance.** Nothing in images 3, 4 or 12 says how the lid is lifted. The
  tube is the only protrusion and it is thin, angled and on a curve.
- **Lid-to-tray registration still unreadable.** The corner posts stand proud of the lid plate but I
  can see no matching pockets in the plate; the lid appears to rest on the rim millimetres above
  the stored counters.

### New this round
- **The slot now bites a small rectangular notch out of the dome's top silhouette** (images 2 and
  12). Read in isolation, at that angle, it looks like a chip or a modelling mistake rather than an
  opening. What helps at three-quarters hurts in profile.
- **The narrowed slot makes the tube look pinched.** In image 1 the barrel very nearly fills the
  slot width, and the tube appears to run hard into the slot's side wall and the fin at a raw
  intersection with no clearance and no blend. Whether that is a true interference or just tight
  clearance I cannot tell from a render, but it looks like two solids meeting rather than a
  designed fit.
- **A small rectangular tab or ledge sits on the dome surface at the edge of the slot,** about
  halfway down (visible in images 3 and 4 as a tiny step sticking out of the left slot wall). It is
  unexplained by anything else in the part and reads as a stray boss.
- **The slot's lower end interrupts the skirt band,** leaving a squared gap cut through the rim
  detail down to the plate (images 1 and 3). The rim moulding just stops and restarts.
- **The tube is out of proportion to the dome** — too short and too thin to read as an instrument
  from anything but the one flattering angle. If the tube were longer and thicker, the elevation
  view might stop reading as a mouse.
