## A. What has visibly changed about the lid

Comparing only the frames shot from the same camera as last time (images 1, 3, 4, 12):

1. **A bracket has been added around the cylinder in the slot.** In image 3 and image 4 there are
   now small tabs projecting from *both* sides of the tube, forming a cross or plus shape around
   it; previously there was a single stray tab on one side only. In image 1 a chunky stepped block
   now sits immediately alongside the tube, where in the previous round that area was plain slot
   wall. In image 2 (new angle) the arrangement is clearest: the tube sits between two small
   cross/T-shaped brackets, one above-left and one below-right, with a further small T at the top
   of the slot.
2. **A new angular wedge/fin has appeared beside the protruding end of the tube** in image 12, just
   behind and above it, rising to a sharp point.
3. Otherwise the lid looks unchanged: same dome profile and height, same slot width and position,
   same rectangular notch bitten out of the top silhouette in image 12, same continuous undercut
   groove round the skirt, same square base plate, same thin fin dropping to the plate.

**One disc has also changed** (not asked in A, but recorded fresh): the cream disc's crescent in
image 7 is now smaller, narrower and more sharply tapered than before, and it now cuts a dark
shadow instead of a pale highlight. The orange disc looks identical to previous rounds.

## B. Recognisable structure on the lid, and what carries it

Yes. **A cylindrical tube — a telescope barrel — lying in the open slot, with its muzzle
projecting out past the dome at about 40-45 degrees.** It reads as a solid object with a definite
shape: crisp circular end face, legible axis, unmistakable in image 1.

**And yes, something now carries it.** There is a bracket assembly straddling the tube: arms
projecting from both sides in images 2, 3 and 4, plus a stepped block beside it in image 1 and a
thin fin dropping from it to the base plate. Functionally that reads as a **fork or yoke mount** —
the tube is no longer floating in the slot with nothing under it, which it effectively was before.

How well it reads is a different question. The mount is made of small rectangular tabs meeting at
right angles, so in images 3 and 4 it reads as **plus signs stuck to the sides of the tube** rather
than as a mount. In image 2 the arms appear to run straight into the tube's silhouette with no
cradle, no saddle and no visible clearance — two solids meeting rather than one part holding
another. It carries the tube; it does not yet look like a thing that carries a telescope.

## C. Image 8, looked at carefully, before re-checking image 7

**No.** There is no mark on the raised middle plateau that is separate from the ring round the
edge.

What image 8 contains, from the outside in: a flat outer rim; one fat sunk groove running almost
all the way round just inside that rim, thick at the top-left, thinning and breaking open on the
right; and inside that, a raised circular plateau. **That plateau is completely blank.** I looked
specifically for a second, smaller device on it and there is nothing — no crescent, no rosette, no
pip, no texture. The only crescent-ish quality on the face belongs to the edge groove itself,
because it is incomplete and varies in width, and that reads as a broken ring or a letter C rather
than as a moon.

## D. Comparing 7 with 8, and 5 with 6

**Cream disc (7 vs 8).** The marks are **different**. Image 7 carries a small, slim, sharply
tapered crescent groove sitting in the middle of a plain flat face — clearly a crescent moon.
Image 8 carries no such crescent at all; it has only the big broken edge ring and a blank plateau.
Flip the piece and its identity disappears.

**Orange disc (5 vs 6).** The marks are **the same**. Both faces carry the identical eight-ray
sunburst around a round central pocket. Image 6 simply adds an annular groove just inside the rim
so the rosette sits on a raised plateau. Flip the piece and it is still obviously a sun.

**Outlines.** Both discs are perfect circles. The plan views (images 9 and 10) confirm it — clean
circles with a chamfered rim ring, no tail, tab, notch, slot, spur or flat anywhere on the
circumference.

**Both plan views are still completely blank.** Straight down, neither disc shows any mark at all.
I still cannot tell from the images whether those renders show the unmarked underside or the
marked face flattened out by the lighting.

## E. Image 11 at a distance

**Yes, and this has improved.** Two things now separate the sides:

1. **Colour** — terracotta/orange near-left, pale cream far-right. Instant, as before.
2. **The marks** — and this is the change. Each cream counter now shows a small, distinct dark curl
   on its face. In the previous round the cream counters read as blank, with only a pale rim
   highlight. Now every one of them visibly carries a mark.

So **neither mark is absent any more.** Strip the colour out and you could still tell the two
groups apart: one group has a many-pointed speckled rosette, the other a single compact curl.

Residual ambiguity, being honest: the crescent is now *visible* but not *readable*. At that
distance it reads as a comma, a hook, a fingernail clipping or a lowercase letter, not
specifically as a moon. It is also small relative to the disc and sits high and right of centre,
so it reads as a small blemish on a blank face rather than as a device occupying the face. The sun
still fills its disc; the moon still does not. They no longer confuse with each other, but they are
not balanced as a pair.

## F. Defects, with status and severity

Severity is judged against what the object visibly is: a domed observatory-shaped lid closing a
box that holds a two-colour sun/moon draughts-style game.

### No longer applies
- **"The crescent is invisible at board distance / the cream pieces read as blank."** Fixed. Image
  11 now shows a definite mark on every cream counter.
- **"The tube floats in the slot with nothing carrying it."** Addressed — a bracket/yoke now
  straddles it.
- **"Stray triangular facet sliver on the dome's right slope."** Still gone.

### Still stands
- **The lid reads as a computer mouse at low viewing angles and in silhouette** (image 12).
  **BLOCKING.** This is the angle a box on a table is actually seen from, and it is the frame doing
  duty as the closed-product shot. The object fails to announce what it is at exactly the moment it
  most needs to.
- **The tube is still cantilevered out past the nose of the dome into open air** (image 12), with
  its far end unsupported above the plate. The new mount holds it *inside* the slot but does
  nothing for the projecting end. **BLOCKING** as a printed part — steep unsupported overhang off a
  curved surface, and the most snappable feature on the object.
- **No grip or opening affordance.** Nothing in images 3, 4 or 12 says how the lid is lifted; the
  only protrusion is the thin angled tube, which is the last thing anyone should pull on.
  **BLOCKING** — it is a box, and it does not show you how to open it.
- **Moon-king face carries no device** (image 8). **BLOCKING** — a crowned piece with no side
  identity is a game-play failure, not a finish issue.
- **Sun and moon do not follow the same rule between faces** (sun keeps its device and gains a
  ring; moon's device is replaced by the ring). **BLOCKING**, same root cause as above.
- **Plan views carry no information** (images 9, 10) — from directly overhead both counters are
  featureless circles. **BLOCKING if those are the marked faces**; I still cannot determine which
  from the images, so this needs checking rather than assuming.
- **The dome dwarfs the box it closes and its interior volume is unused** (images 3, 4, 12).
  COSMETIC — wasteful and oddly proportioned, but nothing fails.
- **The slot cuts the shell in two all the way down to the rim and plate** (images 1, 3, 4).
  COSMETIC — the plate ties the lobes together, but it is worth knowing the shell is not continuous.
- **Thin knife-edge fin inside the slot.** COSMETIC — fragile at print resolution.
- **Undercut skirt**, continuous re-entrant groove right round the base (images 1, 2, 12). COSMETIC
  unless that groove is doing sealing or location work, which I cannot tell.
- **The dome is crowded against the mid-edges of its base plate** (image 2: the skirt spans
  essentially the full plate width; image 1: large clearances at the corners, none at the edge
  midpoints). COSMETIC.
- **Coarse faceting** — pronounced concentric polygon banding across the dome (images 1, 2).
  COSMETIC.
- **Board cells are implied, not defined** — chevron scribe lines only, no cells or dimples;
  several counters straddle a line (images 4, 11). COSMETIC.
- **Counters crowded against the frame** — far-right cream and far-left orange sit hard against the
  wall with visibly less clearance than any piece has to its neighbours (image 11). COSMETIC.
- **Chevron rows are of inconsistent length** (images 4, 11). COSMETIC.
- **Corner posts look like four different parts** — the near one a chunky block, the other three
  slim notched L-brackets (images 3, 4, 11). COSMETIC.
- **Storage packing leaves an empty run through the middle** (image 12); counters will rattle.
  COSMETIC.
- **Lid-to-tray registration is still unreadable** — posts stand proud of the lid plate but no
  matching pockets are visible. Cannot tell; needs checking.

### New this round
- **The mount reads as tabs, not as a mount.** In images 3 and 4 the brackets are small rectangles
  projecting from each side of the tube, which read as plus signs stuck onto it. COSMETIC, but it
  costs the object the credibility the tube just earned.
- **The bracket arms appear to run straight into the tube with no cradle and no clearance**
  (clearest in image 2). Whether that is real interference or just a tight abutment I cannot tell
  from a render, but it looks like two solids intersecting rather than a fit. Flag for checking —
  **BLOCKING if it is genuine interference**, cosmetic if it is only tight.
- **A new angular wedge beside the protruding tube** (image 12) rises to a sharp point at a
  different angle to the tube. From that angle it reads as a spike or a snapped-off flap rather
  than as part of a mount. COSMETIC, but it makes the mouse silhouette worse, not better.
- **The crescent has become small and off-centre.** It is now legible at distance (good) but it
  reads as a comma or hook rather than a moon, and it sits high and right of the disc centre so the
  face looks unbalanced against the sun's centred, disc-filling rosette (images 7 and 11).
  COSMETIC, verging on blocking for a piece whose whole job is to say "moon".
- **The rectangular notch bitten out of the dome's top silhouette** persists in image 12 and still
  reads, at that angle, as a chip in the blob rather than as an opening. COSMETIC.
