# Wish

Jungle Chess played with the solar system. Every piece is a planet, and a planet's size on the board is the fifth root of its real measured diameter, so rank is something you can see rather than something you have to learn: Mercury is the smallest world in play and Jupiter the largest, and the traditional rules are followed exactly as written. Matter faces antimatter. One army's worlds lean their poles one way and the other army's lean the other, by each planet's own true axial tilt, and the bases give the same answer again in white against black. Six of the eight worlds wear the pattern a telescope shows as circles and bands. Earth wears its own coastlines and Mars its own dark continents -- Syrtis Major, the wedge every telescope owner has drawn since 1659, is a triangle here rather than a dot, and the southern seas run together into one dark belt -- because those two are the only worlds in the set whose real surfaces carry a shape you already half know by sight. A cocoa-brown asteroid belt crosses the middle of the field, a star stands at each end with two flames bursting off the board edge, and the three cells around each star are sunken corona wells where a captured world drops out of the light and its rank counts for nothing. You win by walking one of your worlds onto the rival star.
