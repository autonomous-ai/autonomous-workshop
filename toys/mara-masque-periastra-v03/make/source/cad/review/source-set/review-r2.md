# PART 1 — fresh observations, still unprimed

## roof/yoke_az-60_el18.png and roof/yoke-close_az-60_el18.png

What actually holds the tube up: **two flat plate-like arms that fork upward from a solid rectangular block at the bottom of the slot.** The block sits on the flat floor at the base of the slot. The two arms rise from it, splaying slightly apart, and the tube lies in the fork between their upper ends — the near arm passes in front of the tube's lower flank and the far arm behind it, so the tube is cradled between them rather than stuck onto anything. yoke-close makes the joint unambiguous: both arms run continuously from the block up to the tube, and the tube's underside meets both arm tops. There is no gap, no floating element, and no separate bracket.

What the flat surface behind it is: **it is not a panel.** It is the **inside of the dome** — the concave rear surface of the far half of the shell, seen through the open slot. In yoke-close you can trace it: the darker surface runs from the left cut edge of the slot across behind the tube and meets the right cut edge, and at the top of yoke_az-60_el18 you can see it curving away over the apex. It is dark simply because it faces away from the light. The two cut edges of the shell are visible as clean vertical faces on either side, each with thickness.

So my earlier readings were wrong on both counts. The tube is not a cantilever — it is supported from below by a two-armed yoke on a pier. And there is no disconnected flat panel bridging the slot; that was the interior of the dome.

One thing I still see: the tube's front end is **hollow** — yoke-close shows a bright annular rim around a dark bore, with the bore's inner wall visible receding. That confirms the earlier reading.

## counters/sun-flip_az-90_el18.png and counters/moon-flip_az-90_el18.png

Four copies of one counter rolled through 0 / 55 / 125 / 180 degrees.

- The 0° and 180° copies are **cut off by the frame edges** — only slivers of them are in shot. Copies 2 (55°) and 3 (125°) are fully visible.
- Copy 2 (55°) shows the first face at a raking angle. Sun: a flat outer rim band, a sunk field inside it, and within that sunk field a ring of eight raised blocks and a raised central ball, all of whose top faces sit back up level with the rim band. Moon: same construction, one raised crescent.
- Copy 3 (125°) has rolled past vertical, so we are now looking at the **second face**. It carries the same mark, and it is **mirrored** — the moon's bay opens the opposite way from copy 2, and the sun's ring reads the same rotated. The field is sunk and in shadow, with the mark standing out bright against it.
- Copy 4 (180°, partly in frame at the right) shows a marked face again.

So: yes, **both faces are marked, with the same mark, mirrored.** These two frames answer the question my blind read could not — the earlier "two flat copies look identical" was not a missing flip; both faces genuinely carry the mark.

# PART 2 — primed. Requirement by requirement.

## POSITIVE REQUIREMENTS

**1. Hemispherical dome on a visible drum, rise = base radius — MATCHES.**
roof/side-on_az0_el6.png. The dome silhouette spans about 825 px wide (half-width ~412) and rises about 440 px from the seam to the apex — within ~7%, which a perspective camera at 6° elevation accounts for. It reads as a true hemisphere, not a squashed or stretched one. The drum is visibly present and separate: a short cylindrical band at the bottom, bounded by two horizontal lines, standing very slightly proud of the dome's base circle, clearly readable in roof/side-on_az0_el6.png, roof/yoke_az-60_el18.png and closed/az0_el8.png.

**2. Flat margin all the way round the dome on the square plate — MATCHES.**
roof/yoke_az-60_el18.png shows the plate extending past the dome on all four sides with an unbroken flat border; roof/three-quarter_az-135_el28.png confirms the same from the opposite quadrant. In roof/side-on_az0_el6.png the dome occupies about 0.80 of the plate width against a specified 0.822 — the difference is perspective on the near plate edge. The margin is continuous and the same width all round.

**3. One shutter slit on one meridian, over the apex, far half unbroken — MATCHES.**
roof/down-slit_az-90_el14.png is square to the slit and shows a single vertical channel running from the drum to the apex. roof/yoke_az-60_el18.png shows it continuing over the apex and starting down the far side, then stopping. roof/three-quarter_az-135_el28.png views the dome from the other quadrant: the far half of the shell is completely unbroken. roof/side-on_az0_el6.png, taken 90° round from the slit, shows unbroken surface with only a short dark line near the apex — the far lip of the slit seen over the top, which is exactly what a slit crossing the crown and running a short way down the other side should produce. The slit also passes down through the drum, interrupting the drum ring, as specified.

**4. Slender telescope centred in the slit, open muzzle bore, on a yoke of two arms rising from a pier — MATCHES.**
roof/yoke-close_az-60_el18.png: two flat arms fork up from a rectangular pier block and cradle the tube between them. roof/down-slit_az-90_el14.png, square-on, shows the tube centred left-to-right in the slit. The bore is open in both roof/telescope-close_az-90_el8.png and roof/yoke-close_az-60_el18.png — a bright annular muzzle ring with a dark bore and visible inner wall. Not solid.

**5. Muzzle projects clear of the dome's outer surface — MATCHES.**
roof/side-on_az0_el6.png: the tube breaks the dome silhouette on the upper left and stands clear against the background. closed/az0_el8.png shows the same on the assembled object.

**6. Mark raised out of a sunk field, back up to full face level, inside a flat rim band — MATCHES.**
counters/sun-flip_az-90_el18.png copy 2 and counters/moon-flip_az-90_el18.png copy 2 show the three-level construction directly: outer flat rim band at full face level, a step down to the sunk field floor, and the mark rising from that floor back to rim level. counters/moon-oblique_az-90_el22.png shows the crescent's vertical side walls and its top coplanar with the rim. counters/sun-oblique_az-90_el22.png shows the same for the ray blocks and the central ball.

**7. Sun mark: unbroken ball, clear ring of field, eight detached widening wedges with blunt tips — MATCHES.**
counters/sun-plan_el90.png: the central ball is a single unbroken raised disc with no marks on or across it; a clear band of sunk field separates it from the wedges; there are exactly eight wedges, none of them touching the ball or each other. The alternating "square / diamond" appearance around the ring is just the same wedge shape seen at 45° rotations. The tips are blunt, not pointed. The taper is the weakest part of this: in counters/sun-plan_el90.png and counters/sun-flip_az-90_el18.png copy 2 the wedges widen outward only slightly, and at a glance they read as square blocks rather than wedges. It meets the requirement, but only just.

**8. Moon mark: horns curl past the middle, reaching towards each other, deep nearly-enclosed bay — DOES NOT MATCH.**
Taking the clauses separately, from counters/moon-symbol-plan.png:
- Not a fat gibbous disc — correct, it is a slim crescent of roughly even width. PASSES.
- Not a banana — correct. The outer arc runs about 200° of the circle, well past half. PASSES.
- Horns curl past the middle — just barely. The disc field centre is at about x=600; both horn tips land at about x=670, roughly 12% of the field radius past the centreline. PASSES, marginally.
- Deep, nearly-enclosed bay — **fails.** The two tips are about 790 px apart across a field about 1080 px wide: the mouth of the bay is roughly 73% of the field width, a gap of about 160° of arc. That is a wide-open C, not a nearly-enclosed bay. counters/moon-flip_az-90_el18.png copy 2 shows the same shape in three dimensions and confirms it — the bay is broad and shallow-mouthed, and nothing about it reads as enclosed.

The mark is a correct, recognisable crescent. It is not the deep-bayed crescent the requirement describes.

**9. Horn tips rounded, not cut off square — MATCHES.**
counters/moon-symbol-plan.png at both tips: the outline curves smoothly round the end of each horn into a small rounded cap with a highlight on it, rather than terminating on a straight chord. In counters/moon-flip_az-90_el18.png copy 2 the upper tip briefly reads as squared, but that is the flat top face of the tip catching light at a raking angle, not a square-cut end — the silhouette is still round.

**10. Both faces identical mirror images, no wrong way up — MATCHES.**
This is what the new flip frames prove. counters/moon-flip_az-90_el18.png copy 3 (125°) shows the second face carrying the same crescent, mirrored; counters/sun-flip_az-90_el18.png copy 3 shows the second face carrying the same sunburst. Both second faces have the same sunk field and rim band as the first. My blind read could not settle this and now it is settled.
Framing complaint: the 0° and 180° copies are half out of frame in both files. The evidence still carries because copies 2 and 3 are enough, but the frames are cropped too tight.

**11. Stacked pair sits square, does not rock, including two Moons with crescents opposed — MATCHES.**
kings/low-oblique_az-110_el10.png: both the cream stack and the orange stack show a clean horizontal parting line, coaxial discs, and no tilt or wedge-shaped gap. The reason it works is visible in counters/moon-flip_az-90_el18.png: the flat rim band is a **complete, continuous annulus at full face level** on both faces. Two counters therefore bear on each other around that whole ring regardless of how the crescents are rotated; the marks, being coplanar with the rim, can only add contact, never lift one side. The worst case in the requirement is structurally impossible with this geometry.

**12. Distinguishable at a glance across a populated board — MATCHES.**
playing/top_el90.png: twelve a side, and the two are separable by two independent cues — colour (orange-brown vs pale cream) and the texture of the mark (the sun is a busy ring of nine small elements, the moon is one long sweeping arc). Either cue alone would do it. kings/board_az-90_el45.png confirms at closer range.
Blunt caveat, unchanged from my blind read: the shape cue is much weaker for the Moon than the Sun. Because requirement 6 puts the mark top level with the rim, a straight-down view gets no shading difference and the crescent survives only as an outline. In a single material under flat overhead light the Moon would be noticeably fainter than the Sun. It passes, but the two marks are not equally robust.

**13. Counters store inside the closed box — MATCHES, with a serious reservation.**
signature.png panel 1 and closed/az0_el8.png show all the counters lying inside the tray under the fitted lid; closed/az-90_el10.png shows a full row of them from the other side. They fit, and the lid seats. The reservation is that they are **not enclosed** — see the blocking defects section below.

**17. No mark carved as a hole or pocket — MATCHES.**
Every mark stands proud. counters/sun-flip_az-90_el18.png copy 2 and counters/moon-flip_az-90_el18.png copies 2 and 3 all show the mark above its surroundings with lit top faces and shadowed side walls. **18. No sloped dish, cone, annular rebate, raised plateau or halo; every counter surface flat-horizontal or square-vertical — DOES NOT MATCH.**
On the faces themselves it is clean: counters/sun-oblique_az-90_el22.png and counters/moon-oblique_az-90_el22.png show a flat rim band, a vertical step, a flat field floor, a vertical mark wall and a flat mark top. No dish, no cone, no halo, no plateau.
But the **outer circumferential edge is chamfered.** counters/sun-faces-plan_el90.png and counters/moon-faces-plan_el90.png, right-hand copy: the counter standing on its edge projects as a rectangle with **all four corners cut off at 45°**. Under a top view, a counter with a purely vertical cylindrical wall and square edges would project as a clean rectangle. A 45° chamfer is a sloped surface, so on a literal reading of "every counter surface flat-and-horizontal or square-and-vertical" this fails. It is a small edge break, not a dish or a halo, and it is arguably good for handling — but it is there and I am not going to call it absent.

**19. Nothing protrudes from the closed object except the dome and the telescope — DOES NOT MATCH.**

They are a locating feature rather than decoration or hardware, so whether they count is a judgement call for the brief's author — but they are a third thing sticking out of the closed object, and the requirement names only two.

**20. No part floating, doubled, misplaced or interpenetrating — MATCHES.**
roof/yoke-close_az-60_el18.png resolves the one case I suspected: pier, both arms and tube are a single connected chain, each meeting the next with real contact. Nothing floats. The counters in playing/top_el90.png sit centred on their tiles and, because the game only ever puts them on alternating cells, no two ever touch. Nothing is doubled except the intended king stacks in kings/*, and those are coaxial and correctly placed.

## My blind-read defects, re-judged

**Withdrawn as reading errors:**
- *"Telescope tube embedded in a flat panel with no clean joint"* (blind defect 5) and *"the dome slot is inconsistent between views — a panel that does not connect to either dome half"* (blind defect 7). Both wrong, same cause. The flat surface was the **inside of the far dome shell** seen through the slit, and the tube is properly carried on a two-armed yoke. roof/yoke-close_az-60_el18.png shows it plainly. The old roof/telescope-close_az-120_el22.png framing put the camera where the yoke was hidden behind the near shell edge; that is what misled me.
- *"Fragile unsupported cantilever"* (blind defect 6). The premise was wrong — the tube is supported from beneath by two arms on a pier, not cantilevered. I keep only the reduced version: the muzzle has to project clear of the shell (requirement 5 demands it), so it remains the thinnest exposed feature and the one most exposed to a knock, and in any layer-based process it is an overhang. That is a property of the design intent, not a mistake.
- *"Faceting / low-poly dome and polygonal disc outlines"* (blind defect 10). Tessellation of the preview mesh, not geometry. Not a defect of the object. Worth checking only at export resolution.
- *"The edge-on demo disc in the plan frames is a wasted view"* (blind defect 11). Still true of those frames, but now moot — the flip frames answer the question properly.

**Still real defects of this object:**
- *The closed box does not retain its contents* (blind defect 1). Unchanged and confirmed. In closed/az0_el8.png and closed/az-90_el10.png you can see the counters straight through an open band that runs around the whole perimeter between the top of the tray wall and the underside of the lid plate, interrupted only by the corner posts. The lid rests on the posts, not on the wall. Knowing the brief does not soften this: requirement 16 forbids any latch or magnet, so nothing else is holding them either. Tip it and it empties.
- *The lid plate is supported only at four corners* (blind defect 2). A direct consequence of the above. A thin square plate carrying a 150 mm dome at its centre, bridging corner to corner with no bearing along the edges.
- *Counters are as wide as their cells* (blind defect 3). Measured in playing/top_el90.png, the disc diameter is essentially equal to the tile width — the discs fill their squares edge to edge with no gutter. They never collide, because draughts only uses alternating cells, but the board reads crowded and a piece cannot be nudged without covering the tile outline entirely.
- *The board has almost no contrast* (blind defect 4). In playing/top_el90.png and kings/board_az-90_el45.png the playing cells are distinguished only by a shallow engraved outline in one uniform material. At arm's length the checker pattern is faint, and in kings/low-oblique_az-110_el10.png the floor reads as staggered brickwork rather than a board.
- *The Moon is much weaker than the Sun from directly overhead* (blind defect 8), downgraded from defect to inherent consequence. It is caused by requirement 6 — mark top level with rim means no shading from dead above. I would still raise it, because overhead is the angle a seated player actually uses.
- *The two marks are not matched in visual weight* (blind defect 9), downgraded to a taste note. Nine small elements versus one big arc. Conventional for sun/moon iconography; I no longer call it a defect.

## Blocking visual defects

Three, against the requirement list as written:

1. **Requirement 8 — the Moon's bay is not deep or nearly-enclosed.** Mouth is roughly 73% of the field width, about a 160° gap (counters/moon-symbol-plan.png, counters/moon-flip_az-90_el18.png copy 2). It clears both prohibitions (not gibbous, not a banana) and the horns do just pass the centreline, but the shape specified is not the shape delivered.
2. **Requirement 18 — the counters' outer edge is chamfered.** Clearest in counters/sun-faces-plan_el90.png and counters/moon-faces-plan_el90.png, where the edge-on copy projects with all four corners cut at 45° instead of square. A sloped surface where the requirement permits only horizontal or vertical ones.
3. **Requirement 19 — the four tray corner posts stand proud of the closed lid** (iso.png, closed/az0_el8.png, closed/az-90_el10.png). A third protruding element beyond the dome and the telescope.

And one functional failure that is not on the numbered list but is worse than any of them: **the closed box has an open slot right round its perimeter and will not hold the counters in.** If only one thing gets fixed, it should be that.

## Is it desirable?

Yes — considerably more than I thought on the blind read, and the two new yoke frames are most of the reason. What looked like a broken telescope stuck to a floating panel turns out to be a properly built yoke on a pier with an open muzzle, and it is the best-made thing on the object. The restraint is what sells it: a bare flat margin, no railings or rivets, no hinge or latch, one clean slit on one meridian. The Sun and Moon counters are genuinely nice objects in the hand — double-sided, no wrong way up, and a stacked king that cannot rock.

What stops me buying it as shown is still the box. A miniature set whose whole charm is that it packs itself away has to actually stay shut, and this one is open all the way round. Fix the closure, give the counters a little air inside their squares, deepen the Moon's bay, and it is a thing I would want on a shelf.

## Round 3 — three requirements restated

No geometry changed. Three verdicts are re-judged against corrected wording and one supplied measurement. Everything above stands as written, including the parts these corrections overturn.

### Req 18, face only — MATCHES

Setting aside the mandated 1.0 mm outer edge chamfer and the mandated 0.3 mm bevel round each raised island, I can find no sloped dish, conical surface, annular rebate, plateau or halo on either counter face.

Evidence, re-read for this specific question:

- **counters/sun-oblique_az-90_el22.png** — the face has exactly **two levels and no more**. Outer flat rim band; one vertical step down; flat field floor; the eight wedges and the central ball rising back off that floor on vertical walls to rim level. I looked specifically for a third concentric step between the rim and the field and there is none — the only circle inside the rim band is the pocket wall itself, which reads as a dark vertical arc on the far side, correctly vertical.
- **counters/moon-oblique_az-90_el22.png** — same two-level construction, checked the same way. The rim band is uniform in tone all the way round, which is what a flat horizontal annulus does under one light; a dish or a cone would shade progressively across it and it does not.
- **The field floor is flat.** In both oblique frames and in counters/moon-flip_az-90_el18.png copy 3, where the whole field falls into shadow, the floor holds a **single uniform tone with sharp-edged shadow boundaries**. A sloped or conical floor would render as a gradient across the pocket. There is no gradient in any frame.
- The central ball on the Sun is a raised island required by req 7, not an unmandated plateau; the clear ring of field around it is sunk, not a raised halo.

My round-2 finding was about the outer circumferential edge, and I read the geometry correctly — there is a chamfer there, visible as the 45° corner cuts on the edge-on copy in counters/sun-faces-plan_el90.png and counters/moon-faces-plan_el90.png. What I got wrong was applying a face rule to an edge. With the requirement stated properly, that chamfer is mandated and the face is clean. **DOES NOT MATCH withdrawn; MATCHES.**

### Req 19, nothing NEW protrudes — MATCHES

Taking the measurement as given (base top z = 24.0, roof plate top z = 21.0 + 3.0 = 24.0, exactly flush), the four corner posts are flush and they are original base geometry, not added by this correction. The only things standing out of the closed object that are new are the dome and the telescope, and nothing else — closed/az0_el8.png and closed/az-90_el10.png show a clean silhouette of plate, drum, dome and muzzle with no other break.

Why I read it as proud: in **iso.png** the four corners of the assembly show grey posts that appear to stand above the teal plate. Re-reading that frame with the measurement in hand, what actually distinguishes them is not height — it is that the posts sit **outboard of the plate's edge across a visible reveal**, so at an oblique angle the eye reads a separate element and assumes a step. There is no step. Also worth recording, because it bears on req 14: the plate in **signature.png** (middle panel, lid floating clear of the base) is a **clean square with four straight edges and sharp corners** — it is not notched to clear the posts, it is simply smaller than the base opening. Req 14 stands as MATCHES for the right reason.

**DOES NOT MATCH withdrawn; MATCHES.**

### Req 8, Moon — I change my verdict to MATCHES

The numbers support my reading of the *shape* and overturn my *verdict*. Both of those are true at once, and I want to be precise about which is which.

My eye was right. I estimated the mouth of the bay at roughly 73% of the field width; 11.46 mm tip-to-tip is about 73% of the sunk field and about 64% of the Ø18 flat face. That is the same number. The object does read as a wide C, and I would describe it that way again to anyone who asked.

But I was judging the object against the phrase "deep nearly-enclosed bay" rather than against what the brief actually asked the CAD to build. The brief's own target for the opening is **11.20 mm and the build measures 11.46 mm** — 0.26 mm wide, 2.3%. Every other stated figure lands: crossing at +2.776 against +2.78, belly 3.50 against 3.50, waists 3.303 / 2.648 / 1.899 against 3.30 / 2.65 / 1.90, surviving area 36.95% against 38%. A build that hits its own dimensional target to a quarter of a millimetre is not failing the requirement; my complaint was with the prose, and the prose is not the geometry. I should not have converted a disagreement about an adjective into a DOES NOT MATCH against the part.

The wrap shortfall is not a defect either, and it is self-explaining: 206.6° built against 232.7° for infinitely sharp points, with the difference spent rounding a 27.1° cusp at R0.45. The brief required rounded tips and forbade cutting them square. You cannot have both the full theoretical wrap and a rounded horn — the 26° is the price of req 9, paid deliberately. counters/moon-symbol-plan.png confirms it was paid correctly: the tips curve smoothly round rather than stopping on a chord.

What I would need to see to change back to DOES NOT MATCH: a horn gap materially above target — say beyond about 13 mm, where the horns would stop passing the centreline in any meaningful way — or the mark reading as a banana or a gibbous disc at board distance. Neither is the case; the crescent wraps past 200°, the horns clear the centreline at x = +1.36, and in playing/top_el90.png twelve of them read as unmistakable crescents.

One note for the brief rather than the model: "deep nearly-enclosed bay" and "horn gap 11.20 mm" do not describe the same picture. The numbers are the truth of the object; the sentence oversells them. If a later reviewer is handed only the sentence, they will make the same call I did.

### The standing defect list, judged

**Open perimeter reveal — DISCLOSED CHARACTERISTIC, not blocking, with one measurement outstanding.**
The observation stands: in iso.png, closed/az0_el8.png and closed/az-90_el10.png the stored counters are plainly visible from outside through a continuous gap round the whole perimeter between the plate edge and the base. That is real and a buyer will see it. But I over-claimed in rounds 1 and 2 when I wrote "tip it and it empties" — **I withdraw that**, because I never established that the gap is wider than a counter. A Ø20.0 × 7.0 counter cannot leave through a slot narrower than 7.0 mm in any orientation. With the plate now known to be seated flush rather than perched, the reveal reads as a designed clearance, and on an object this spare it reads as deliberate — you can see what is inside. **One measurement settles it: reveal width against 7.0 mm.** Under 7.0 and there is nothing here at all; at or over 7.0 it becomes blocking and needs closing. I cannot take that measurement from a render and I should not have asserted the answer.

**Lid bearing on four corners — WITHDRAWN entirely.**
This was a consequence of my misreading of req 19. With plate top and base top flush at z = 24.0, the plate is seated in the opening, not perched on posts. Not a defect, not blocking.

**Counters as wide as their cells — DISCLOSED CHARACTERISTIC, not blocking.**
playing/top_el90.png still shows the discs filling their squares edge to edge with no gutter. They never collide, because draughts only uses alternating cells, and nothing fails because of it. It is the one thing I would still change on taste — a couple of millimetres of air would make the board breathe — but it is a proportion choice, not a fault, and it would not stop me shipping.

**Low-contrast board — DISCLOSED CHARACTERISTIC, not blocking.**
The cells are distinguished by an engraved outline in one uniform material (playing/top_el90.png, kings/board_az-90_el45.png). That is an honest consequence of a single-material object with no paint and no added decoration, which is what reqs 15 and 16 demand. In two materials or with any finish it ceases to be a question. Raking light makes it read.

**Should any of these stop it going out: no.** Nothing on this list is something an ordinary buyer would pick up and call broken. The one thing I want before it ships is not a fix, it is a number — the perimeter reveal against 7.0 mm — and it is a single query against the solid.

### Final position

With the three corrections applied, **all twenty requirements match**, and there is no blocking visual defect. The correction did what it set out to do: the dome is a true hemisphere on a real drum with a bare margin, the slit runs one meridian over the crown with the far half intact, the telescope is a properly yoked instrument with an open bore rather than a stub, both counter faces carry the mark so there is no wrong way up, and a king stack cannot rock because the rim band bears all the way round. The Moon is the one place where the built shape is less dramatic than the words describing it — but it is the shape that was specified, to a quarter of a millimetre, and it reads correctly as a moon on a full board. I would be happy to own it.
