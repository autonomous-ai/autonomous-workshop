# Waterloo Command
Four battles on one battlefield
Use rules v1.1 for every sheet. Each starts at round 1, all listed combat units at stress 0 and unactivated. N/E/S/W means facing; L/C/Sq means infantry line/column/square. Units without formations use their usual single piece. Put every unlisted unit on the reserve apron. Excluded units cannot enter or count for elimination; only scheduled reinforcements may arrive. These are original command-group scenarios, not exact regimental reconstructions.

## 1 • The ridge and the road — main battle
Eight rounds; six orders per side per round. French first in odd rounds, coalition first in even rounds.

The opening display evokes about 11:30 on 18 June 1815, before the major attacks and before Prussian intervention on this field. The terrain and grouped armies are selectively compressed. Wellington's side includes British, King's German Legion, Dutch-Belgian and Nassau groups, followed by Prussian cooperation. The positions are an interpretive command-group display, not a surveyed battalion deployment.

P1 Bülow advance group, P2 Zieten support group and BP Blücher start on the labelled reserve apron. At the beginning of round 4, P1 enters J6 in column facing west, stress 0, accompanied by Blücher. At the beginning of round 5, P2 enters J3 in column facing west, stress 0. Entry is free and the units may activate that round. If any combat unit occupies the entry cell, defer that arrival to the next round. Check P1 before P2 when both are deferred. A commander alone does not block entry; an enemy commander is displaced under the normal rule. Pending arrivals do not extend the game. F5 is already on field and may act from round 1; choosing when to spend orders bringing it forward is your reserve decision.

At the end of round 8 count French combat units on these four objectives: **B4 Hougoumont, E3 La Haye Sainte, E2 ridge road and H6 Plancenoit**. Three or four: French win. Zero or one: coalition win. Exactly two: draw. Empty cells do not count. The universal elimination rule can end the game earlier.

Return all pieces to this table and the three marked apron positions to restore the historical display. The three following games deliberately alter allocations and orders; their setups are counterfactual, and do not assert that a different order would have changed history.

## 2 • Westward diversion — counterfactual
Four rounds; three orders per side per round. No arrivals.

| French | Cell / formation / facing | Coalition | Cell / formation / facing |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | B5 C N | A1 | B4 Sq S |
| F2 | C5 C N | A2 | C3 L S |
| FS | A5 N | AS | A3 S |
| NF commander | beside C5 | WA commander | beside C3 |

At the end of round 4 the French win if a French combat unit occupies **B4**; otherwise the coalition wins. Apply immediate elimination normally. Can the French concentrate enough orders to erode the garrison, or tempt defenders into the open? The French may choose infantry support, change to line or seek a flank. A1's square is resistant to flanks but has weak firepower.

## 3 • A breach at the farm — counterfactual
Five rounds; four orders per side per round. No arrivals.

| French | Cell / formation / facing | Coalition | Cell / formation / facing |
|---|---|---|---|
| F2 | E5 C N | A2 | E3 C S |
| F3 | F5 L N | A3 | F2 L S |
| FC1 | F4 N | AC | F1 S |
| FG1 | E4 N | AG | E2 S |
| NF commander | beside F5 | WA commander | beside F2 |

At the end of round 5 count French combat units on **E3 and E2**. Both: French win. Neither: coalition win. Exactly one: draw. Apply immediate elimination normally. The gun starts in front of the column: decide when to fire and when to clear its approach. Cavalry cannot enter E3. Combined-arms support uses a second order and activates its contributor; this is a real cost, not an automatic bonus.

## 4 • The eastern commitment — counterfactual
Five rounds; four orders per side per round. No arrivals; both Prussian units are already on field. This deliberately combines forces from different moments.

| French | Cell / formation / facing | Coalition | Cell / formation / facing |
|---|---|---|---|
| F4 | H6 C E | P1 | J6 C W |
| F5 | G6 C E | P2 | J4 C W |
| FC2 | G5 E | A5 | H3 C S |
| NF commander | beside G6 | BP commander | beside J6 |
| — | — | WA commander | beside H3 |

At the end of round 5 the coalition wins if a coalition combat unit occupies **H6** and at least one of P1/P2 remains on field. If no coalition combat unit occupies H6, the French win. Otherwise draw: a surviving Anglo-Allied unit holds H6 but both Prussian groups were removed. Apply immediate elimination normally. Commit the Guard to contain the eastern pressure, or use cavalry in the open while the village delays the Prussians. The village admits column infantry and skirmishers only.

## Make your next game
Replay the same sheet with sides exchanged, or agree different initial facings and infantry formations legal in each cell. For a custom deployment agree unit IDs, one unit per legal cell, fixed round cap, orders per side (up to six), exact objective scoring including a draw rule, and any arrival cells/rounds before moving anything. Use only the supplied roster and rules. These changes are counterfactual games, not new historical evidence.
