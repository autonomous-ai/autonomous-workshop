# Tidal Crown

Chess — interacting-galaxies theme: tidal arms strip rival stellar structures until a galactic nucleus has no safe square.

This is standard two-player chess. The sculptures change its physical expression; they add no powers, scenarios or exceptions. The grid abstracts a galactic encounter, not orbital physics.

## In the set

One board and 34 separate chessmen: per side, one king, two queens, two rooks, two bishops, two knights and eight pawns. One queen per side is a customary spare; set those two aside at the start. There are 35 physical parts altogether. No storage compartment, clock or hardware is included.

Round bases identify White. Rounded-square bases identify Black, independently of color. Identify the roles by their familiar cues:

| Chess role | Sculptural cue | Galactic structure |

| King | Tall cross | Nucleus with polar jets |
| Queen | Curved crown | Spiral arms |

| Knight | Hooked horse-neck profile | Tidal tail |
| Pawn | Low round head | Star-forming knot |

## Set up

Sit on opposite sides. Each player's near-right corner must be a light square. Dark squares have recessed borders; all square centers support the men at the same height.

White's back row, from White's left to right, is rook, knight, bishop, queen, king, bishop, knight, rook. Put White's eight pawns directly in front. Black's pieces occupy the opposite two rows, with matching roles facing each other. Each queen starts on her own color: White on light, Black on dark. White moves first.

For notation, files a–h run left to right from White's view; ranks 1–8 run away from White. White's king starts on e1 and Black's on e8. Spare queens stay off the playing field.

## Move and capture

Players alternate turns. A normal turn moves one of your men to a legal destination. You cannot land on your own man. To capture, remove the opposing man on the destination and put yours there; en passant is the special exception described below. Captured men stay off the field.

- **Rook:** any unobstructed distance along one rank or file.
- **Bishop:** any unobstructed distance diagonally.
- **Queen:** either a rook's or a bishop's move.
- **Knight:** two squares along a rank or file and one perpendicular, landing on the opposite color. It can jump over intervening men.
- **King:** one square in any direction, provided the destination is safe. The kings must never stand next to each other.
- **Pawn:** one square straight forward into an empty square. From its starting rank it may instead advance two squares if both are empty. It captures one square diagonally forward. White advances toward rank 8; Black toward rank 1. Pawns never move backward.

Rooks, bishops and queens cannot jump. A move must not leave or place your own king under attack. A king is **in check** when attacked; answer by a legal king move, capture or interposition. An attacking piece still controls squares for king-safety purposes even when moving that piece would expose its own king.

Lift men for crowded moves and captures. The knight's jump is a rules permission, not a demand to physically leap over sculptures.

## Special moves

**Castling:** move your king two squares toward a rook, then move that rook to the square the king crossed. This counts as one turn. Neither that king nor that rook may have moved before; every square between them must be empty. Your king must not begin in check, cross an attacked square or finish on one. An attacked rook does not by itself prevent castling. Moving a king or rook away and back does not restore its lost castling rights.

**En passant:** immediately after an opposing pawn advances two squares from its starting square and lands beside your pawn, your pawn may capture it as though it had advanced only one square. Move diagonally forward to that passed-over square and remove the adjacent pawn. This opportunity lasts only for your immediately following move and must satisfy king safety.

**Promotion:** when a pawn reaches the farthest rank, replace it immediately with a same-side queen, rook, bishop or knight of your choice. The new role acts immediately. Promotion can accompany a capture. You may have multiple pieces of a role; your choice is never limited to previously captured men or the stock in this box.

If the required promotion piece is unavailable, obtain another correctly identified piece or print another copy of the appropriate supplied role and side. Do not substitute a different legal choice merely because it is in the box. In competition, pause the clock to seek the arbiter's assistance. One spare queen per side is conventional supply, not coverage for every possible position.

## End the game

**Checkmate:** a king is in check and its side has no legal answer. The attacking side wins immediately; never capture a king.

**Resignation:** a player may concede. Under the governing rules, resignation instead produces a draw if the opponent cannot possibly deliver mate by any legal sequence.

Draws include:

- **Stalemate:** the side to move has no legal move and is not in check.
- **Dead position:** neither side can ever mate through any legal sequence; do not replace this test with a simple count of pieces.
- **Agreement:** both players agree, after each has made at least one move, subject to any event restrictions.
- **Threefold repetition:** the player to move may claim when the same position has appeared three times, or will appear after that player's declared intended move. It need not occur consecutively. Side to move, piece locations and available moves must match; castling and en passant rights can distinguish positions.
- **Fifty-move claim:** the player to move may claim after each side has made 50 moves without a pawn move or capture, or when that player's declared intended move will complete that count.
- **Automatic draws:** five occurrences of the same position, or 75 moves by each side without a pawn move or capture, end the game without a claim. A checkmate delivered by the final move takes priority over the 75-move rule.

For claims based on an intended move, use the official claim procedure before making it; do not move first and assume that preserves the claim. Ordinary scoring is one point for a win, half each for a draw and zero for a loss, unless an event specifies another scoring system. There is no additional thematic score.

## Handling and organized play

Use one hand for a move. Deliberately touching your own movable man commits you to move it; deliberately touching a capturable opposing man commits you to capture it when legal. Announce an adjustment before touching solely to straighten a man, and do so on your turn. For castling, touch the king first. Releasing a man on its legal destination generally fixes the move; promotion and castling have their own completion details.

A separate chessclock and notation materials can be used for organized play. Agree the time control in advance and apply its governing clock, recording and claim procedures. A player who runs out of time normally loses, but the result is a draw if the opponent cannot mate by any possible legal sequence. Competition rules also govern irregular moves, assistance and arbiter decisions; these sculptures do not alter them.

The authoritative reference for the full procedures is the [FIDE Laws of Chess effective 1 January 2023](https://handbook.fide.com/chapter/e012023), including the appropriate rapid/blitz provisions when applicable. ## The pictured move

The demonstration is an ordinary endgame, not the normal starting position: White king f6, White queen h1, Black king f8; White to move, every other square empty. Slide Qh1–h8, a travel of 126 mm. The queen checks along rank 8; White's king covers e7, f7 and g7 while the queen covers e8 and g8. Black has no legal reply: **Qh8#**. Both kings remain on their squares.

## Making and using the physical set

The intended design has a 196 mm overall platter envelope, 12 mm board depth, 18 mm squares and 12–28 mm-tall chessmen within 14 mm base envelopes. Print each separate part upright with its flat bottom on the build plate. The design targets manufacture without supports or hardware; this document alone is not a print-ready certification. Use the accompanying verification results to determine which digital checks have actually passed.

Dark-square borders are 2 mm-deep recesses. Their rounded outer and inner outlines, with sloped outer walls, provide visible and tactile contrast while preserving level central support. Flat bases are intended to span the narrow recesses during a slide. Rendered clearances and digital geometry do not prove low friction, comfortable handling, physical print quality, durability or safety. Inspect fabricated parts before use; physical printing and human play have not been established by this guide.

Captures represent stripped stellar matter, promotion develops a knot into another stellar structure, and castling relocates the core and bar. Checkmate confines the nucleus; draws leave the encounter unresolved. These meanings explain the imagery and never decide a legal move.
