# Independent blind visual review — phase 1

## Evidence boundary

Inspected only four supplied static render images: iso.png, signature.png, white-seat.png, black-seat.png. No design brief, rules, CAD, measurements, or other reviewers' work was read. This records an image-only interpretation before reveal. Motion and physical testing are unverified.

## Literal object and volume

A thin rectangular gridded board supports two opposing sets of seven upright sculptural tokens. One set is pale with circular plinths; the other blue with clipped-square plinths. Seven differently shaped tokens are mirrored across the sides. Each occupies approximately one cell with visible clearance to cell edges. The board is otherwise flat, with faint engraved lines, some doubled cell borders or inset corner marks, and small edge symbols.

The held objects appear to be discrete tabletop game pieces, graspable by their raised forms or plinths. Visible profiles include a large ribbed balloon/bulb, a faceted irregular crystal/rock, a tilted oval globe, a pointed fluted bulb, a ringed globe, a cluster of lobes, and a sphere-like body with two unequal upright bars. Another slender flared spire appears among the seven: counting and identification from the low angle is less certain, while the seated views clearly separate seven profiles per side.

## Subjects, action, and relationship

The ringed globe reads most immediately as Saturn or a ringed planet. The other globe and clustered-body forms support an astronomy or space interpretation, although individual identities remain ambiguous. Several fluted forms could independently read as finials, balloons, or decorative chess pieces. There are no figures visibly holding objects.

The board and two matching teams strongly suggest an abstract opposing-player strategy game. The paired signature image shows pieces changing cells, a blue piece near the pale set, and fewer blue pieces in the second panel. This suggests advance and capture, but no arrows, text, or trajectory establish exact rules or temporal sequence. The seated views show each player receives the same family of silhouettes.

## Distinctive signature

The clearest non-generic combination is a family of varied astronomical sculptures on a fine gridded field, with ownership redundantly expressed by both color and round-versus-clipped-square bases. The asymmetric two-pronged body and clustered-lobe token are more distinctive than the generic ovoids. Shape identity across opposing pieces is visually consistent. A specific named source game or unique astronomical narrative cannot be identified from these images alone.

## Visible defects and uncertainties

- No obvious floating pieces, intersecting neighbors, missing plinths, or board penetrations are visible.
- Engraved cell accents, base markings, and edge symbols are very low contrast and mostly unreadable at the supplied whole-board views. Their meaning cannot be judged.
- The large ribbed bulb and shorter fluted pointed form share a visual vocabulary; individual game-role recognition would benefit from context or handling. The planet and ringed planet are easier to distinguish.
- Some shapes look like stylized ornaments more strongly than unmistakable astronomical objects. This is a theme-legibility uncertainty, not an observed fabrication defect.
- Perspective overlap in the low isometric view obscures some neighboring profiles; opposite seated views resolve most of that ambiguity.
- Thin upright prongs and the ring-to-body junction exist visibly, but strength, comfort, support requirements, and scale cannot be established from renders.
- Static changed positions cannot prove motion, valid moves, retention, actual fit, printing success, or play quality.

## Phase 1 conclusion

Visually coherent opposing-team tabletop game with varied space-associated sculptural pieces and a useful base-shape distinction. No definite gross geometry defect is visible. Fine markings and individual celestial identities are the principal visual uncertainties. Awaiting reveal for comparison against intended signature; no compliance verdict is possible before that reveal.

## Additional blind close view

The root supplied piece-comparison.png before reveal. This unobstructed image corrects my board-view count: there are **eight different paired token types**, sixteen tokens total, with base numerals 1 through 8. My earlier seven-per-team statement was an image-reading error, not missing geometry. The close view shows an obvious small angular crater-like cut in the tilted globe (3), triangular facets on the rock/crystal (2), a large central lobe with smaller companions (1), and a ringed planet (4). The short pointed fluted form (5), unequal prongs rising from a ball (6), flared narrow-tipped spire (7), and tall bulb (8) are well differentiated at this closer scale. The twin bars could represent a comet tail; the tall bulb could be an elongated celestial plume, but these identities are speculative. The numeric ranks become legible in this close view, so their faintness is primarily a whole-board render limitation. All eight matching shape pairs are consistent; no clear construction defect is newly exposed.
