# Pearl Pulse

A wind-up pearl jellyfish desk companion with eight bead-chain tentacles and four inner ribbon arms. Press its bell to lower the inner ring and spread the tentacle roots. A rubber motor and grease-damped cam are designed to repeat that pulse after winding.

## Print setup

Use a 0.4 mm nozzle and a 220×220×220 mm bed (`--bed 220x220x220`). All dimensions are mm. Use the isolated `part_*.step.py` print stances. The hanging combined assembly is an installation reference.

Use the same PETG family for all four colours: pearl #F4F1E8, blush #E6BFCB, mist #BCD5E5, lavender #C4BDD8. Co-print the three bell material regions at their shared registered origin. Do not print them as loose wedges. Print eight complete chain modules in the supplied flat print-in-place arrangement; the nine solids in each module must retain their relative positions. Print retaining clips flat. Start with 0.16 mm layers; keep support generation off only after checking the supplied print-gate evidence and your slicer. Preserve the 0.4 mm bead clearances and remove any first-layer expansion that closes them.

## Additional materials

One closed natural-rubber motor loop, approximately 70 mm relaxed doubled length, 4×1 mm strip; two small natural-rubber return loops, approximately 56 mm relaxed circumference, 1×0.4 mm strip; a thin film of damping grease such as the specified Nyogel 774H grade. These are consumables, not printed parts. Rubber and grease properties vary; the duration and return force require a physical trial. Keep grease away from the rubber and dry journals. Smooth loaded hook edges and remove print burrs before installing elastic.

## Assembly sequence

1. Check each PIP strand for clear gaps without forcing a stuck bead. Fit the motor loop to the lower removable anchor. Insert the anchor under the foot, turn 45° clockwise viewed from below to its hard stop, and draw it up to its shoulder.
2. Align the lower carrier's keyway with the stand key and lower it onto the collar. Fit the four ribbon roots through their yoke slots and attach the two return arms through their square yoke openings. Their wider caps remain above the floor.
3. Lower the yoke over the stand. Its guide sleeve will stop on the carrier hub after 4 mm of travel.
4. Place the cam and cup on the shaft's square coupling, with the cam web on its lower shoulder. Fit the upper motor bight through the hook's side opening onto its lower ledge. Apply only a thin grease film to the cup's working annulus.
5. Lower the fixed frame over the narrow shaft and into the three carrier sockets. Fit the thrust washer over the shaft, then squeeze and insert its split cross-key sideways. The key rests on the washer and captures the shaft.
6. Thread each return loop through a rounded fixed-frame slot and girth-hitch it around the inner land. Feed the other bight through the matching moving arm eye; with the arm detached, pass the complete arm through the bight to form the second hitch, then reinstall its top in the yoke. Pass the elastic through the keeper's open outer passages.
7. Fit each chain root between its fork cheeks. Insert a tangent pin and slide a flat C-clip onto its distal neck. Place all eight horn pins in the lower yoke track. Install the ring keeper over them, add its three pins and three identical C-clips. Check each clip is fully seated in its circular neck seat.
8. Register the bell 12° before its final position, lower its sockets over the three headed posts and turn 12° to lock. Its socket roofs seat on the post heads. The shell remains removable for service.

## Use and adjustment

Hold the foot and wind in the raised arrow direction: **clockwise viewed from below, no more than 16 turns from relaxed**. Release the key. The intended effect is a slow swimming pulse for roughly 30 seconds. That duration and acoustic quietness have not been measured.

For manual play, press gently until the physical 4 mm stop and release. The follower separates from the cam during a hand press. After the motor stops at an arbitrary phase, turn the key gently until the bell reaches its highest position to restore the full hand stroke. Never force a stuck mechanism.

The return loops must raise the loaded bell without pulling the frame out of its seats. If return is weak or the motor stalls, check rubbing/burrs, band routing and grease quantity before changing elastics. A physical trial must establish the final loop tension and grease charge. Do not stretch printed retainers farther than needed for insertion. Reverse assembly order for service.

## Verification limits

The supplied CAD reports distinguish source build, static fit, wall thickness, overhang and native still-image observations. They do not prove a successful physical print. Motion is **unverified** under the selected run option: no motion sweep, animation or independent motion review ran. Physical fit, fatigue, settling, sound and timed runtime remain untested. There was no human playtest.
