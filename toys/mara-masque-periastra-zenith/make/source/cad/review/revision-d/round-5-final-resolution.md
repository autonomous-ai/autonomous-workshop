## 1. FRESH OBSERVATION — `roof/slit-elevation_az-90_el0.png`

Cold, before deciding anything.

A teal hemisphere on its stepped collar and square plate, camera level with it. A straight-sided
channel about 20% of the dome's width is cut down through the middle of the dome, **and it takes the
crown with it**: the dome's profile rises on the left to a peak, stops, and instead of carrying over
the top it is interrupted by two flat parallel walls and the flat floor between them, then resumes on
the right. The two shoulders where the channel breaks the skyline are at the same height on both
sides, symmetric about the centre.

Standing in that channel is a tube. It rises from the floor near the collar and runs up to just below
the shoulder line, its length filling about half the dome's width — as long as the dome's radius. The
top is an open mouth, a clean bright annulus with a dark bore inside it. At the bottom the tube
spreads into a smooth cove that flows out into the floor of the channel on both sides, with no line
where it lands. The tube is centred in the channel with an even gap either side, roughly a quarter of
the channel's width per side. The collar below is unbroken from side to side.

**What it reads as:** an open, opened-up dome with a long vertical pipe standing inside it, mouth
pointing at the sky. My words, unprompted: a standing tube in a cut-open dome — a periscope, a flue, a
telescope aimed at the zenith. It does not read as a barrel poking through a wall, because you can see
its whole length and you can see it standing on a floor. It reads as a thing inside a building.

That is a genuinely different object from the one in `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0.png`. In the round-3
blind read I never saw this frame, because it did not exist.

Second new frame, `roof/foot-macro_az-90_el55.png`: looking down onto the root from high up. The cove
is a clean, even, circular collar all the way round the tube — the "generous cove carried right round
the foot" is exactly what is there, and from this angle it is obvious and well-formed. The mouth is
open with an even wall. The mottling I complained about is **much reduced** but not wholly gone: it
survives as faint concentric contour rings at the outer edge of the cove, left and right. That it
changes character this much with camera elevation is itself the proof of your explanation — geometry
does not reorganise itself when you raise the camera 27 degrees; flat shading against a tangency does.
My round-4 acceptance is now corroborated rather than merely conceded.

## 2. B2 — **WITHDRAWN**

Withdrawn without reservation. The measurement I asked for was taken and it goes against me: 0.0000 mm³
of roof material in a 4 mm column on the dome's own axis between z 85.5 and the apex, and 0.0000 mm³
of asymmetric volume about x = 0 out of 1009918.6 mm³. The slit crosses the crown and is meridional to
the limit of measurement. `roof/slit-elevation_az-90_el0.png` shows the same thing in pixels: the
channel interrupts the skyline at the top of the dome, with symmetric shoulders.

**What I misread, and why.** My round-3 sentence was *"the rectangle sits right of the dome's centre in
plan and does not reach the zenith"*. Looking at `roof/slit-plan_el90.png` again with the numbers in
hand: the slit floor runs x −15.000 to +15.000 and y −75.000 to +29.206. Across its width it is dead
centred on the axis. Along its length it is deliberately unequal — 29.2 mm past the centre on the far
side, then all the way down the near flank to the drum — so in a top-down view the rectangle's two ends
sit at very different distances from the middle of the circle, and the whole rectangle looks shoved to
one side. I read that length asymmetry as a width offset. Then, having decided the shape was off-axis,
I read its far end as the near end and concluded the crown was solid.

Two compounding errors on my part: I judged a three-dimensional cut on a sphere from a single
orthographic plan, which flattens exactly the information needed to tell a crown-crossing slit from a
flank slit; and I had no elevation along the slit axis to check against, because that frame did not
exist. The first was avoidable and is mine. The second is why I flagged it as the one finding I would
trade for a measurement — that offer is now called in, correctly.

## 3. P9 — judged against the frames its own text describes — **MATCHED**

The brief's positive text is *"a long instrument standing on the observatory floor and lying across an

clause of that names a view down the open slit. Judged there:

  `roof/foot-macro_az-90_el55.png`: the tube stands on the channel floor on its own spread root, with
  nothing else in the slit. Confirmed by my own blind sentence, *"There is no fork, no yoke, no pier, no
  trunnion, no pivot pin, no bearing block, no ring, no strap, no clamp, no base plate, no fork-arms
  either side."*
- **Lying across an open dome** — `roof/slit-elevation_az-90_el0.png`, `roof/down-slit_az-90_el14.png`:
  the dome is open over the crown and the tube lies in the opening along its length.
- **Most of its length visible down the open slit** — `roof/into-slit_az-90_el28.png`,
  `roof/down-slit_az-90_el14.png`; my blind figure was *"85–90% swallowed, 10–15% showing"*, and in
  the new elevation the visible shaft is about as long as the dome's radius.
- **Only its mouth clearing the shell** — `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0.png` for the clearance,
  `roof/slit-elevation_az-90_el0.png` for the muzzle sitting below the shoulder line, which also
  settles P6 directly rather than by the positional inference I used in round 4.
- **Not a short barrel poking through a wall** — in the new elevation it is plainly not that: you see

**MATCHED.** I got P9 wrong in round 4 by judging it in a frame its own text does not name, because you
gave it to me as a bare sentence and I reached for the only elevation I had. Corrected here.

What still does not land, and I will not pretend it does: the shaft carries no instrument features — no

the verdict. But "instrument" is being supplied by the dome around it, not by the tube.

## 4. B1 — **WITHDRAWN**, and I am not withdrawing it to be agreeable

I have gone looking for a reason to keep it and I do not have one that survives contact with the brief
and the freeze.

You offered me the defensible version: that a buyer sees the side elevation on a shelf and the brief
cannot legislate that away. I agree with the premise and I reject the conclusion, for one reason: a
blocker has to be something this correction introduced or failed to remove. The silhouette requirement
in the brief is purely negative — must not read as spout, cannon, mortar or gun — and my own blind three
words were *"snapped-off peg"*, with runners-up *"broken tab, sawn stub"*, and none of the four
forbidden readings anywhere in my answer. The correction was never asked to make the az0 elevation read
as a telescope, and it removed the reading it was asked to remove. Meanwhile the positive reading is
delivered in the frames that own it. Keeping B1 would mean blocking a build for failing a test that is
not in its brief and cannot be passed inside its envelope.

**What would have to change for my az0 reservation to go away, in one sentence:** the straight side
elevation would have to show either a legible aperture or a manufactured-looking muzzle, and it can
show neither — the aperture is invisible from az0 because the hemisphere is solid and the slit is
meridional, so from that bearing it produces only the two edge nicks; a muzzle ring, flange, chamfer or
dew shield is barred by the frozen 15.0 mm OD, 7.0 mm bore and flat crosscut, and would in any case put
a fresh overhang on a part required to print support-free at 45 degrees; and simply protruding further
is barred by "clears, and only just", and is precisely what produced the cannon the correction exists
to kill. So it is **not possible** inside the freeze, which is exactly why it cannot be this build's
blocker.

The real fix costs no geometry at all: **stop shooting `roof/side-elevation_az0_el0.png` as the hero
frame and shoot `roof/slit-elevation_az-90_el0.png` instead.** Same camera height, same elevation
discipline, one bearing round, and the object goes from "chipped cloche with a nub" to "opened dome
with an instrument in it". That is a photography decision, it violates no freeze, and it is the single
highest-value change available on this object right now.
