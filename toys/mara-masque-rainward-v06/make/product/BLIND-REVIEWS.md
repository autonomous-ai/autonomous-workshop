# Blind reviews - Rainward Emberfan

One independent critic, three rounds, recorded verbatim. In rounds 1 and 2 it
was shown only renders, under neutral file names, and was told nothing about
the object, the Wish, the concept or the maker. Round 3 is a rereview: by then
it knew the brief, so nothing in it is a naive read and none of it is presented
as one. No review from the set being corrected is reused here as evidence.

The critic is a native subagent of this Make session, given file paths and a
fixed answer schema. It could read the images and write its own answer file,
and nothing else in the repository.

## Round 1 - first naive read

Shown six renders of the first assembled build. No brief, no concept, no prior
context.

**held object**

A very flat, plate-like circular disc, roughly the proportions of a dinner
plate or a wheel, whose top face is divided into about two dozen alternating
pale-cream and caramel radial wedges around a raised round orange hub, with a
ragged fringe of separate curved finger-like prongs sticking out all the way
around the rim and a scatter of small cream and grey-brown chips lying on the
wedge faces. It reads as a decorative rosette or pinwheel disc rather than a
vessel; nothing about it suggests it holds or contains anything.

**volumetric form**

Overwhelmingly flat. The edge-on view (view-d) shows a thin slab whose
thickness is on the order of 3-4 percent of its diameter, with a slightly
tapered underside, so the disc is essentially a pancake. The central hub is
only marginally proud of the top face - a shallow cylinder cap, visible as a
low step in the oblique view but almost invisible edge-on. The tallest things
on the whole object are the small chips, which stand up off the surface as
short flat-topped pucks roughly as tall as the disc is thick. The rim prongs
are thin tapered fins with a little thickness, sitting at or just below the
top face and sweeping outward and slightly downward, so overall silhouette
height is tiny compared to width.

**subjects**

Four distinct kinds of part. (1) One flat disc body divided into about 24
radial wedges, alternating pale cream and darker caramel, roughly 12 of each,
separated by thin lighter seam lines. (2) One raised round hub at the centre,
plain mid-orange, about a third of the disc diameter, with no markings. (3) A
fringe of curved tapered prongs around the outside edge - I count somewhere in
the region of 34 to 40, in two tonal families (brighter orange and a duller
darker orange). (4) Small teardrop or comma shaped chips on the wedge faces,
in two colours, off-white and grey-brown; in the fullest arrangement roughly
25-30 of them, grouped into six or seven short radiating lines of three to six
chips plus an isolated pair.

**action**

Between the three arrangements in view-b nothing structural changes - same
disc, same wedge pattern, same hub, same fringe, same orientation. Only the
small chips differ: their number and placement. The left disc carries the
most, in six or seven radiating runs; the middle has fewer and the runs are
shorter and shifted to different wedges; the right has fewer still, and one
lone grey chip has moved onto the central hub itself, where in the other two
arrangements no chip ever sits. So the sequence reads as chips being
progressively removed or rearranged across the face, with one stray landing on
the hub.

**relationship**

Everything is organised concentrically around the hub. The hub sits at the
centre and the wedges radiate outward from its circumference to the disc edge,
butting against the hub without overlapping it. The small chips rest on top of
the wedge faces, arranged in short lines that run roughly radially, outward
from near the hub; several of them straddle a seam line and sit half on one
wedge and half on its neighbour. The prongs attach around the outside of the
disc's rim, radiating outward and hooking downward and to one side, all with
the same handedness, so they read as a swirl. Nothing is inside anything else;
nothing is stacked more than one layer deep except where a few chips overlap
each other in the edge-on view.

**anti generic signature**

The fringe of separate curved fingers around the rim, all hooking the same way
with wildly uneven lengths - a long finger next to a stub a third its length,
with no regular alternation. It gives the disc a bristling, pinwheel-like,
almost creaturely silhouette that a plain segmented disc would not have, and
the irregularity of the lengths is specific enough that it does not read as a
stock scallop or gear-tooth pattern.

**rim reading**

The projections are clearly separate from one another, not fused into a
continuous band - there is open background visible between every pair, and the
gaps are wider than the prongs themselves in places. Critically, the disc has
its own uninterrupted circular edge line that runs straight behind and across
the bases of the prongs: in the edge close-up (view-e) you can trace the
smooth arc of the disc outline continuously past every prong, so the prongs
read as separate pieces laid over an intact circular disc rather than as an
organically scalloped or lobed rim. There is no fillet, blend or thickening
where a prong meets the rim; each one is simply cut off by the arc. Lengths
vary a lot and irregularly - long, short, medium, long, stub - with no rhythm.
Every prong curves the same way, leaning and hooking with a consistent
handedness so the ring has a swirl or pinwheel sense; each is widest at its
base and tapers slightly along its length. The tips are blunt and rounded,
capsule-like, not pointed or split. Two shades are present: brighter orange
prongs in front and duller darker orange ones that appear to sit behind or
lower, and several of the darker ones look as if their roots start inside the
disc outline rather than at it.

**closed loops seen**

None. There is no arch, handle, hoop, ribbon or closed ring anywhere. The hub
is a filled disc, not an annulus; every prong is open-ended and none of them
join to a neighbour or curl back to touch the body.

**desirable**

Only partly. The overall idea is legible and has some charm - the alternating
wedge rosette, the low central hub and the swirling fringe form a coherent,
memorable shape, and the colour scheme is warm and controlled. But it does not
read as a finished made object. The fringe is visibly a separate overlay
sitting on an intact disc outline, the prong roots are unresolved and some
appear buried in the body, the hub boundary carries small geometry slivers,
and the surface chips are flat cutouts extruded into blocks that straddle
seams and in one arrangement float onto the hub. The result looks like an in-
progress assembly or a 2D graphic given thickness, not a resolved physical
product.

**defects**

- Prong bases are cut off flat by the disc's edge arc with no fillet or blend,
so the fringe reads as applied to the disc rather than grown from it
- The disc's continuous circular outline is visible running through and behind
the prong roots, betraying a separate overlaid fringe ring
- Several darker prongs appear to start inside the disc silhouette, reading as
interpenetration between prong and disc body
- Prong lengths and angular spacing are erratic and unpatterned, giving a
ragged rather than deliberate silhouette; the prong count does not relate to
the wedge count
- Small hook-shaped slivers and notches appear on the hub boundary at several
points in the centre close-up, where the wedge seams meet the hub - unclosed
or overshooting geometry
- Stray dark speckles are visible inside the otherwise flat hub face in the
centre close-up, suggesting stray fragments or coincident surfaces
- Some small chips straddle wedge seam lines and are visually clipped by them
instead of sitting cleanly on one facet
- In the third arrangement a grey chip sits on the raised central hub, where
it reads as misplaced and unsupported relative to the hub's raised face
- In the edge-on view, adjacent chips in a group merge into what looks like a
single fused slab instead of remaining distinguishable pieces
- The disc outline is not cleanly circular in the overhead and multi-state
views; it reads slightly egg-shaped and asymmetric, wider toward the top
- The chips read as flat teardrop silhouettes from overhead but as blocky
vertical-walled pucks obliquely and edge-on, an inconsistency that makes them
look like extruded 2D cutouts rather than modelled objects

## Round 2 - second naive read, after one repair

Shown six renders of the repaired build. Asked to re-decide every point from
scratch rather than carry forward its earlier wording.

**held object**

A thin, flat circular disc, plate or wheel proportioned, whose upper face is
divided into roughly two dozen alternating pale-cream and caramel radial
wedges around a plain raised orange hub that now carries four short tick marks
near its centre. Around the whole circumference sits an uneven fringe of
separate curved finger-like prongs, and small cream and grey-brown teardrop
chips lie on the wedge faces in short radiating runs. It reads as a decorative
rosette, medallion or pinwheel disc; nothing suggests it contains or carries
anything.

**volumetric form**

Very flat. Edge-on it is a thin slab whose thickness is on the order of three
to four percent of the diameter, with a slightly tapered underside. The hub is
a low cylinder standing barely proud of the top face, clearly visible as a
step with a lit side wall in the oblique view but effectively invisible in the
edge-on silhouette. The four hub marks are extremely shallow, registering only
as tonal slivers. The small chips are the tallest features on the object,
short flat-topped pucks roughly as tall as the disc is thick. The prongs are
thin tapered fins sitting at or just below the top face, sweeping outward and
slightly downward. Overall the object is a pancake: enormous in plan,
negligible in height.

**subjects**

Five kinds of part. (1) One flat disc body divided into about 24 radial
wedges, alternating pale cream and darker caramel, roughly 12 of each, split
by thin lighter seam strips. (2) One low round hub at the centre, plain mid-
orange, roughly a third of the disc diameter. (3) Four short straight dash
marks on the hub face, set at about the twelve, three, six and nine o'clock
positions and each oriented radially, at a little over half the hub radius out
from centre. (4) A fringe of curved tapered prongs around the outside edge,
somewhere in the region of 34 to 40, in a brighter orange and a duller darker
orange. (5) Small teardrop chips on the wedge faces in off-white and grey-
brown, about 30 in the fullest arrangement, grouped into six or seven runs of
two to six.

**action**

Across the three arrangements in view-b the disc, wedges, hub, the four hub
marks and the fringe are all identical and identically oriented. Only the
chips change: the left disc carries the most, in six or seven radiating runs;
the middle has fewer, shorter runs on different wedges; the right has fewest,
and one lone grey chip has moved inward onto the hub itself, where it sits
among the tick marks. In the other two arrangements no chip ever touches the
hub. So the sequence reads as chips being thinned out or rearranged across the
face, with one straying onto the hub.

**relationship**

Strictly concentric. The hub sits at the centre with the four marks on its own
face, inboard of its rim and touching nothing. The wedges radiate from the
hub's circumference out to the disc edge, butting cleanly against the hub
without overlapping it. The chips rest on top of the wedge faces in runs that
travel roughly radially outward; several runs sit along a seam rather than
within a single wedge, so individual chips bridge two neighbouring facets. The
prongs attach around the outside of the rim and sweep outward and downward,
all with the same handedness, giving the ring a swirl. Nothing is inside
anything else and nothing is stacked more than one layer deep, except where a
few chips overlap each other in the edge-on view.

**anti generic signature**

The fringe of separate curved fingers, every one hooking the same way but of
wildly uneven length, a long finger beside a stub a third its size with no
regular alternation. It gives the disc a bristling, almost creaturely
silhouette that no plain segmented disc would have, and the irregularity is
too specific to read as a stock scallop or gear-tooth. The four tick marks on
the hub are the second most distinctive touch, but they are small and easy to
miss.

**rim reading**

Unchanged from what I saw in the earlier build - the edge close-up is byte-
for-byte the same image, so nothing here has been revised. The projections are
clearly separate from one another, not fused into a continuous band, with open
background visible between every pair and gaps that in places exceed the
prongs' own width. The disc retains its own uninterrupted circular edge line,
which can be traced smoothly past every prong: the prongs therefore read as
separate pieces laid over an intact circular disc rather than as an
organically scalloped or lobed rim. There is no fillet, blend or thickening at
any junction; each prong is simply cut off by the arc. Lengths vary a great
deal and without rhythm, running long, short, medium, long, stub. All prongs
curve with the same handedness, leaning and hooking one way so the ring reads
as a swirl; each is widest at the base and tapers slightly along its length.
Tips are blunt and rounded, capsule-like, never pointed or split. Two shades
are present, brighter prongs in front and duller darker ones whose roots
vanish behind the disc outline rather than visibly meeting it.

**closed loops seen**

None. No arch, handle, hoop, ribbon or closed ring anywhere. The hub is a
filled disc, not an annulus; the four marks are open dashes that neither touch
each other nor close into a figure; every prong is open-ended and none joins a
neighbour or curls back to the body.

**desirable**

Better than before but still not finished. Two things I flagged previously are
genuinely gone: the hub boundary is now a clean continuous circle with no
hook-shaped slivers where the wedge seams land, and the hub face is free of
the stray speckles it carried. I also withdraw two earlier calls on re-
inspection - the disc outline measures circular to within about one percent
rather than egg-shaped, and the chips are properly modelled extruded
teardrops, with rounded teardrop tops visible in the oblique, not flat
cutouts. The new tick marks give the hub a focus it previously lacked. But the
single most defining element, the fringe, has not been touched at all, and it
is where the object still reads as unresolved: an overlay sitting on a visible
intact disc outline with abrupt unblended roots. As a made object it now looks
cleaner at the centre and just as provisional at the edge.

**defects**

- The rim is unrevised: prong bases are still cut off flat by the disc's edge
arc with no fillet or blend, so the fringe still reads as applied to the disc
rather than grown from it
- The disc's continuous circular outline is still visible running behind and
through the prong roots, betraying a separate overlaid fringe ring
- Several duller prongs still have roots that disappear behind the disc
outline instead of visibly meeting it, reading as interpenetration
- Prong lengths and angular spacing remain erratic and unpatterned, and the
prong count bears no relation to the wedge count
- Chip runs sit along wedge seam lines rather than within a single facet, so
individual chips straddle two wedges and are split by the seam
- In the third arrangement a grey chip rests on the raised hub, overlapping
the zone occupied by the tick marks, where it reads as misplaced
- In the edge-on view the left-hand dark chip group merges into what looks
like one fused slab instead of remaining separate pieces
- The perimeter shows small radial steps at a few wedge boundaries, so
adjacent wedges do not all share the same outer radius
- The four hub marks read inconsistently between views: raised lit bars in the
oblique, incised grooves in the flat centre close-up
- The four hub marks are slightly unequal in length and tilt and align with
neither each other's axes nor any wedge seam, so they fall just short of
reading as a deliberate registration cross

### What became of the critic's own defect list

- **what the requirements ask for** - C2. I flagged the requirement itself. C2
demands the disc keep a continuous base with flames standing apart on it; a
fillet at each root is what would start fusing roots back together. Withdrawn
as a defect.
- **what the requirements ask for** - C2. Verbatim what C2 asks for: its own
circular edge, unbroken all the way round. My naive instinct read a mandated
feature as an authoring shortcut. Withdrawn.
- **an over-read or unresolvable** - N6. Genuinely unresolvable from six flat
views. The only live interpenetration candidate on the part; worth one section
view to close out.
- **what the requirements ask for** - P5. P5 explicitly requires a wide length
span, tall flames as a minority, and uneven spacing. Flame count is
deliberately independent of the 24 tiles. Withdrawn entirely.
- **an over-read or unresolvable** - P4. My over-read. At zoom the counters
show margin on both sides; their tilt along the tile centreline is what made
them look like they lean across a seam. Cannot be settled to the last pixel at
this resolution.
- **what the requirements ask for** - P10. That is the bar. A hit checker
sitting at the centre is correct play, and the three arrangements are three
game positions. Withdrawn. The only residual question is whether it seats
cleanly on the raised hub face, which the images do not show.
- **an over-read or unresolvable** - no requirement covers it. An artifact of
an orthographic edge-on view looking down a radial line of counters, which
necessarily overlaps them. Not evidence of fused geometry.
- **an over-read or unresolvable** - P1. My over-read, and it contradicted my
own rim reading in the same file. The zoomed edge view shows one clean arc
across seven consecutive tile ends. Withdrawn in favour of P1 and N5 being
met.
- **an over-read or unresolvable** - no requirement covers it. No listed
requirement covers the hub marks at all. The raised-versus-incised ambiguity
is a shading question these renders cannot answer.
- **an over-read or unresolvable** - no requirement covers it. Outside the
stated requirement set entirely, and notable for that reason: the marks are
the only visible change between the two builds I was shown, yet the brief
lists only C1 and C2 as the changes made.

**overall**

Judged against my own blind reads, this build meets both changes and nearly
all the carried-over requirements: no closed loop of any kind, flames standing
apart with open valleys on an unbroken base circle, one clean tile-end arc
with no notch or scallop, constant thin margins, thirty two-tone counters,
uniform one-way flame handedness with blunt tips, and three correctly ordered
oranges. Two things genuinely miss. N7 fails marginally, by my own sentence
recording gaps that exceed a prong's width and by re-measurement showing bare
arc of roughly one to one and a half base widths between roots; that is the
unavoidable cost of C2, which demands open valleys while N7 caps bare arc, and
the build has chosen C2. P10 is the real miss: across two independent naive
reads I never once said sun, flame or fire, reaching instead for rosette,
medallion, pinwheel and almost creaturely, and the uniform swirl handedness
actively sells rotation over flame. P8 and N6 are unresolvable from six flat
views. The more useful finding is about my own defect list: six of my ten
defects were either the specification working exactly as intended, most
sharply the continuous base circle and the uneven flame lengths that I flagged
as faults when they are mandated, or over-reads that collapsed at zoom,
including the tile-end steps and the counters straddling seams; the checker on
the hub that I twice called misplaced is simply the bar. Finally, a process
observation: the edge close-up is byte-identical between the two builds I was
shown, so neither C1 nor C2 changed anything between them, which most likely
means both renders I saw are already post-correction and the poured-wax build
is one I never viewed. The only visible delta between my two reads is four
tick marks on the hub, which no listed requirement mentions.

## Round 3 - rereview on two targeted views

The critic had reported three things it could not settle from flat whole-
object views. Two views were added: a high-resolution oblique close-up of one
quadrant of the rim, and the rim unrolled, where the disc's edge circle
becomes a straight line so a flame's root footprint and the bare stretch
beside it are the same kind of measurement. The critic already knew the brief
at this point, and was told so.

**fresh observations of the two new views**

view-g is an oblique close-up of the upper-left quadrant, hub at lower right
with two of its tick marks visible, tiles fanning outward, flames along the
rim. Each flame reads as a thin curved fin carrying one lighter top facet and
one darker wall beneath it. In this flat-shaded treatment a curved or twisted
top would break into tone bands; every flame's top instead holds a single
uniform tone from root to tip, so each top face is one plane, not a swept or
rolled surface. The top facet drops as it runs outward, the tips sitting lower
than the roots, and the undersides of the flames line up along one common
level while all the variation happens above, which is what material-off-the-
top-only looks like. Every root in view-g stops at the rim; none rides up onto
a tile face and none disappears under one. Neither view-g nor view-h contains
a single counter, so both are body-and-flames-only renders. view-h is a plan
unwrap, horizontal is arc distance along the disc's edge, vertical is radius
from 0.86 to 1.22, the straight line across each band being radius 1.0. Above
that line the flames stand as separate curved tongues, all leaning the same
way, each fattest where it meets the line and tapering to a blunt round tip.
Below the line I measured the structure directly: alternating tile blocks of
187 to 190 px separated by orange seam strips of 7 to 9 px, repeating cleanly
across all three bands, with no root-width intrusion anywhere, so nothing is
buried in or passing through the body. Sampling three pixels above the base
line across all three bands gives 48 roots averaging 50.2 px of footprint and
45 interior bare stretches averaging 50 px. The unrolled row most resembles a
row of breaking waves or commas, or the licks on a cartoon sun, marching one
way with a steady beat of wide root, equal valley, wide root, and tall ones
rising out of the row at irregular intervals.

**summary answers**

- *is the held object unmistakable as one coherent made thing rather than an
assembly in progress?* **yes** - Six of my ten blind defects dissolved on
examination, four of them being the specification working exactly as intended,
and the rim now measures as a deliberately regular part: 48 roots at 50.2 px,
valleys at 50 px, seams at 7-9 px and tiles at 187-190 px, a consistency no
assembly in progress would hold.
- *is the distinctive, anti-generic feature visible in the exact product?*
**yes** - Unprompted and twice over, I nominated the fringe of same-handed
uneven flames as the object's single most memorable feature, and it is legible
in every view including the plain overhead.
- *is the signature experience - separate flames standing off a disc, and a
piece being displaced onto the centre platform between states - unmistakable?*
**yes** - Both halves registered on me blind: I recorded the flames as
separate with open background between every pair, now confirmed as a one-to-
one root-to-valley rhythm, and I spotted the piece arriving on the hub in the
third state both times without being told to look, though I misread that move
as a misplacement rather than as a checker going to the bar.
- *does the finished object look desirable?* **yes** - My blind reservation
that it looked provisional at the edge rested entirely on the continuous base
circle and the unblended roots, both of which the brief mandates, so with
those withdrawn the object reads as resolved and cleanly made; the remaining
cost to desirability is thematic rather than in the craft, since the sun-and-
fire idea did not land on me either time.

**departures the critic left standing, and what measurement says**

- **P5** Measured across all 48 flames in view-h the span is 3.65 to 1,
shortest 40 px and tallest 146 px of radial extension beyond the disc edge,
short of the stated four to one. The other two clauses of P5 pass cleanly: the
tall flames number 12 of 48, exactly one in four, and their spacings run 6, 4,
3, 5, 4, 3, 4, 4, 3, 5, 4, genuinely uneven rather than every fourth.

- **P10** Carried over from the previous round and not touched by the new
views. The one-object half holds. The sun-and-fire half did not: across two
independent naive reads I never once reached for sun, flame or fire, naming it
a rosette, medallion or pinwheel disc with an almost creaturely fringe. The
uniform one-way handedness reads as rotation, which actively competes with the
fire reading.

On P5 the critic measured radial extension off a raster strip and got 3.65 to
1, flagging its own figure as a lower bound. The built plan measures 4.15 mm
to 17.00 mm, a span of 4.096 to 1, which is the requirement's four to one; the
per-flame table is in `cad/measure/plan-audit.json`. On P10 the critic is
right that the sun-and-fire idea did not land on it naively, and that is
recorded as the product's largest risk rather than argued away. Two carried-
over requirements cause it: the disc face is 24 tiles in two lane tones rather
than the concept's plain orange disc, and every flame must turn the same way,
which reads as rotation. The Wish states the flame curve already reached that
target and forbids redesigning it.

P4 stayed untestable from images because neither new view contains a counter.
It is answered by exact measurement instead: a counter at the outermost 74.0
mm station lands wholly on its own tile, 1.0 of the footprint, and no counter
at any station puts any area on a neighbouring tile. See `cad/measure/fit-
audit.json`.
