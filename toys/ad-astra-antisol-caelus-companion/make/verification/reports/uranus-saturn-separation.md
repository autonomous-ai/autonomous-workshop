# Uranus against Saturn at the product's own frame

Ranks 6 and 7, Ø22.02 and Ø26.00 mm -- 3.98 mm apart in a set whose
ranks are told apart by size. Measured on the canonical side-by-side
renders at the hero frame (azimuth -55, elevation 22), one per army.

This revision took every marking off Uranus, so this pair is asked
again on this run's own renders rather than inherited from the
archive.

## The size cue

| | Uranus | Saturn | apart |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| rank | 6 | 7 | 1 |
| globe diameter mm | 22.02 | 26.00 | 3.98 |
| piece height mm | 25.98 | 29.00 | 3.02 |
| obliquity deg | 97.77 | 26.73 | 71.04 |

3.98 mm is 18.1 per cent of Uranus's own diameter and 10 nozzle
widths, and the pieces stand 3.02 mm apart in height.
**Size separates this pair on its own**, which is what the size
ladder is for: they are 1 rank apart and they look it.

## The colour cue

| | sealed hex | luma of 255 |
|---|---|---:|
| Uranus globe `cyan` | #00FFFF | 200.8 |
| Saturn globe `yellow` | #FFD834 | 212.5 |

Sealed, the two separate **11.7** of 255 luma levels, and they
separate in hue as well. For scale, this project accepted 9.4 as
its narrowest usable separation, on Saturn's bands.

As rendered, measured on the pixels of the two globes themselves:

| army | Uranus mean RGB | Saturn mean RGB | luma apart | Uranus marked | Saturn marked |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sol | 19,205,205 | 213,198,142 | 31.6 | 10.8% | 29.7% |
| Anti-Sol | 16,190,190 | 192,174,109 | 20.1 | 17.2% | 16.9% |

The `marked` columns are the share of each ball that is not its
own globe colour, which is the surface cue reduced to one number.
Uranus's is 10.8 to 17.2 per cent of its visible face; Saturn's is 16.9
to 29.7 per cent.

**Uranus's own figure is the ring and nothing else.** There is no
marking on that globe, so every pale pixel inside its
silhouette belongs to the hoop standing over it or to the
specular highlight the renderer puts on a curved surface.

## The silhouette cue

| | Uranus | Saturn |
|---|---|---|
| silhouette | a sphere with an upright hoop standing on edge, in the planet's own equatorial plane, which on a world tipped 97.77 degrees stands 7.77 degrees past vertical, so it arches over the ball's equator while both poles point sideways | a sphere with a flat plate lying almost level, in the planet's own equatorial plane, 26.73 degrees off horizontal |
| piece height mm | 25.98 | 29.00 |
| widest, ring included | Ø24.02 | Ø30.00 |
| ring filament | `white` | `white` |

**Both worlds carry a ring and, since this revision, both rings
print in the same `white`.** The colour of the ring is therefore no
longer a cue at all, and the whole weight falls on orientation
and on size.

Orientation carries it. Saturn's ring is a flat plate lying almost level, in the planet's own equatorial plane, 26.73 degrees off horizontal: its equatorial plane is
26.73 degrees from horizontal, so the ring reads as a brim beside
the ball and widens the piece without raising it. Uranus's is an upright hoop standing on edge, in the planet's own equatorial plane, which on a world tipped 97.77 degrees stands 7.77 degrees past vertical, so it arches over the ball's equator while both poles point sideways:
its equatorial plane stands 7.77 degrees PAST vertical, so the
hoop reads as a band over the top of the ball -- over its EQUATOR,
since this world's poles point sideways -- and raises the piece
without widening it much. One is wider than it is tall and
the other is taller than it is wide: Ø30.00 against Ø24.02 across,
29.00 mm against 25.98 mm tall. **The pair separates on silhouette
and on size, and no longer on colour anywhere.** Whether that is
enough without the colour is a question for the blind review,
which is asked it in so many words; the answer is in
`snap/SIGNATURE-REVIEW.json`.

## The surface cue

| world | what it wears |
|---|---|
| Uranus | nothing. Since the owner's second pass this globe carries no marking of any kind: no hood, no cap, no band, no spot. It is one undivided cyan sphere with a white hoop standing on edge around its equator |
| Saturn | five unequal soft bands in a mid amber, a bright white cap above +58, and a flat white ring plate lying almost level |

**This is the cue the revision spent.** Uranus used to carry two
pale polar hoods and now carries nothing, so on surface alone it
is a bare ball against a marked one. That is a separation rather
than a collision -- bare is as distinctive as marked, and no
other world in the set is bare -- but it is a separation that
only works while Uranus stays the only unmarked world.
