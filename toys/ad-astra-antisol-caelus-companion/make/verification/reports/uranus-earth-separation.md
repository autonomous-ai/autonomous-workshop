# Uranus against Earth at the product's own frame

Ranks 6 and 4, Ø22.02 and Ø16.70 mm -- 5.32 mm apart in a set whose
ranks are told apart by size. Measured on the canonical side-by-side
renders at the hero frame (azimuth -55, elevation 22), one per army.

This revision took every marking off Uranus, so this pair is asked
again on this run's own renders rather than inherited from the
archive.

## The size cue

| | Uranus | Earth | apart |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| rank | 6 | 4 | 2 |
| globe diameter mm | 22.02 | 16.70 | 5.32 |
| piece height mm | 25.98 | 19.70 | 6.28 |
| obliquity deg | 97.77 | 23.44 | 74.33 |

5.32 mm is 24.2 per cent of Uranus's own diameter and 13 nozzle
widths, and the pieces stand 6.28 mm apart in height.
**Size separates this pair on its own**, which is what the size
ladder is for: they are 2 ranks apart and they look it.

## The colour cue

| | sealed hex | luma of 255 |
|---|---|---:|
| Uranus globe `cyan` | #00FFFF | 200.8 |
| Earth globe `blue` | #004EA8 | 67.9 |

Sealed, the two separate **132.9** of 255 luma levels, and they
separate in hue as well. For scale, this project accepted 9.4 as
its narrowest usable separation, on Saturn's bands.

As rendered, measured on the pixels of the two globes themselves:

| army | Uranus mean RGB | Earth mean RGB | luma apart | Uranus marked | Earth marked |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sol | 15,202,202 | 12,146,149 | 44.6 | 8.5% | 5.2% |
| Anti-Sol | 14,200,200 | 4,123,132 | 62.4 | 9.5% | 14.5% |

The `marked` columns are the share of each ball that is not its
own globe colour, which is the surface cue reduced to one number.
Uranus's is 8.5 to 9.5 per cent of its visible face; Earth's is 5.2
to 14.5 per cent.

**Uranus's own figure is the ring and nothing else.** There is no
marking on that globe, so every pale pixel inside its
silhouette belongs to the hoop standing over it or to the
specular highlight the renderer puts on a curved surface.

## The silhouette cue

| | Uranus | Earth |
|---|---|---|
| silhouette | a sphere with an upright hoop standing on edge, in the planet's own equatorial plane, which on a world tipped 97.77 degrees stands 7.77 degrees past vertical, so it arches over the ball's equator while both poles point sideways | a bare sphere on a disc |
| piece height mm | 25.98 | 19.70 |
| widest, ring included | Ø24.02 | Ø16.70 |
| ring filament | `white` | -- |

Uranus carries a ring and Earth does not, which is a difference a reader
sees before any colour arrives. **The pair separates on
silhouette.**

## The surface cue

| world | what it wears |
|---|---|
| Uranus | nothing. Since the owner's second pass this globe carries no marking of any kind: no hood, no cap, no band, no spot. It is one undivided cyan sphere with a white hoop standing on edge around its equator |
| Earth | the world's coastlines as green land with beige dryland inside it and a white ice cap, an outline map a reader can check against an atlas |

**This is the cue the revision spent.** Uranus used to carry two
pale polar hoods and now carries nothing, so on surface alone it
is a bare ball against a marked one. That is a separation rather
than a collision -- bare is as distinctive as marked, and no
other world in the set is bare -- but it is a separation that
only works while Uranus stays the only unmarked world.
